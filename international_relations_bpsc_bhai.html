BRICS — India's Chairmanship Year

The biggest multilateral story of 2025–26

BRICS is now an 11-member grouping: Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa, Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, Saudi Arabia, UAE and Indonesia.

BRICS Expansion Timeline

India assumed the BRICS Chairship on 1 January 2026 — its fourth (after 2012, 2016, 2021) — under the theme "Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability", whose initials spell BRICS itself.

17th BRICS Summit Where the story begins

6–7 July 2025 · Rio de Janeiro, Brazil

Theme: "Strengthening Global South Cooperation for More Inclusive and Sustainable Governance." PM Modi articulated the "Humanity First", people-centric approach here — the seed of India's 2026 chairship theme. Brazil then passed the gavel to India.

  • Condemnation of CBAM: BRICS nations condemned and rejected the EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) and similar restrictive trade measures, saying they undermine the clean-economy transition.
  • MoEFCC notified formation of a National Designated Authority (NDA) to kick-start carbon trading in India — fulfilling Article 6 of the Paris Agreement.

G20 — From Africa's First Summit to Miami

20th G20 Summit

22–23 November 2025 · Johannesburg, South Africa

Theme: "Solidarity, Equality, Sustainability." The first-ever G20 Summit held on the African continent. PM Narendra Modi represented India; the US boycotted the summit, yet a Leaders' Declaration was still adopted.

  • Spirit of Ubuntu: declaration emphasises the African philosophy of Ubuntu — shared global responsibility and interconnectedness.
  • Climate finance: members agreed to scale global climate finance toward a "billions-to-trillions" shift.
  • Mission 300: led by the World Bank and African Development Bank, aims to give electricity to 300 million people in Sub-Saharan Africa by 2030.
  • Critical Minerals Framework: welcomed to secure sustainable value chains; focus on mineral exploration and local beneficiation in developing countries.
  • Youth & Gender targets: adopted the Nelson Mandela Bay Target to cut youth NEET rate by 5% by 2030; committed to 25% gender parity in labour force participation by 2030.
Extra / Trap The African Union (AU) became a permanent member of the G20 on 9 September 2023 at the 18th G20 Summit in New Delhi, on a proposal by PM Modi — the second regional organisation in the G20 after the European Union. Separately, the Trump administration boycotted the Johannesburg summit citing allegations about treatment of white Afrikaners, which South Africa rejected.

Upcoming — 21st G20 Summit Upcoming

14–15 December 2026 · Miami (Doral), Florida, USA

The US holds the 2026 presidency; venue announced as Trump National Doral, Miami. Previous (20th) Summit: Johannesburg, South Africa.

Trap 22nd G20 Summit (2027) is scheduled for the United Kingdom — USA hands over to UK. Reason given: the US takes on the G7 presidency the following year.

G7 — Évian and the IMPACT Proposal

The G7 Summit is an annual forum for leaders of France, United States, United Kingdom, Germany, Japan, Italy, Canada (in order of rotating presidency), plus the European Union.

Mnemonic: JUICE GF (Japan, UK, Italy, Canada, EU / Germany, France).

52nd G7 Summit

15–17 June 2026 · Évian-les-Bains, France

India attended as an invited partner — Modi's seventh consecutive G7. At outreach sessions including "Forging New Partnerships and Rebuilding International Solidarity," PM Modi proposed IMPACT — the International Mobilization Partnership for Accelerating Connectivity and Trade — and pressed for treating the Global South as an equal partner.

  • IORA backing: India officially supported Canada becoming a Dialogue Partner of the Indian Ocean Rim Association (IORA).
  • Nuclear Energy Partnership: Canada recognised as an essential energy exporter; Security Energy Partnership advanced, highlighted by a CAD $2.6 billion commercial deal between Canada's Cameco and India's Department of Atomic Energy for long-term uranium supply spanning 2027–2035.
  • India–UK: reviewed progress under India-UK Vision 2035; reaffirmed commitment to early implementation of CETA (Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement) — tech, defence, education partnerships.

Fact Box · COP-30 (30th UN Climate Change Conference)
Date
10–21 November 2025
Venue
Belém, Pará, Brazil
Host
Brazil
Focus
"Implementation COP" — first COP held in the Amazon region

Major outcomes:

  • Belém Political Package (Global Mutirão) adopted — focus on implementing the Paris Agreement rather than negotiating new targets.
  • Tropical Forests Forever Facility (TFFF) launched by Brazil for long-term, performance-based tropical forest conservation financing. Long-term target: US$125 billion; initial commitments: ~US$6.7 billion; 20% of payments earmarked for Indigenous Peoples and Local Communities (IPLCs). India joined as an Observer.
  • Tripling of adaptation finance by 2035 endorsed to strengthen resilience in vulnerable countries.
  • 59 indicators adopted under the Global Goal on Adaptation (GGA) to monitor progress.
  • Launch of RAIZ (Resilient Agriculture Investment for Net-Zero Land Degradation) to restore degraded farmland and mobilise finance.
  • Belém 4× Sustainable Fuels Pledge launched — quadruple sustainable fuel use by 2035; India joined this initiative.
  • Continued operationalisation of the Loss and Damage Fund for climate-vulnerable countries.
  • NO agreement on a mandatory global phase-out of fossil fuels — one of the summit's biggest criticisms.
