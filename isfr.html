0. What Qualifies as "Forest" (FSI Definition)

FSI's Working Definition of "Forest Cover"

Any patch of land ≥ 1 hectare in area, with a tree canopy density of >10%, is classified as forest cover — irrespective of land ownership, legal status, or the type of species (natural forest, plantation, orchard, bamboo, etc.).

  • Canopy density > 10%
  • Area ≥ 1 hectare
  • Tree height ≥ 2 metres

This is a purely satellite/remote-sensing definition — it has nothing to do with legal or ownership status. A mango orchard or roadside plantation meeting these three criteria counts as "forest cover," even though it is not legally notified forest land. This is exactly why "forest cover" and "recorded forest area" figures differ (see Section 14).

Published every two years. First published in 1987. ISFR-2023 is the 18th report in the series.

2. National Forest Policy Benchmark

Under the National Forest Policy, 1988, 33% of the country's total land area is meant to be under forest cover — 1/3rd for plains and a higher share prescribed for hilly/mountainous regions in the policy's spirit.

Actual vs Target

India's actual forest area: 21.76% of total land area — short of the 33% target.

States/UTs Meeting Target

Only 19 States/UTs currently have more than 33% forest area.

Q2. With reference to the FSI's definition of "forest cover" used in ISFR-2023, which of the following combinations of criteria is correct?

(a) Canopy density >10%, area ≥1 hectare, tree height ≥2m   (b) Canopy density >40%, area ≥1 hectare, tree height ≥5m
(c) Canopy density >10%, area ≥1 km², tree height ≥2m   (d) Canopy density >25%, area ≥0.5 hectare, tree height ≥2m
Show Answer
(a). "Forest cover" is any patch of land ≥1 hectare with canopy density >10% and tree height ≥2m — irrespective of legal status, ownership, or land-use category.

Q3. With reference to forest and tree cover in India as per ISFR-2023, which one of the following sequences correctly represents the descending order of area?

(a) Forest cover > Total green cover > Tree cover   (b) Total green cover > Forest cover > Tree cover
(c) Tree cover > Forest cover > Total green cover   (d) Total green cover > Tree cover > Forest cover
Show Answer
(b). Total green cover (8,27,357 km²) > Forest cover (7,15,343 km²) > Tree cover (1,12,014 km²), since Total green cover is the sum of the other two.
