Fundamental Physics Laws & Equations

Exact mathematical formulas for physics textbooks and competitive exams, formatted with full vector calculus and differential/integral representations.

1. Maxwell's Equations

Maxwell's equations can be written in two forms: Integral Form (calculating over lines, surfaces, and volumes) and Differential Form (calculating at a specific point).

Equation Name Integral Form Differential Form What it Means Mathematically
Gauss's Law for Electricity $\oint \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{A} = \frac{Q_{\text{enclosed}}}{\varepsilon_0}$ $\nabla \cdot \vec{E} = \frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0}$ The total electric flux through a closed surface depends on the enclosed charge ($Q$).
Gauss's Law for Magnetism $\oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{A} = 0$ $\nabla \cdot \vec{B} = 0$ The total magnetic flux through any closed surface is zero (no isolated magnetic poles).
Faraday's Law of Induction $\oint \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{\ell} = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$ $\nabla \times \vec{E} = -\frac{\partial \vec{B}}{\partial t}$ A time-varying magnetic field ($\Phi_B$) induces an electromotive force or electric field.
Ampere-Maxwell Law $\oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{\ell} = \mu_0 \left( I + \varepsilon_0 \frac{d\Phi_E}{dt} \right)$ $\nabla \times \vec{B} = \mu_0 \left( \vec{J} + \varepsilon_0 \frac{\partial \vec{E}}{\partial t} \right)$ Magnetic fields are generated by electric currents ($I$) and changing electric fields ($\Phi_E$).

Variables Guide:

$\vec{E}$ = Electric field
$\vec{B}$ = Magnetic field
$\varepsilon_0$ = Permittivity of free space
$\mu_0$ = Permeability of free space
$\rho$ = Charge density
$\vec{J}$ = Current density

2. Ampere's Circuital Law

For a regular straight wire carrying current, the simplified formula derived from Ampere's Law to find the magnetic field ($B$) at a distance ($r$) is:

$$B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}$$
  • $B$ = Magnetic field strength (Tesla, T)
  • $\mu_0$ = Permeability of free space ($4\pi \times 10^{-7} \, \text{T}\cdot\text{m/A}$)
  • $I$ = Current flowing through the wire (Amperes, A)
  • $r$ = Distance from the wire (meters, m)

3. Lenz's Law

Lenz's law is represented mathematically as the negative sign in Faraday's Law of Induction. It determines the induced electromotive force ($e$ or $\mathcal{E}$) in a coil with $N$ turns:

$$e = -N \frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$$
  • $e$ = Induced electromotive force (Volts, V)
  • $N$ = Number of turns in the coil
  • $\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$ = Rate of change of magnetic flux over time

Note: The $(-)$ sign is Lenz's law, indicating that the induced EMF opposes the change in flux.

4. Ohm's Law

The standard macroscopic formula for an electrical circuit component is:

$$V = I \cdot R$$
  • $V$ = Potential difference / Voltage (Volts, V)
  • $I$ = Current (Amperes, A)
  • $R$ = Resistance (Ohms, $\Omega$)

Note: In vector calculus form, Ohm's law is written as $\vec{J} = \sigma \vec{E}$, where $\vec{J}$ is current density and $\sigma$ is electrical conductivity.
