The Citizenship Ledger

Constitutional and statutory provisions for Indian citizenship are built on simple core rules once you break down the target groups, key cutoff dates, and requirements.

Constitutional Provisions (Articles 5 to 11)

These articles decided who became a citizen on January 26, 1950.

Article 5 (Citizenship by Domicile)

Who
People residing permanently in India on Jan 26, 1950.
Condition
Must meet any 1 of these three:
  1. Born in India, OR
  2. Either parent was born in India, OR
  3. Resided in India for at least 5 years right before Jan 26, 1950.

Article 6 (Migrated from Pakistan to India)

Who
People who moved from Pakistan to India during Partition.
Condition
Person/Parent/Grandparent born in undivided India AND met one of these based on the July 19, 1948 cutoff (the day permit rules started):
  • Before July 19, 1948: Became a citizen automatically upon living in India.
  • On or After July 19, 1948: Must live in India for 6 months and formally register with an officer.

Article 7 (Migrated to Pakistan, then Returned)

Who
People who left India for Pakistan after March 1, 1947, but came back to India for permanent resettlement.
Condition
Treated like post-July 19, 1948 migrants — must live in India for 6 months before applying for registration.

Article 8 (Persons of Indian Origin Living Abroad)

Who
People born in undivided India (or parents/grandparents born there) who were living in foreign countries (e.g., UK, USA).
Condition
Simply register as an Indian citizen at the Indian diplomatic consulate in that country.

Articles 9, 10 & 11 (Foundational Rules)

  • Article 9: Banned dual citizenship (voluntary foreign citizenship = automatic loss of Indian status).
  • Article 10: Citizenship continues until Parliament makes a law changing it.
  • Article 11: Gives Parliament absolute authority to regulate citizenship (which led to the Citizenship Act of 1955).
2

Acquisition of Citizenship (Citizenship Act, 1955)

How to get citizenship after January 26, 1950.

A. By Birth (in India)

  • Jan 26, 1950 – July 1, 1987: Born in India = Automatic citizen (regardless of parents' nationality).
  • July 1, 1987 – Dec 3, 2004: Born in India = Citizen ONLY IF at least 1 parent is an Indian citizen.
  • Dec 3, 2004 Onwards: Born in India = Citizen ONLY IF both parents are citizens OR 1 parent is a citizen and the other is not an illegal migrant.
Exception

Children of foreign diplomats stationed in India or enemy aliens do not get citizenship by birth.

B. By Descent (Born outside India)

  • Jan 26, 1950 – Dec 10, 1992: Father must be an Indian citizen at the time of birth.
  • Dec 10, 1992 – Dec 3, 2004: Either parent (mother or father) is an Indian citizen.
  • Dec 3, 2004 Onwards: Birth must be registered at an Indian consulate within 1 year (with a declaration that the minor holds no foreign passport).

C. By Registration (For PIOs & Spouses)

Must not be an illegal migrant. Requires applying after fulfilling continuous stay requirements:

  • 7 Years Residence: For Persons of Indian Origin (PIO) or non-citizens married to an Indian citizen.
  • 5 Years OCI Status: Cardholders who have lived in India for 12 continuous months before applying.

D. By Naturalisation (For Foreigners)

For non-citizens seeking citizenship who fulfill these criteria:

  • Not a citizen of a country that bans Indians from naturalising.
  • Agrees to renounce original citizenship.
  • Resided in India (or worked for the Indian Govt) for 12 months continuous before applying, plus 11 years out of the previous 14 years.
  • Good character + fluent in an 8th Schedule language.

E. By Incorporation of Territory

When a foreign region becomes part of India (e.g., Pondicherry, Goa), the Central Government specifies which inhabitants automatically become Indian citizens.

F. Special Provisions: Assam Accord (Section 6A)

Designed to handle migrants entering Assam from Bangladesh:

  • Entered before Jan 1, 1966: Full citizens.
  • Entered Jan 1, 1966 – March 25, 1971: Must register; granted citizen rights, but voting rights suspended for 10 years.
  • Entered after March 25, 1971: Identified as illegal migrants / foreigners.
3

Loss of Citizenship (3 Methods)

Renunciation (Voluntary)

You voluntarily sign a form giving up citizenship.

Effect on Children: Minor kids also lose citizenship, but can reclaim it within 1 year of turning 18.

Termination (Automatic)

You voluntarily take a foreign passport → Indian citizenship ends instantly (enforcing single citizenship).

Deprivation (Compulsory Cancellation by Govt)

Penalty applied to naturalised/registered citizens for:

  1. Obtaining citizenship by fraud.
  2. Disloyalty to the Constitution.
  3. Assisting an enemy nation during war.
  4. 2+ years of imprisonment abroad within 5 years of citizenship.
  5. Continuous residency abroad for 7 years without notifying the government.

Background & Evolution of OCI

  • L.M. Singhvi Committee (Sept 2000): A High-Level Committee on the Indian Diaspora, chaired by Dr. L.M. Singhvi, recommended amending the Citizenship Act, 1955 to grant dual citizenship to Persons of Indian Origin (PIOs).
  • Scheme Rollout (Dec 2, 2005): Following the Citizenship (Amendment) Act, 2005, the OCI scheme was launched for PIOs of all countries, except those from Pakistan and Bangladesh.
  • Legal Nature (No Dual Citizenship): OCI is not true dual citizenship. Article 9 of the Constitution strictly prohibits dual nationality. OCI is merely a statutory status providing specific economic, educational, and travel privileges.
  • CAA 2015 Amendment: Officially renamed the category to "Overseas Citizen of India Cardholder" (OCIC) and formally merged the former PIO Card scheme into the OCI Card scheme.

Eligibility to Register as an OCI Cardholder

Under Section 7A of the Citizenship Act, 1955, any foreign citizen (except an illegal migrant) is eligible to register as an OCI Cardholder if they fulfill any one of the following criteria:

  • Historical Connection: Was an Indian citizen on or after January 26, 1950.
  • Eligibility at Independence: Was eligible to become an Indian citizen on January 26, 1950.
  • Territorial Accession: Belonged to a territory that became part of India after August 15, 1947 (e.g., Goa, Pondicherry, Sikkim).
  • Lineage: Is a child, grandchild, or great-grandchild of a person who meets any of the three criteria above.
  • Minor Children: A minor child whose both parents are Indian citizens, or whose one parent is an Indian citizen.
  • Foreign Spouse: A foreign-origin spouse of an Indian citizen or of an OCI Cardholder, provided the marriage has been registered and has continuously subsisted for at least 2 years prior to the application.
Absolute Exclusion — Exam Trap for BPSC

No person who has ever been a citizen of Pakistan or Bangladesh (or any other country specified by the Central Government) is eligible to register as an OCI Cardholder, regardless of their lineage or marriage.
