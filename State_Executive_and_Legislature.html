Constitutional Position of the Governor

Place in the Constitution

  • Constitution envisages the same pattern of government in the States as at the Centre — a parliamentary system
  • Arts 153–167 in Part VI deal with the Government of the State; the State executive = Governor, Chief Minister, Council of Ministers and Advocate General
  • The Governor is the executive head of the State, also called the constitutional head (not the CM, Speaker or HC Judge)
  • Art 153: there shall be a Governor for each State; Art 154(1): executive power of the State is vested in him, exercised directly or through subordinate officers per the Constitution
  • All executive works of the State are formally done in the name of the Governor

Responsibility and Relationships

  • The Governor is responsible to the President — not the PM, Legislative Assembly or CM
  • Holds office at the pleasure of the President, which in effect means the Union Government
  • Remains in office as long as the President wishes — unlike Election Commissioner, SC Judges and Lok Sabha Speaker
  • Is an integral part of the State Legislature (Legislature = Governor + Assembly + Council where it exists); he has judicial powers too (pardon, Art 161)
  • Acts on the advice of the CoM headed by the CM except in discretionary matters (Art 163(1)) — see Ch 13

Elected vs Nominated Governor — Constituent Assembly

The Debate

  • Draft Constitution planned an elected Governor; the original proposal was dropped after much debate in the Constituent Assembly
  • In favour of election: Krishnamachari, B.G. Kher, J.B. Pant; in favour of nomination: B.R. Ambedkar and Jawaharlal Nehru
  • Finally by general consensus a nominated Governor (appointed by the President) was accepted

Reasons for Dropping Election

  • Would have increased election expenditure — meant another election
  • Avoids clash of political ambition of CM and Governor: an elected Governor might consider himself superior to the CM; election fought on major issues, they would pursue different policies
  • Governor had to function under the Parliamentary System
  • Otherwise the Centre would not have effective control over the State
MCQ Trap / Source ConflictThe key for the “why elected Governor was dropped” question is (1, 3, 4), yet the explanation also lists clash of policies from election on major issues (option 2) and Centre’s lack of control; both appear in the explanation. Learn all reasons above.

Appointment and Qualifications

Appointment

  • Appointed by the President (Art 155) by warrant under his hand and seal — not by PM, CJI, Chief Justice of High Court, Lok Sabha Speaker or Central Cabinet
  • Art 155 deals with appointment — it is not the Article for removal
  • Appointed under Art 153–155; generally one person is Governor of one State, but after the 7th Constitutional Amendment Act 1956 the same person can be Governor of two or more States (Assertion “cannot function as Governor of two States” is false; Art 153 reading “a Governor for each State” does not prevent it)

Qualifications (Art 157)

  • (i) Citizen of India; (ii) age over 35 years (completed 35)
  • Citizenship by birth is not required
  • Art 158: Governor shall not be a member of either House; so he need not be eligible for Lok Sabha membership
  • Article 157 prescribes qualifications (not Arts 156, 159, 160)

Sarkaria Commission (constituted 1983; Report 1988) on Governor’s Appointment

  • Governor should be an eminent person and not belong to the State where posted
  • A detached figure without intense political links; should not have taken part in politics in recent years; not a member of the ruling party
  • State Chief Minister should have a say in the appointment
  • Others in the options: First Administrative Reforms Commission (1966), Rajamannar Committee (1969), National Commission to Review the Working of the Constitution (2000)

Oath of Office

Art 159

  • Every Governor and every person discharging the Governor’s functions makes and subscribes an oath or affirmation before entering office
  • Administered by the Chief Justice of the High Court exercising jurisdiction over the State, or in his absence the senior-most Judge of that Court available — not the President, CJ of Supreme Court or outgoing Governor
  • The process of oath/affirmation is given in Art 159

Tenure, Resignation and Removal

Term and Resignation

  • 5 years from the date he enters upon his office (Art 156(3)) — subject to the pleasure of the President (156(1))
  • Resigns at any time by letter to the President (156(2))
  • 156(4): Governor continues after expiry of term until his successor enters office — so a Governor can continue beyond 5 years and can be removed before completing term

Removal

  • Removed by the President at his pleasure, in effect on advice of the Union Council of Ministers; the State Legislature has no power to remove by resolution
  • The Constitution lays down no procedure for removal of the Governor, and has no provision for his impeachment; he may be dismissed for unconstitutional acts
  • Dismissals: Prabhudas Patwari (Governor of Tamil Nadu) removed October 1980 — the first Governor dismissed; Raghukul Tilak (Governor of Rajasthan) dismissed August 1981

Emoluments and Multiple Governorships

Emoluments

  • Salary, allowances and office expenditure are charged on the Consolidated Fund of each State (Art 202(3)(a)) — not the Consolidated Fund of India or the State Contingency Fund
  • Art 158(4): emoluments and allowances cannot be diminished during his term of office
  • Art 158(3A): when one person is Governor of two or more States, emoluments are allocated among the States in such proportion as the President by order determines — not by the Governor’s wish or the Home Ministry
8

Executive Powers

Appointments and Executive Authority

  • Executive power of the State vested in him (Art 154); all executive action is in his name
  • Appoints the Chief Minister and, on the advice of the CM, the Council of Ministers (Art 164(1)) — not a discretionary act
  • Appoints the Advocate General (Art 165): a person qualified to be a High Court Judge; appointed on CM’s advice
  • Appoints Members of the State Public Service Commission on CM’s advice
  • Does not appoint High Court Judges: Judges are appointed by the President (as with Supreme Court judges) after consultation with the CJI, the Governor and, for a Judge other than the Chief Justice, the Chief Justice of the High Court (Art 217(1)); Governor only advises
  • Makes rules for convenient transaction of State business (Art 166(3)) — not discretionary
9

Legislative Powers

Powers over the Legislature

  • An integral part of the State Legislature; he is its part — the statement “not part of the Legislature” is false
  • Right to address and send messages to the House; power of summoning, proroguing and dissolving the Legislative Assembly
  • Nominates one-sixth of members of the Legislative Council (Art 171(3)(e))
  • Terminating a CM who lost the Assembly’s confidence and asking the government to prove majority are exercised on his own judgment

Anglo-Indian Nomination (Ended)

  • Governor could nominate one Anglo-Indian member to the Assembly if the community was inadequately represented (Art 333)
  • 104th Constitutional Amendment Act 2019 did not extend the arrangement beyond 25 January 2020 — nomination has ended

Bill Needing Governor’s Recommendation

  • If a Bill requiring the Governor’s recommendation is introduced without it, passed, and the Governor then gives assent, the Act is not invalid (Rajasthan case)
10

Bills, Assent and Reservation for President

Art 200 — Governor and Bills

  • A Bill passed by the Legislature (both Houses if bicameral) is presented to the Governor under Art 200, who may: give assent, withhold assent, reserve it for the President, or return it (not a Money Bill) for reconsideration
  • If passed again by the Legislature, the Governor may assent or reserve it for the President
  • No time limit for returning a Bill — “as soon as possible” is not defined and is left to his discretion
  • Art 200 is the Article for reserving a Bill — not Art 169, 199, 201, 202, 166, 239, 240 or 356
  • These choices are discretionary powers

Art 201 — President

  • For a Bill reserved by the Governor, the President declares whether to give assent or not
  • The President cannot, on his own, reserve a State Bill — it must be reserved and sent by the Governor
11

Ordinance-Making Power

Art 213

  • Governor promulgates an ordinance when the State Legislature is not in session (recess) and circumstances require immediate action
  • Has the same force and effect as an Act of the Legislature assented to by the Governor
  • Must be laid before the Legislative Assembly (both Houses if Council exists); ceases six weeks after reassembly, or earlier if the Assembly passes a resolution disapproving it and the Council agrees
  • Needs approval of the State Legislature — not the President or Council of Ministers
  • Art 212 bars courts from inquiring into legislative proceedings; Art 213 (not Art 123, 155, 156, 208, 214, 219, 220) is the Governor’s ordinance power
12

Pardoning Power

Art 161

  • Grants pardons, reprieves, respites, remissions and can suspend, remit, commute the sentence of anyone convicted of an offence against a law relating to a matter within the executive power of the State
  • Governor cannot pardon a death sentence
  • Statement “Governor has no judicial powers” is false (Art 161)
MCQ Trap / Source ConflictThe key calls “Governor has no emergency powers” accurate (emergency powers are the President’s), yet he reports under Art 356 and uses discretion in dissolving the Assembly (Ch 13).

Discretionary Powers and Art 163

Rule

  • Art 163(1): there is a Council of Ministers with the CM at its head to aid and advise the Governor — except where the Constitution requires him to act in discretion
  • The Constitution explicitly mentions spheres of discretion
  • Courts cannot inquire into the advice tendered by Ministers (Art 163(3))

Immunities and Legal Protections

Art 361

  • 361(1): Governor and President not answerable to any court for exercise of powers and duties; no Court can compel Governor to perform a duty
  • 361(2): no criminal proceedings instituted or continued during term — complete criminal immunity
  • 361(4): civil proceedings may be instituted for personal acts after two months’ written notice — so “cannot be sued for any act” is false
  • Reason for Assertion–Reason question: President is impeached; Governor may be dismissed for unconstitutional acts (156(1))
  • Art 74(2)/163(3): courts cannot inquire into Ministers’ advice to the President/Governor

Rajpramukh, Reorganisation and J&K Governor

Rajpramukh

  • Rajpramukh was an administrative title from independence 1949–1956
  • States Reorganisation Commission (formed by Centre, December 1953; Justice Fazl Ali, K.M. Panikkar, H.N. Kunzru) recommended abolition
  • Abolished by the 7th Constitutional Amendment with effect from 1 November 1956; Governor replaced it
  • Gurumukh Nihal Singh was appointed first Governor of Rajasthan on 25 October 1956 (not 1 Nov)

J&K

  • Art 370 gave J&K special status in Part XXI
  • Before 1965 the executive head (Sadar-e-Riyasat) was elected by J&K’s Legislative Assembly; the 6th Amendment of the State Constitution of J&K renamed him Governor, appointed by the President
  • J&K Reorganisation Act 2019: UT of J&K with legislature and UT of Ladakh without; both administered by the President through Lieutenant Governors

Union Territories — Lt Governors and Delhi CM

Administration

  • Art 239(1): UTs administered by the President through an administrator he appoints with designation he specifies, unless Parliament provides otherwise
  • In a UT with a legislature, the CM is appointed by the President (Art 239AA for Delhi) — not by the Lt Governor

Constitution of the State Legislature

Framework

  • Arts 168–212 in Part VI deal with the State Legislature; Art 168: Legislature = Governor + Legislative Assembly and, where it exists, Legislative Council
  • Art 168(2): with two Houses, they are the Legislative Council (Upper House) and Legislative Assembly (Lower House); with one House it is the Legislative Assembly
  • Unicameral State: Governor + Assembly; bicameral State: Governor + Assembly + Council
  • Only 6 States have Councils: Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Bihar, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh
  • Councils absent: Madhya Pradesh (though the 7th Amendment 1956 provides for it), Tamil Nadu, Rajasthan, Kerala, Himachal Pradesh, Delhi
  • State Legislature follows Lok Sabha rules when it has no rule on a matter
3

Legislative Council

Creation and Abolition (Art 169)

  • Parliament by law abolishes or creates a Council if the Assembly passes a resolution by majority of total membership and not less than two-thirds of members present and voting
  • The resolution does not oblige the Union Government to initiate legislation (and none on the Governor to reserve it for the President); Art 368, 69 and 269 are wrong options (amendment, VP oath, Union-assigned taxes)
  • Art 169 is creation/abolition; Art 171 is composition; Council can be abolished but not dissolved — Rajya Sabha neither; Lok Sabha and Assemblies can be dissolved, not abolished

Composition (Art 171)

  • Maximum one-third of Assembly strength; minimum 40; a larger Council than half the Assembly is impossible; Chairman and Deputy chosen by the Council (Art 182), not nominated by Governor
  • UP: 100 members — largest Council
  • 171(4): elected members by proportional representation, single transferable vote, secret ballot
  • Composition currently governed by Sec 10 and Schedule 3 of Representation of People Act 1950 (Art 171(2))

Legislative Assembly

Composition

  • Members chosen by direct election, universal adult suffrage, from territorial constituencies (Art 170)
  • Strength between 60 and 500 (not 450, 400, 550); Sikkim exception 32; Art 170(2): population-to-seat ratio same throughout State
  • 84th Amendment 2001: number fixed until first census after 2026 is published
  • Election by the Election Commission of India (Art 324), not State EC
  • Smallest: Sikkim (32) among States, Puducherry (33) among UTs; largest UP (403)
  • Only 3 UTs have assemblies: Delhi, Puducherry, J&K; J&K (90 elected + 5 nominated, Sec 14(3) J&K Reorganisation Act 2019; 24 seats in PoK vacant)
  • Ladakh UT has no legislature (Delhi/J&K/Puducherry have); separate LGs for J&K and Ladakh from 5 Aug 2019

Presiding Officers — Speaker and Deputy Speaker

Speaker

  • Elected by Assembly from among its members; usually in office during life of Assembly; Art 178; rights/functions same as Lok Sabha Speaker
  • Casting vote in a tie; link between House and Governor; Deputy Speaker acts in his absence
  • Vacates office if he ceases to be member, resigns to the Deputy Speaker (Art 179(b)), or is removed by a resolution of the Assembly
  • On dissolution Speaker continues until immediately before the first meeting of the new Assembly (179(c))
  • Presiding officer of the Council: Chairman; on defection (Tenth Schedule) Speaker/Chairman decides

Sessions, Proceedings and Procedure

Governor and the House

  • Governor summons, prorogues (Art 174(2)); delivers special address at first session after general election and first session of each year (Art 176) to inform of causes of summons
  • Quorum (Art 189(3)): 10 or 1/10 of total, whichever is greater
  • Business in official language(s) of State, Hindi or English (Art 210(1)); Advocate General cannot vote; House cannot discuss conduct of an SC/HC Judge; proceedings not invalid for irregularity
  • Assembly takes part in election of President, Rajya Sabha members, Council members — not Vice-President
  • MP order: oath/affirmation → Governor’s address → introduction of ministers → mention of death

Rajasthan Assembly Rules

  • Adjournment Motion: requires Presiding Officer’s consent; limited to a specific matter of recent occurrence; cannot raise a question of privilege; only one per sitting
  • Committee on Subordinate Legislation (Rule 240/241) examines orders under delegated power, may examine Bills with delegation; Speaker does not refer such Bills; Home Department’s estimates not under Estimates Committee ‘A’

Disqualification of Members

Art 191 and 192

  • Art 191(1): grounds; Art 192: Governor decides, obtaining and following the Election Commission’s opinion; his decision final
  • Tenth Schedule (defection): Speaker (Assembly) or Chairman (Council) decides; Chief Minister, High Court, State EC do not
  • RPA 1951: sentence ≥2 years disqualifies from conviction plus 6 years after release — not permanent
8

Bills, Money Bills and Finance

Ordinary and Money Bills

  • Ordinary Bill passed by Assembly: Council can withhold 3 months; passed again, 1 month more — total 4 months (Art 197)
  • Money Bills introduced only in Assembly; Council returns within 14 days; Assembly may reject recommendations; Speaker decides whether a Bill is a Money Bill; Art 199 defines; Art 207(1) needs Governor’s recommendation and cannot be introduced in Council
  • Budget disclosed before presentation: Finance Minister resigns; punishment under Official Secrets Act 1923

Contingency Fund

  • Established by the State Legislature by law as an imprest; operated by the Governor for urgent/unforeseen expenditure pending authorisation under Art 205/206
9

Chief Minister and Council of Ministers

Appointment

  • Art 164(1): CM appointed by the Governor; other Ministers by the Governor on CM’s advice; CM and Ministers are not “selected” by the Governor — chosen by the majority party; Ministers hold office at Governor’s pleasure
  • CM generally presides over Cabinet; Governor does not exercise all functions on CM’s advice (discretion exists); State executive = Governor, CM, CoM, Advocate General (Arts 153–167)
  • 164(1A) 91st Amendment: ≤15% of Assembly, ≥12 ministers; 164(4): six months without membership (person must be qualified under Art 173, not merely a voter); 164(5): pay by Legislature law, else Second Schedule
  • 164(2): CoM collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly; 164(3): oaths per Third Schedule
  • J&K CM tenure: 6 years earlier; now Assembly term 5 years (Reorganisation Act 2019)

Art 167 Duties of CM

  • Communicate all CoM decisions and legislative proposals to Governor; furnish information on request; submit for CoM consideration a matter decided by a Minister but not by the Council, if Governor requires; NDC participation is only an administrative duty

State Secretariat and Chief Secretary

Administration

  • Secretariat is the apex wing of State administration; framer of procedure principles for State government work
  • Chief Secretary: chosen by CM, appointed by Governor; no fixed tenure; head of administrative machinery; all files go to the Cabinet through him
