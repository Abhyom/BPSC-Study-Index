Plant Nutrition — The Element Ledger

Every macro- and micronutrient, its function, and its deficiency disease — arranged by exam priority tier.

Yellow — Concept Blue — Fact / Number Green — Person / Institution Red — MCQ Trap

01Nutrition Basics

  • Every organism is an open system linked to its environment by a continuous exchange of energy and materials.
  • In ecosystems, plants and other photosynthetic autotrophs transform inorganic compounds into organic ones.
  • Plants need sunlight as the energy source for photosynthesis, plus inorganic raw materials — water, CO₂, and inorganic ions.
  • CO₂ is obtained from air, diffusing into leaves through stomata.
  • Most vascular plants obtain water and minerals from soil through roots.
  • Plants require 9 macronutrients and at least 8 micronutrients to sustain life.
  • Plants receive their nutrients mainly from soil; of the ~17 essential elements, only C, O, H are absorbed from air — the rest (including N) mainly from soil, taken up by roots along with water.

02Macro vs Micro — Classification

  • Macronutrients — required in relatively large quantities. 9 total: 6 major ingredients of organic compounds (C, O, H, N, S, P) + K, Ca, Mg.
  • Micronutrients — required in very small quantities. 8 total: Fe, Cl, Cu, Zn, Mn, Mo, B, Ni. Most function as cofactors — non-protein helpers in enzymatic reactions.
MCQ Trap Sodium (Na) is NOT an essential plant nutrient — it is repeatedly used as the "odd one out" against Ca, K, Mg in BPSC-style questions.

03Macronutrients — Tier 1: Main (C · H · O)

Structural backbone elements — sourced mainly from air & water
ElementCore RoleSourceDeficiency / Notes
CCarbon Forms the backbone of most plant biomolecules — proteins, starches, cellulose. Fixed through photosynthesis; converts CO₂ from air into carbohydrates used to store/transport energy. —
HHydrogen Building sugars, proteins, fats and other plant compounds. Obtained almost entirely from water. H⁺ ions drive the proton gradient for the electron transport chain in photosynthesis & respiration.
OOxygen Component of many organic & inorganic molecules within the plant. As O₂ & CO₂ (mainly air, via leaves) and as H₂O, NO₃⁻, H₂PO₄⁻ & SO₄²⁻ (mainly soil water, via roots). Produced during photosynthesis along with glucose; required for aerobic respiration.

04Macronutrients — Tier 2: Critical (N · P · K)

Highest MCQ frequency — deficiency symptoms are exam favourites
ElementCore RoleUptake FormDeficiency / Disease
NNitrogen 40–50% of dry matter of protoplasm; constituent of amino acids, chlorophyll, some vitamins, nucleic acids (DNA & RNA). Mainly as NO₃⁻ (nitrate); in acid environments (e.g. boreal forests, low nitrification) NH₄⁺ (ammonium) dominates instead. Stunted growth, slow growth, chlorosis.
PPhosphorus Structural component of nucleic acids & membrane phospholipids; part of ATP (cellular energy source). Stimulates root development, tillering, early flowering, hastens maturity. Soil form: polyprotic phosphoric acid (H₃PO₄); taken up mostly as H₂PO₄⁻. Uptake boosted via mycorrhiza mutualism. Intense green coloration / reddening of leaves (chlorophyll shortage); symptoms resemble N-deficiency but are harder to diagnose.
KPotassium Regulates opening/closing of stomata via a potassium-ion pump. — Necrosis or interveinal chlorosis; higher risk of pathogens, wilting, brown spotting, and frost/heat damage.
MCQ Trap Fertilizer component that stimulates early growth = Phosphorus (promotes root development), NOT Nitrogen — N supports rapid leaf/stem growth instead. (71st BPSC-style distinction.)
Fact Nitrogen uptake form asked repeatedly across UPPCS/Jharkhand PCS/RO-ARO — always Nitrate (NO₃⁻), not nitrite, nitric oxide, ammonia, or chloride.

05Macronutrients — Tier 3: Secondary (Mg · S · Ca)

Structural / regulatory secondary macronutrients
ElementCore RoleDeficiency / Disease
MgMagnesium Constituent of the chlorophyll molecule; also a carrier/activator in numerous enzyme reactions, closely associated with energy-supplying phosphorus compounds. (chlorosis pattern, tied to chlorophyll role — not explicitly listed in source but implied via chlorophyll linkage)

06Micronutrients — B · Cl · Cu · Fe · Mn · Ni · Mo · Zn

Trace elements — mostly enzyme cofactors, required in minute quantities
ElementCore RoleDeficiency / Disease
BBoron Affects flowering, fruiting, pollen germination, cell division, active salt absorption. Strongly affects metabolism of amino acids, carbohydrates, calcium and water. Essential for forming/strengthening cell walls, for new cell growth in plant meristem, and for nodule formation in legumes. Associated with translocation of sugars, starches, N and P. Short, thick cells → stunted fruiting bodies and roots. Failure of calcium metabolism → hollow heart in beets and peanuts.
ClChlorine As chloride, necessary for osmosis and ionic balance; also plays a role in photosynthesis. — (osmotic/ionic imbalance)
CuCopper Important for photosynthesis; involved in many enzyme processes, manufacture of lignin (cell walls), and grain production. Chlorosis.
FeIron Necessary for photosynthesis; acts as an enzyme cofactor; helps electron transport. Not a structural part of chlorophyll, but essential for its synthesis. Interveinal chlorosis and necrosis.
MnManganese Necessary for photosynthesis, including chloroplast building. Coloration abnormalities — discolored spots on leaves.
NiNickel Absorbed by higher (vascular) plants as Ni²⁺. Essential for activation of urease, an enzyme in nitrogen metabolism that processes urea. In lower (non-vascular) plants, activates several other enzymes. Without nickel, toxic levels of urea accumulate → necrotic lesions.
MoMolybdenum Cofactor to enzymes building amino acids; involved in nitrogen metabolism. Part of nitrate reductase (reduces nitrate) and nitrogenase (biological N-fixation) enzymes. — (linked to N-metabolism disruption)
ZnZinc Required in a large number of enzymes; essential role in DNA transcription. Stunted growth of leaves ("little leaf"), caused by oxidative degradation of auxin. Khaira disease of paddy (rice) is due to zinc deficiency.
MCQ Trap Zinc deficiency → auxin degradation → "little leaf"; Zinc deficiency ALSO → Khaira disease of rice. Two separate BPSC-testable consequences of the same deficiency — don't confuse with iron/manganese chlorosis patterns.

07Vascular System — Xylem & Phloem

  • The vein/no-vein split divides the plant kingdom into vascular (higher) and non-vascular (lower) plants.
  • Most plants have xylem & phloem = vascular. Algae and mosses (Bryophyta) lack xylem/phloem = non-vascular.
Xylem vs Phloem — a recurring comparison MCQ
FeatureXylemPhloem
Also calledWater-conducting tissueBast tissue
TransportsWater & minerals from roots to rest of plant (unidirectional, upward)Food from leaves (photosynthesis site) to non-photosynthesizing parts — roots, stems
Composed ofTracheids, vessels, wood parenchyma, wood fibresSieve elements, companion cells, fibres, bast fibres (sclereids), phloem parenchyma

MCQ Trap Girdling (removing bark in a ring near the base) kills a tree because the phloem (innermost soft bark layer, food transport) is cut → roots starved of energy. It is not because water can't rise — xylem lies deeper and is unaffected.

08Stem, Roots & Stomata

Stem Types & Functions

TermDefinition
PetioleAttaches leaf to stem.
ShrubThick, hard stem with branching near the base.
TreeThick, hard stem with branches high on the plant.
CreeperWeak stem which cannot stand upright.
  • The stem transports food from roots to all plant parts, stores food made by leaves, and provides shape & structural support; it bears leaves, flowers, fruits — aiding vegetative reproduction too.
  • Maximum water absorption happens through root hairs — the outermost layer of the zone of cell/root maturation.
  • A nail driven into a tree trunk stays at the same relative position even after years — trees grow in length via apical meristems (tips of roots/shoots) and in girth via lateral meristems under the bark; new growth is added outward around the nail.

Stomata & Transpiration

  • Stomata = small pores in leaf epidermis; guard cells surrounding them regulate gas exchange with the atmosphere; transpiration also occurs through stomata.
  • Opening/closing of stomata is governed by turgor pressure changes in guard cells (driven by water's osmotic flow) — not by nucleus position or protein composition.
  • Day: transpiration pull → water absorbed by roots → transported via xylem → guard cells swell/turgid → pore opens.
  • Night: less water absorbed → guard cells shrink/stiffen → pore closes.
  • Water reaches great heights in trees due to suction pull caused by transpiration — not evaporation or absorption alone.

09Amarbel (Cuscuta / Dodder)

  • Cuscuta is an angiospermic total stem parasite, genus of about 100–170 species, yellow/orange/red in colour.
  • Grows over host plants with inter-twined stems — common name "Devil's Hair".
  • The plant is leafless and rootless (leaves reduced to minute scales).
  • Its twining stem develops haustoria — rootlike structures that penetrate the host stem, forming contact with the host's xylem and phloem, drawing water, minerals, and prepared food.
  • Used in traditional medicine as a purgative, and to treat disorders of the liver, spleen and urinary tract.
MCQ Trap (Assertion-Reason) Cuscuta is indeed a parasitic angiosperm (Assertion TRUE) — but it draws nutrition via haustoria connecting to the host's xylem and phloem, not merely "from the leaves of the host plant" (Reason FALSE as commonly framed).

10Applied Facts

  • Crop-logging = a method of plant analysis for assessing nutrient requirement for crop production. Developed by Clements (USA) for sugarcane cultivation in Hawaii, where fertilizer requirements are strongly influenced by weather/climate.
  • Sulphur is the nutrient most useful for enhancing oil content in the mustard crop.

Plant Hormones — The Phytohormone Ledger

Growth promoters, growth inhibitors, and every commercial/chemical application asked in prelims.

Yellow — Concept Blue — Fact / Number Green — Person / Institution Red — MCQ Trap

01What Are Phytohormones

  • Plant hormones (phytohormones) — organic substances formed in one tissue/organ, transported to other sites where they produce specific growth & development effects.
  • Single molecules, produced within plants, occurring in extremely low concentrations.
  • Unlike animals — where hormone production is restricted to specialized glands — every plant cell is capable of producing hormones.
  • Classified into two broad categories: Growth Promoters (Auxins, Gibberellins, Cytokinins) and Growth Inhibitors (Abscisic Acid, Ethylene).
MCQ Trap Insulin, thyroxine and estrogen are frequently planted as decoys — all are animal hormones, not plant hormones. Correct plant hormones: Auxin, Gibberellin, Cytokinin, Abscisic acid, Ethylene.

02Growth Promoter I — Auxins

  • One of the most important hormone groups; the first growth factors identified as plant hormones.
  • Chief natural auxin: Indole-3-Acetic Acid (IAA, C₁₀H₉O₂N) — the most common auxin in plants.
  • Other natural auxins: indole-3-acetaldehyde, indole-3-pyruvic acid, indole-3-acetonitrile, indole-3-ethanol.
Synthetic auxin-like compounds
Indole-3-butyric acid (IBA)Rooting hormone — promotes root development
1-Naphthaleneacetic acid (NAA)Synthetic auxin
Phenylacetic acidSynthetic auxin
2,4-D (dichlorophenoxyacetic acid)Used as a weedicide
2,4,5-T (trichlorophenoxyacetic acid)Used as a weedicide

Functions of Auxins

  • Primary physiological effect: prolongation (elongation) of cells in the shoot.
  • Controls tropic movements — phototropism and geotropism.
  • Enhances apical growth while retarding growth of axillary buds — this is apical dominance.
  • Promotes differentiation of tissues/organs — e.g. root development in grafting (root initiation).
  • Used to produce parthenocarpic (seedless) fruits.
  • Retards abscission of fruits and leaves.
  • Retards growth of buds — applying auxins allows potatoes to be stored longer in cold storage.

03Growth Promoter II — Gibberellins

  • Discovery traces to the 19th century — a Japanese farmer noticed diseased rice plants growing abnormally thin and tall, calling it "Bakaene" or foolish seedling disease.
  • Caused by infection from the fungus Gibberella fujikuroi; the hormone it secreted was named gibberellin.
  • Later discovered that plants themselves also produce gibberellins, controlling multiple aspects of development across the life cycle.

Functions of Gibberellins

  • Protects the apical meristem from the inhibitory effect of endogenous growth inhibitors such as dormin.
  • Promotes shoot growth mainly by accelerating cell elongation & cell division in the sub-apical meristem region (developing young internodes).
  • Lang (1960) demonstrated a marked rise in gibberellin level at the time of bolting, when a rosette (dwarf) growth form starts growing longitudinally.
  • Gibberellins do NOT affect root growth.
  • Other functions: flowering, seed germination, parthenocarpy, breaking dormancy, cell division in the cambial zone, change in leaf shape/size, and enzyme activity.
  • Used as a spraying agent to increase sugarcane yield by lengthening the stem.

04Growth Promoter III — Cytokinins

  • Organic substances that promote cytokinesis during cell division.
  • Kinetin and zeatin act as cytokinins.
  • Coconut milk naturally contains cytokinin.
  • Control: cell division, morphogenesis, counteraction of apical dominance, breaking of dormancy, and delay of senescence (chlorophyll disappearance & protein degradation).
  • Practical use: keep cut flowers fresh for longer.

05Growth Inhibitor I — Abscisic Acid (ABA)

  • Regulates dormancy of buds and seeds.
  • Accelerates senescence of leaves; promotes ageing and abscission of leaves.
  • Inhibits plant growth, metabolism, and germination of seeds.
  • Inhibits gibberellin-stimulated growth — hence also known as anti-gibberellin.
  • Inhibits gibberellin-induced α-amylase formation in barley aleurone.
  • Used widely as a spraying agent on trees to regulate the dropping of fruits.
  • Drought-tolerance hormone: causes temporary closure of stomata during water shortage, useful for plants in water-deficient soil; functions as an antitranspirant; also known as the "stress hormone".

06Growth Inhibitor II — Ethylene (CH₂=CH₂)

  • Gaseous plant hormone — the only one in gaseous form.
  • Produced in minute quantity but active at extremely low concentrations.
  • Synthesized from methionine, an essential amino acid produced in the chloroplast.
  • Both a growth promoter and a growth inhibitor depending on context.

Effects of Ethylene

  • Enhances respiration rate during fruit ripening — this phenomenon is called "respiratory climacteric".
  • Induces epinasty.
  • Inhibits elongation growth in stems and roots.
  • Stimulates cells to grow isodiametrically rather than longitudinally — enhancing radial growth over elongation growth.
  • Induces root hair formation.
  • Promotes leaf & flower abscission and senescence.
  • Applied to rubber trees to stimulate the flow of latex.
