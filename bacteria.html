01What Is a Virus

  • The word 'VIRUS' comes from the Latin neuter word meaning 'venomous substance'.
  • A virus is an ultramicroscopic (20–300 nm), metabolically inert, infectious agent that replicates only within the cells of living hosts (bacteria, plants, animals); composed of an RNA or DNA core, a protein coat, and — in complex types — a surrounding envelope.
  • The protein coat is called the capsid; its subunit is the capsomere.
  • Genetic material may be single- or double-stranded RNA or DNA.
  • The entire infectious particle is a virion — the active, infectious form — whereas 'virus' refers to the nucleoprotein particle generally.
  • A virus is considered a link between non-living and living matter: non-living outside a host cell (no cellular machinery of its own), but 'living' once inside a host, hijacking host machinery to replicate and eventually destroying the host cell.
  • Viruses lack enzymes necessary for energy generation and therefore cannot be cultured in any synthetic medium.
  • Some viral particles do carry a small number of their own enzymes — e.g. reverse transcriptase, RNA-dependent RNA polymerase, integrase, neuraminidase.
  • Viruses are transmitted between organisms by multiple means — air, water, food, insects, direct contact — not by biological vectors only.
  • Viruses exist in very large numbers in ocean waters and remain highly active — the statement "no virus can survive in ocean waters" is factually incorrect; likewise viruses can infect bacteria (bacteriophages) and can alter transcriptional activity of host cells.
  • A bacteriophage is a virus that infects bacteria — its outer covering is protein, with DNA or RNA inside. The river Ganga is noted for self-cleansing/healing properties partly attributed to bacteriophages that kill bacteria in its water; Phage Therapy uses bacteriophages to treat bacterial infections.
  • The branch of biology studying viruses is Virology.
  • Viruses are on the WHO List of Essential Medicines is NOT correct — rather, certain antiviral drugs/vaccines are; the list covers the most effective/safe medicines needed in a health system generally.
  • DNA viruses: genome of DNA replicated by DNA polymerase; may be double-stranded (dsDNA) or single-stranded (ssDNA) — DNA-based viruses are usually double-stranded, NOT single-stranded (a reversed MCQ trap).
  • RNA-based viruses accumulate MORE genetic mutations than DNA-based viruses (higher mutation rate — lack of proofreading in RNA replication).
MCQ Trap A classic "which is NOT true about viruses" question tests: term 'virus' coined by Beijerinck (not Ivanovsky); genome can be DNA or RNA; DNA viruses are usually double-stranded (not single); RNA viruses mutate faster than DNA viruses.

03Polio (Poliomyelitis)

  • Caused by the poliovirus; spreads person-to-person mainly through infected fecal matter entering the mouth (fecal-oral route), also via contaminated food/water or, less commonly, infected saliva.
  • Infected people may spread the disease for up to 6 weeks even without symptoms.
  • Poliovirus destroys cells of the brain and spinal cord controlling muscle function, causing muscle weakness and paralysis.
  • Jonas Salk developed the first (injectable) polio vaccine in 1952, used from 1955.
  • Albert Sabin developed the oral polio vaccine, in commercial use from 1961.
  • Trivalent OPV covered all three poliovirus types (Type 1, 2, 3).
  • India was declared smallpox-free in 1977 (separate disease, noted alongside polio timeline).
  • WHO gave India its official 'Polio Free' certification on 27 March 2014.
  • In September 2015, WHO declared poliovirus Type 2 eradicated globally — no case since November 1999 — leading to a switch from trivalent to bivalent OPV (Type 1 & 3) in April 2016.

04Rhinovirus — The Common Cold

  • Rhinovirus (Greek 'rhinos' = of the nose) is the most common viral infectious agent in humans and predominant cause of the common cold.
  • Proliferates best at 33–35°C (91–95°F) — the temperature inside the human nose.
  • Three species (A, B, C) comprise around 160 recognized human rhinovirus types, differing by surface protein serotypes.
  • Symptoms: sore throat, runny nose, nasal congestion, sneezing, cough, fatigue, headache.

05Hepatitis-B

  • Caused by the Hepatitis-B virus (HBV) — a partially double-stranded DNA virus (genus Orthohepadnavirus, family Hepadnaviridae) — affects the liver.
  • Can cause acute and chronic infection; may lead to liver scarring/cirrhosis and liver cancer.
  • Spreads via contact with blood, open sores, or body fluids — transmitted much like HIV; the most frequent route is around birth or via childhood blood contact — NOT via water contamination (unlike typhoid/cholera/jaundice).
  • Symptoms: jaundice, fever, prolonged fatigue, loss of appetite, nausea, vomiting, belly pain.
  • Hepatitis-B HAS a vaccine (recommended at birth and up to 18 years) — Hepatitis-C does NOT have a vaccine (reverse of a commonly planted false claim).
  • Globally (WHO 2022 est.): ~254 million infected with Hep-B, ~50 million with Hep-C — several times more than global HIV infections.

06Mumps & Meningitis

Mumps

  • Caused by the mumps virus; spreads via saliva, nasal secretions and close contact.
  • Primarily affects the salivary (parotid) glands — hallmark symptom is swelling of the salivary glands.
  • Initial symptoms: fever, muscle pain, headache, poor appetite, malaise; more severe in adults than children.
  • Complications: meningitis, pancreatitis, permanent deafness, testicular inflammation (rarely causing infertility).

Meningitis

  • Inflammation of the meninges — the three membranes covering the brain and spinal cord — occurring when fluid around them becomes infected.
  • Symptoms: fever, headache, neck stiffness, confusion, vomiting, intolerance to light/loud noise.
  • May be caused by viruses, bacteria, or other microorganisms — meningococcal-bacterial meningitis may show a characteristic rash.

07Herpes & Rabies

Herpes

  • Caused by the herpes simplex virus; causes sores around the mouth/lips and genital organs.

Rabies (Hydrophobia)

  • Caused by Rabies lyssavirus (formerly 'rabies virus'), a neurotropic virus.
  • Transmitted through the saliva of infected animals — dogs, cats, bats, foxes and other wild animals — via bite or scratch.
  • Also called hydrophobia ('fear of water') — patients become afraid of rivers, lakes, seas.
  • Early symptoms: fever, tingling at exposure site; later: violent movements, uncontrolled excitement, fear of water, confusion, loss of consciousness.
  • Once symptoms appear, the result is nearly always death.

08Dengue Fever

  • Transmitted by the bite of an Aedes mosquito (mainly A. aegypti) infected with dengue virus; the tiger mosquito (Aedes albopictus) can also serve as vector.
  • Symptoms begin 3–14 days after infection: high fever, headache, vomiting, muscle/joint pains, characteristic skin rash, retro-orbital (behind-the-eye) pain, bleeding from nose/gums.
  • Also known as 'breakbone fever'.
  • Causes bone marrow suppression → deficiency of blood cells → low platelet count — platelets are the blood component most reduced by dengue.
  • Dengue is viral, NOT protozoan — a frequently planted false statement.

09Chikungunya & Zika Fever

Chikungunya

  • Caused by Chikungunya virus (CHIKV), transmitted by Aedes albopictus (tiger mosquito) and Aedes aegypti.
  • Causes fever and severe joint pain; also muscle pain, headache, nausea, fatigue, rash.
  • NOT contagious — an infected person cannot directly spread it to others (only via mosquito).

Zika Fever

  • Caused by Zika virus; symptoms: fever, red eyes, joint pain, headache, maculopapular rash.
  • Transmitted primarily via Aedes aegypti / Aedes albopictus bite; also sexually transmissible and potentially via blood transfusion.
  • Infection in pregnant women can spread to the baby.
Fact Zika transmission is by the same mosquito that transmits dengue (Aedes) in tropical regions, and sexual transmission is genuinely possible — both correct statements in a common two-part MCQ.

10Yellow Fever & Japanese Encephalitis

Yellow Fever

  • An acute viral haemorrhagic disease transmitted by yellow fever virus via infected mosquito, mainly Aedes aegypti (tiger mosquito Aedes albopictus can also serve as vector).
  • Symptoms: fever, headache, jaundice, muscle pain, nausea, vomiting, fatigue.
  • Prevented by an extremely effective, safe and affordable vaccine.
  • Japanese Encephalitis is the ONE mosquito-borne disease NOT transmitted by tiger mosquitoes among Yellow Fever/Dengue/Chikungunya/JE — JE uses Culex instead.

Japanese Encephalitis (JE)

  • Brain infection caused by the Japanese Encephalitis Virus (JEV).
  • Vector: Culex mosquitoes (main vector) — NOT Anopheles or Aedes.
  • Most infections mild/asymptomatic; occasional brain inflammation causes headache, vomiting, fever, confusion, seizures.
  • No specific treatment or cure — only symptomatic relief; prevention/care are the best approach.
  • Domestic pigs and wild birds are viral reservoirs — keeping pigs away from human settlements helps eradicate JE.
  • Anopheles mosquitoes (besides malaria) can occasionally also serve as vectors for certain encephalitis strains and filariasis in specific regions — a nuanced exception to the "Anopheles = malaria only" assumption.

11AIDS / HIV

  • AIDS = Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome, a spectrum of conditions caused by Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV), a retrovirus (RNA + reverse transcriptase, protease, integrase enzymes).
  • HIV infects T-helper lymphocytes, disabling both B-lymphocytes (antibody production) and other T-lymphocytes (virus-infected cell killing) — rendering the immune system ineffective against HIV and other infections.
  • Transmission: sexual intercourse, blood transfusion, shared needles, mother-to-child (placenta/birth/breast milk) — NOT via mosquitoes or other blood-sucking insects, coughing, sneezing, or casual contact.
  • Symptoms: rapid weight loss, recurring fever/night sweats, extreme tiredness, swollen lymph glands, prolonged diarrhoea, mouth/genital sores, pneumonia, skin blotches, memory loss/depression.
  • Tests: ELISA (first widely used, high sensitivity screening test) confirmed by Western Blot — combined ~99.9% accurate.
  • HIV-1 subtype C (HIV-1C) is the dominant strain in India.
  • Robert Gallo discovered HTLV-III, the AIDS-causing factor.
  • Most-used AIDS medicine: Zidovudine (Azidothymidine), an anti-retroviral.
  • World AIDS Day — 1 December, observed every year since 1988.
  • Tattooing can transmit Hepatitis-B and HIV-AIDS via contaminated equipment (Chikungunya, being mosquito-borne, is NOT tattoo-transmissible).

12Ebola & Nipah Virus

Ebola Virus Disease

  • A rare, deadly haemorrhagic disease in people and non-human primates; first identified in 1976 in two simultaneous outbreaks — Nzara (South Sudan) and Yambuku (DR Congo), a village near the Ebola river, from which the disease takes its name (named after a river, not a city, province, or mountain).
  • Transmission: direct contact with an infected animal (bats, non-human primates) or a sick/dead infected person.
  • Largest outbreak: West Africa, Dec 2013 – Jan 2016 — 28,646 cases, 11,323 deaths.

Nipah Virus Infection

  • Caused by Nipah virus (NiV), an RNA virus normally circulating among fruit bats.
  • First identified in Malaysia, 1998, named after the village Sungai Nipah.
  • Indian outbreaks in Kerala: May 2018 (17 deaths), September 2021 (1 death, 12-yr-old), July–Sept 2024 (2 deaths).
  • Symptoms range from none to fever, cough, headache, shortness of breath, confusion — may worsen to coma.
  • No vaccine or specific treatment exists — management is supportive care only; prevention = avoiding bats, sick pigs, and raw date-palm sap (toddy).
  • For Nipah, several monoclonal antibodies (man-made proteins that bind specific antigens to trigger immune response) have been developed, e.g. m102.4, hu1F5, MBP1F5.

13COVID-19 — Overview & Timeline

  • Coronaviruses are a large family of RNA viruses causing respiratory infections from common cold to severe disease: SARS (2002), MERS/camel flu (2012), and COVID-19.
  • COVID-19 is caused by SARS-CoV-2 (Novel Coronavirus/nCOV); first identified December 2019 in Wuhan, China.
  • WHO declared it a Public Health Emergency of International Concern on 30 January 2020, and a pandemic on 11 March 2020.
  • Symptoms: respiratory issues, fever, cough, shortness of breath, fatigue, loss of smell/taste; severe cases may progress to ARDS via cytokine storm, multi-organ failure, septic shock, severe clotting.
  • ACE2 (Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme 2) is a surface protein/enzyme regulating blood pressure, wound healing, inflammation — it also serves as the cellular entry receptor for SARS-CoV, SARS-CoV-2, and HCoV-NL63.
  • Vaccination stimulates immunity by introducing a live, attenuated form of the virus (per BPSC 2023 framing) or, more generally across platforms, harmless viral components.
  • Viral vector vaccines work by placing the target virus's genetic material inside a modified, harmless different virus (the 'vector'), which then enters cells to trigger spike-protein production and immunity.
  • India's vaccination rollout: 16 Jan 2021 — healthcare workers (Covishield & Covaxin); 2 Feb 2021 — frontline workers; 1 Mar 2021 — age 60+ and 45+ with comorbidities; 1 Apr 2021 — all above 45; 1 May 2021 — all above 18; 3 Jan 2022 — adolescents 15–18; 10 Jan 2022 — precaution booster doses begin; 16 Mar 2022 — children 12–14 (Corbevax only); 10 Apr 2022 — booster doses via private centres for 18+.
  • As of 5 May 2026, India had the second-highest confirmed case count (after USA) at ~45,056,126 cases, and the third-highest death count (after USA and Brazil) at 533,847 deaths.
  • By December 2025, India had administered over 2.2 billion doses overall.

15Bird Flu & Swine Flu

  • Bird flu (avian influenza) is caused by the H5N1 virus; contagious, mainly affects birds (hens, cocks, ducks); spreads to humans via direct hen-contact or (rarely) human-to-human transmission.
  • Tamiflu is an antiviral blocking influenza A and B virus action.
  • Swine Flu is caused by H1N1 — first detected in 2009, named for similarity to viruses found in pigs; the virus gained the ability to spread human-to-human via coughing/sneezing.

16Rotavirus, Foot & Mouth Disease, Rinderpest, Ranikhet

  • Rotavirus — a group of double-stranded RNA viruses (family Reoviridae) — is the most common cause of severe diarrhoea among infants/young children; in India, 1 in every 200 babies dies of diarrhoea caused by rotavirus.
  • Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD) is caused by an Aphthovirus (family Picornaviridae); affects cloven-hoofed animals — cattle, sheep, goats, pigs.
  • Rinderpest (cattle plague) — caused by a virus of family Paramyxoviridae, genus Morbillivirus — was declared eradicated in 2011, the first animal disease ever eliminated in human history.
  • Ranikhet disease is a highly infectious viral disease related to chicken.
  • Blackleg/Black Quarter (bacterial, Clostridium chauvoei), Anthrax (bacterial), Cowpox (viral) and FMD (viral) are all infectious diseases of milching animals.

17Endemic · Epidemic · Pandemic · Epizootic

TermDefinition
EndemicA disease regularly found in a particular place or among a particular group, difficult to eliminate.
EpidemicA large number of cases of a disease occurring in a given population within a short period.
PandemicAn infectious disease spread across a large region — a whole country, multiple continents, or worldwide — affecting a substantial number of people.
EpizooticA disease affecting a large number of animals (not humans) in a particular region within a short time.

01What Is Bacteria

  • Bacteria are single-celled, prokaryotic microorganisms — cosmopolitan, thriving in soil, ocean, and the human gut.
  • The human relationship with bacteria is complex: helpful (curdling milk into yogurt, aiding digestion) as well as destructive (causing disease).
  • Pathogenic bacteria can infect all systems of the human body.

03Salmonella & Botulism

Salmonella / Food Poisoning

  • Salmonella bacilli is responsible for food poisoning — a gram-negative, flagellated, facultatively anaerobic bacillus with flagella around it.
  • Salmonella infection (salmonellosis) affects the intestinal tract; typically transmitted via contaminated water/food.

Botulism

  • A rare but serious illness caused by a toxin attacking the body's nerves, causing breathing difficulty, muscle paralysis, even death.
  • Toxin produced by Clostridium botulinum, and sometimes C. butyricum, C. baratii — all gram-positive bacteria.
  • Botulism is classified as food-borne intoxication (not infection) — the harmful bacteria produce the toxin in low-oxygen environments (e.g. home-canned food).
  • Three forms: Foodborne (home-canned food), Wound (bacteria enter a cut), Infant (spores grow in a baby's intestinal tract — the most common form).

04Dental Caries

  • Tooth decay is mostly caused by interaction in the mouth between bacteria and food particles of carbohydrates — not proteins, fats, or salads.
  • Four criteria required for caries: a tooth surface, caries-causing bacteria, fermentable carbohydrates (e.g. sucrose), and time.
  • Dental caries is caused by bacterial infection, not viral, contaminated water, or hereditary reasons.

05DPT & BCG Vaccines

  • DPT vaccine (Triple Antigen) provides immunity against three bacterial diseases: Diphtheria, Pertussis (Whooping Cough), Tetanus — NOT Polio, T.B., or Smallpox (commonly planted decoys).
  • BCG (Bacillus Calmette-Guérin) is used primarily against Tuberculosis; must be inoculated just after birth (can be given up to 12 months, but effectiveness decreases with delay).
  • ATS (Anti-Tetanus Serum) is used for prophylaxis/treatment of Tetanus — not BCG (a commonly mismatched pair).
  • Correctly matched pair: Malaria — Chloroquine; incorrectly matched decoys: Scurvy–Thiamine (actually Vitamin-C deficiency; thiamine is B1), Tuberculosis–ATS (actually BCG), Tetanus–BCG (actually ATS).

06Sulpha Drugs & Antibiotics

  • Sulpha drugs (sulphonamides) are synthetic antibacterial substances containing the sulphanilamide molecular structure — the first chemical substances systematically used to treat/prevent bacterial infections in humans.
  • Prontosil was the first sulpha drug discovered (1932), by German physician-chemist Gerhard Domagk (1895–1964).
  • Other sulpha drugs: Cibazole (short-acting, effective vs wide range of gram-positive/negative organisms, still used in cattle), sulfadiazine, sulfasalazine, sulfisoxazole, sulfacetamide, sulphaguanidine.
  • Broad-spectrum antibiotics act on both gram-positive and gram-negative bacteria (or a wide range of disease-causing bacteria), unlike narrow-spectrum antibiotics effective against only a specific group. Ampicillin is a commonly used broad-spectrum antibiotic.
  • Chloramphenicol (a.k.a. chloronitromycin) is a broad-spectrum antibiotic; used as an eye ointment for conjunctivitis, and orally/by injection for meningitis, plague, cholera, typhoid.
  • The term 'antibiotic' was coined by Selman Waksman in 1942 — he also discovered streptomycin.
  • Alexander Fleming discovered Penicillin in 1928, from the mould Penicillium notatum, which inhibited bacterial growth.
  • Aspirin and Paracetamol are NOT antibiotics (analgesic/antipyretic) — a frequently repeated "which is an antibiotic" trap alongside Penicillin/Sulphadiazine/Chloramphenicol/Sulphaguanidine as correct answers.

07Antimicrobial Resistance (MDR)

  • Per the Department of Biotechnology (DBT) 'Scoping Report on Antimicrobial Resistance in India' (Nov 2017), main drivers of multi-drug resistance (MDR) include: incorrect antibiotic dosing / self-medication, heavy antibiotic use in livestock farming, high consumption of broad-spectrum antibiotics, injudicious fixed-dose combinations, pharmaceutical pollution, poor sanitation/sewage disposal, poor infection-prevention in healthcare.
  • Genetic predisposition and multiple chronic diseases in patients are NOT listed causes of MDR per the report — a commonly tested exclusion.

01What Is Fungi

  • Fungi form a separate kingdom of living things; a fungus (plural fungi) includes yeast, moulds (molds), and mushrooms.
  • Fungi are eukaryotic organisms (cells have nuclei).
  • The fungal cell wall contains chitin, unlike plant cell walls, which contain cellulose.
  • Most fungi grow as tubular filaments called hyphae; an interwoven mass of hyphae is a mycelium. Hyphal walls are strengthened with chitin, a polymer of N-acetylglucosamine.
  • The study of fungi is Mycology.
  • All fungi are heterotrophs — either parasitic or saprotrophic — never autotrophic.

02Aflatoxins

  • Aflatoxins are a family of toxins produced by certain fungi found on agricultural crops — maize, peanuts, cotton seeds, tree nuts.
  • Main producing fungi: Aspergillus flavus and Aspergillus parasiticus, abundant in warm/humid regions.
  • Contamination can occur in the field, at harvest, or during storage.
  • Aflatoxin is a natural carcinogen linked to increased liver cancer risk — causes DNA damage; prolonged exposure accumulates mutations, raising cancer-cell risk.
  • The liver is the human organ most affected by aflatoxin food-adulterant consumption.

03Ergotism & Amanita

Ergotism

  • Long-term ergot poisoning, traditionally from ingesting alkaloids produced by Claviceps purpurea fungus, which infects rye and other cereals (i.e. caused by consumption of contaminated grains, not vegetables, water, or stale food).
  • Also called ergotoxicosis or Saint Anthony's Fire.
  • Toxins affect the nervous, vascular (vasoconstriction and gangrene), and endocrine systems.
  • In poultry: decreased food intake/growth, necrosis of beak/comb/toes, distorted feather development, enteritis; primarily affects leghorns and other laying breeds.

Amanita & Mushroom Toxins

  • Amanita is a toxic mushroom genus responsible for approximately 95% of fatalities from mushroom poisoning.
  • Most potent toxin present: α-amantin.

01Protozoa · Platyhelminthes · Nematoda

  • Besides virus, bacteria, and fungus, some members of phylum Protozoa, Platyhelminthes (flatworms), and Nematoda (roundworms) cause disease.
  • Parasites like Toxoplasma gondii and Plasmodium spp. cause disease directly; others act via toxins they produce.
  • Fasciola, Schistosoma, and Taenia are all members of phylum Platyhelminthes.
  • Roughly 50 species of phylum Nematoda (roundworms) are found as human parasites.

02Malaria

  • A life-threatening disease transmitted through the bite of the female Anopheles mosquito — primary host is man, secondary host is the female Anopheles.
  • Infected mosquitoes carry the Plasmodium parasite (a unicelled protozoan); released into bloodstream on bite, travels to the liver to mature, then infects red blood corpuscles (RBCs).
  • Within 48–72 hours, parasites inside RBCs multiply, bursting infected cells; symptoms cycle every 2–3 days.
  • Four species infecting humans: Plasmodium vivax, P. ovale, P. malariae, P. falciparum.
  • P. falciparum causes malignant malaria — the most severe form, with the highest death risk.
  • P. vivax is responsible for about 65% of malaria cases in India (and in Asia/South America generally).
  • Symptoms: shaking chills, high fever, profuse sweating, headache, nausea, vomiting.
  • Complications: cerebral malaria (blood vessel swelling in brain), pulmonary oedema, kidney/liver/spleen failure, anaemia (RBC destruction), low blood sugar. The spleen is the organ most classically noted as affected/enlarged.
  • Congenital malaria: an infected mother can pass the disease to her baby at birth.
  • Also transmissible via organ transplant, blood transfusion, shared needles/syringes — malaria is blood-transmitted.
  • Ronald Ross, a British Medical doctor (in British Indian Army), discovered the malarial parasite in the mosquito gastro-intestinal tract in 1897, proving mosquito transmission — won the Nobel Prize for Physiology or Medicine in 1902.
  • Quinine, first isolated in 1820 from cinchona bark, is the age-old malaria drug; other antimalarials: chloroquine, amodiaquine, atovaquone, proguanil, sulphonamides, mefloquine, primaquine.
  • Chemoprophylaxis = regular use of antimalarial drugs to prevent malaria in affected regions.
  • Control: pesticides like DDT against Anopheles; Gambusia affinis fish eats mosquito larvae; ducks also help eradicate larvae.
  • World Malaria Day: 25 April every year.
  • R21/Matrix-M — malaria vaccine developed by Oxford's Jenner Institute + Serum Institute of India (using Novavax's saponin adjuvant tech); WHO-recommended for children in endemic regions, October 2023. RTS,S/AS01 (Mosquirix) was the FIRST WHO-approved malaria vaccine, not R21.
  • Besides malaria, Anopheles mosquitoes are also vectors for certain Encephalitis strains (e.g. JEV in some regions) and Filariasis — NOT Dengue.

03Kala-azar & Sleeping Sickness

Kala-azar (Visceral Leishmaniasis)

  • Caused by protozoan parasite genus Leishmania; chronic, potentially fatal, chiefly tropical.
  • Infects the reticuloendothelial system — found in bone marrow, spleen, liver.
  • Name meaning: 'Black fever' — greyish discolouration of skin (hands, feet, abdomen, face) in light-coloured persons; also fever, weight loss, liver/spleen inflammation.
  • Vector: female sandfly Phlebotomus argentipes — most active in humid environments, warmer months, night (dusk to dawn).
  • Dogs can serve as reservoirs; transmission animal→sandfly→human, also human-human via blood transfusion/shared needles.
  • The second-largest parasitic killer in the world, after malaria.

Sleeping Sickness (African Trypanosomiasis)

  • Caused by Trypanosoma gambiense or T. rhodesiense (unicellular protozoan animals), especially common in tropical Africa.
  • Transmitted by the bite of the tsetse fly (genus Glossina).
  • Stage 1: fever, headache, muscle/joint pain, lymph node inflammation. Stage 2 (weeks to 1–2 years later): brain/spinal cord involvement, personality changes, sleep disturbances, profound lethargy — often fatal if untreated.
  • NOT a mosquito-borne disease — a commonly tested exclusion alongside genuinely mosquito-borne Dengue, Filariasis, Malaria.

04Amoebiasis (Amoebic Dysentery)

  • Caused by anaerobic parasite Entamoeba histolytica (phylum Protozoa).
  • Symptoms may be absent, mild, or severe: abdominal pain, diarrhoea, or bloody diarrhoea.

05Schistosomiasis & Fascioliasis

Schistosomiasis

  • Also known as Snail fever or bilharzia; caused by parasitic flatworms called schistosomes (phylum Platyhelminthes).
  • Urinary tract or intestines may be infected; symptoms: abdominal pain, diarrhoea, bloody stool or blood in urine.
  • In developing countries, malaria, dysentery, and schistosomiasis have taken epidemic form due to unsafe potable water and poor sanitation.

Fascioliasis (Liver Rot)

  • Caused by liver flukes Fasciola hepatica and Fasciola gigantica.
  • Main hosts: ruminants (cattle, sheep); humans get infected by eating raw watercress/water-grown plants contaminated with larvae.
  • Young worms move through intestinal wall, abdominal cavity, liver tissue, into bile ducts.
  • Symptoms: fever, sweating, weight loss, abdominal pain.

06Taeniasis & Neurocysticercosis

  • Taeniasis = intestinal infection caused by three tapeworm species: Taenia solium (pork), T. saginata (beef), T. asiatica — only T. solium causes major health problems.
  • T. solium taeniasis acquired via ingestion of larval cysts (cysticerci) in undercooked/infected pork; T. solium eggs (via poor hygiene/fecal-oral route or contaminated food/water) develop into larvae (cysticerci) in body organs.
  • When larvae enter the central nervous system (brain/spinal cord), the condition is called Neurocysticercosis (NCC) — causes neurological symptoms including epileptic seizures.
  • NCC is a leading cause of seizures/acquired epilepsy and the most common CNS parasitic disease in the developing world.
  • Tennis player Leander Paes was forced off court in 2003 after a cyst (cysticercus granuloma) was detected in his brain — caused by the larval form of the pork tapeworm.

07Filariasis & Ascariasis

Filariasis (Elephantiasis)

  • Caused by the roundworm (nematode) Wuchereria bancrofti; also called elephantiasis — swollen lymph glands.
  • Can affect legs, arms, vulva, breasts, scrotum (hydrocele formation).
  • Vector: female Culex mosquito.
  • Edema is a common symptom, potentially progressing to elephantiasis with thickened skin and massive swelling.

Ascariasis

  • Infection of the small intestine caused by parasitic roundworm Ascaris lumbricoides.
  • Acquired by accidentally ingesting eggs found in soil contaminated by human feces, or uncooked food contaminated by such soil; children often infected via hand-to-mouth contact after playing in contaminated soil.
  • Symptoms: coughing/gagging, wheezing/shortness of breath, blood in mucus, chest discomfort, abdominal swelling/pain, diarrhoea; can cause nausea, vomiting, appetite loss, growth impairment (malabsorption) in children.
  • Treatment: Anthelminthic medications — chenopodium oil, albendazole, mebendazole.

08Guinea Worm Disease (GWD)

  • Dracunculiasis — was an important public health problem in many Indian states before being eradicated in 2000.
  • Caused by a large nematode, Dracunculus medinensis, with a life cycle through two hosts — Man and Cyclops (water fleas).
  • Infection occurs by drinking water containing infected Cyclops carrying guinea worm larvae; initially asymptomatic — about one year later, the female worm forms a painful blister in the skin, usually on the lower limb.

01What Is a Genetic Disease

  • A genetic disease is any disease caused by an abnormality in an individual's genome.
  • Some genetic disorders are inherited from parents; others are caused by acquired changes/mutations in pre-existing genes.
  • Mutations occur either randomly or due to environmental exposure.
  • Genetic disorders passed generation to generation: Albinism, Haemophilia, Muscular Dystrophy, Phenylketonuria, Alkaptonuria.
  • Down syndrome, Klinefelter syndrome, Turner syndrome are genetic disorders caused by chromosomal aberrations rather than single-gene inheritance.
  • Night blindness is NOT a genetic disease — it's caused by Vitamin A deficiency, a commonly planted decoy against Albinism/Haemophilia/Colour blindness (all genuinely genetic).
  • Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS) is NOT a genetic disorder — it's a common gut disorder of unclear cause (decoy against Down syndrome/Haemophilia/Sickle Cell Anaemia).

02Sex Chromosomes & Sex-Linked Inheritance

  • A sex chromosome is either of a pair of chromosomes determining whether an individual is male or female.
  • Besides the sex-determining gene, other genes on the sex chromosomes determine body characters — termed sex-linked characters, and their inheritance is sex-linked inheritance.
  • Males have only one X-chromosome, so one recessive mutation is sufficient to cause an X-linked condition; females (two X-chromosomes) are usually protected by the second, unaffected copy and act as carriers.
  • A characteristic of X-linked inheritance: fathers cannot pass X-linked traits to their sons (sons get the Y from father).

03Albinism

  • A congenital genetic disorder marked by complete/partial absence of melanin pigment in skin, hair, eyes.
  • Increases susceptibility to sunburn and skin cancer; also affects immune-cell granules, raising infection susceptibility.
  • Results from inheritance of recessive gene alleles.
  • Due to absence/defect of tyrosinase, a copper-containing enzyme involved in melanin production.

04Phenylketonuria (PKU)

  • A type of amino acid metabolism disorder; an inherited disease.
  • Due to lack of phenylalanine hydroxylase enzyme, phenylalanine is not converted to tyrosine, raising phenylalanine levels in the body.
  • Can damage the brain → severe intellectual disability / mental idiocy and mental disorders.
  • The 'error' in this inborn error of metabolism refers to an inherited lack of an enzyme (not hormonal overproduction, gland atrophy, or non-disjunction).
  • Dominant gene 'P' codes for phenylalanine hydroxylase in liver cells; in homozygous recessive genotypes, the enzyme's absence causes high phenylalanine in blood/tissue fluid — effects include progressive mental retardation from a few months after birth, seizures, tooth/bone anomalies.

05Haemophilia

  • An inherited condition impairing the body's ability to form blood clots.
  • Caused by a defective gene for blood-clotting factors, located on the X-chromosome — making it an X-linked recessive disease.
  • Generally found in males, while females are carriers — women carry it, men manifest it (since men have only one X-chromosome).
  • Blood clotting can take up to 24 hours due to lack of thromboplastin protein.
  • Not contagious; also known as Bleeder's Disease.
  • Also called 'Royal Disease' because Queen Victoria of England suffered from it.
  • Haemophilia is NOT caused by bacteria, virus, or pollutants — it is purely a hereditary defect.

06Alkaptonuria (AKU)

  • A rare disorder of autosomal recessive inheritance — the body cannot process phenylalanine and tyrosine.
  • Caused by a mutation in the HGD gene, resulting in accumulation of homogentisic acid (HGA).
  • Characteristic sign: sufferers pass dark urine that turns black on standing.
  • Over time, HGA deposition in collagenous tissue causes ochronosis (bluish/black tissue discolouration) and ochronotic osteoarthropathy.

07Muscular Dystrophy & SCID (Bubble Baby Disease)

Muscular Dystrophy

  • Caused by an X-linked recessive gene.
  • Abnormal genes interfere with production of proteins needed to form healthy muscle; an inheritable disease.
  • Caused by expression of a recessive gene on a sex chromosome (not rheumatism, nervous shock, or cerebral haemorrhage).

Bubble Baby Disease (SCID)

  • Also known as Severe Combined Immunodeficiency (SCID), alymphocytosis, Glanzmann-Rinker syndrome, severe mixed immunodeficiency syndrome, thymic alymphoplasia.
  • Children are born without a functioning immune system; historically kept in a sterile plastic bubble — hence the name.
  • Rare genetic disorder from disturbed development of functional T cells and B cells, caused by numerous genetic mutations.
  • Only routine cure: bone marrow transplant (provides a new immune system); gene therapy has shown success in trials but with complications.

08Sickle Cell Disease & Thalassemia

Sickle Cell Disease (SCD)

  • A group of inherited disorders affecting haemoglobin, the main oxygen-carrying protein in RBCs.
  • Gene mutation makes RBCs misshapen — crescent/'sickle'-shaped instead of disc-shaped and flexible — blocking blood flow.
  • A chronic single-gene disorder causing chronic anaemia, acute painful episodes, organ infarction/damage, reduced life expectancy.
  • Complications: chronic pain, stroke, lung problems, eye problems, infections, kidney disease.
  • National Sickle Cell Anaemia Elimination Mission (NSCEM) — launched by PM Narendra Modi in Shahdol, Madhya Pradesh, July 2023 — targets tribal populations; aims to eliminate sickle cell genetic transmission by 2047 in India.

Thalassemia

  • Inherited blood disorders characterized by abnormal haemoglobin production; body has fewer RBCs and less haemoglobin — mild to severe anaemia.
  • Haemoglobin synthesis needs two proteins — alpha and beta; deficiency of either impairs oxygen carriage.
  • Alpha thalassemia = lacking alpha haemoglobin; Beta thalassemia = lacking beta haemoglobin.
  • The body is unable to synthesise enough haemoglobin — the disease affects blood.

09Colour Blindness & Inheritance Probabilities

  • Colour Blindness (Daltonism), a.k.a. Colour Vision Deficiency, is a decreased ability to see colour/differences in colour.
  • Most common cause: an inherited problem in development of colour-sensing cone cells — a recessive X-linked inheritance disease.
  • Males are more likely affected than females, since the responsible genes sit on the X-chromosome; females (two X's) are usually compensated by the second copy and act as carriers.
  • The Ishihara Test (designed by Shinobu Ishihara, University of Tokyo, first published 1917) is the classic red-green colour-deficiency test, using pseudoisochromatic plates.

10Down Syndrome (45+2 = 47 Chromosomes)

  • Usually caused by an error in cell division called non-disjunction — prior to/at conception, the 21st chromosome pair in sperm or egg fails to separate.
  • Results in an embryo with three copies of chromosome 21 instead of the usual two — replicated in every body cell as development proceeds.
  • This type is called Trisomy 21.
  • Signs at birth: flat facial features, small head/ears, short neck, bulging tongue, upward-slanting eyes, poor muscle tone, mild-to-moderate mental disability.
  • Also known as Mongoloid idiocy / Mongolian idiocy.
  • Caused by changes in chromosome number (not structure of chromosome/DNA/RNA).

01Deficiency vs Non-Contagious Diseases

  • Deficiency diseases are caused by the lack of nutrients in the diet — usually a particular vitamin or mineral.
  • Non-contagious diseases include diabetes, high blood pressure, cancer, etc. — they do not spread person-to-person.

02Cancer, Metastasis & Oncogenes

  • Cancer = a group of diseases involving abnormal cell growth with potential to invade/spread — contrasted with benign tumours, which do not spread.
  • Signs: a new lump, abnormal bleeding, prolonged cough, unexplained weight loss, changed bowel movements.
  • Metastasis = spread of cancer cells from their primary location to another body region, via bloodstream or lymphatic vessels. Steps: local invasion → intravasation → circulation → arrest/extravasation → proliferation → angiogenesis.
  • Oncogene = a gene with potential to cause cancer in tumour cells, often mutated/overexpressed; normal cells undergo apoptosis (programmed cell death) when malfunctioning — cancer cells evade this.
  • The first identified oncogene was SRC (sarcoma), in 1970, from the Rous sarcoma virus — a retrovirus, the first oncovirus ever described, causing sarcoma in chickens.
  • Carcinogens = physical, chemical, or biological agents that transform normal cells into cancerous (neoplastic) cells via DNA mutation.
  • Ionized radiation can both cause AND cure cancer, depending on intensity/use (unlike tobacco, alcohol, UV rays, which are purely harmful in this context).
  • Treatments: Intensity Modulated Radiotherapy (IMRT) — precise radiation dose confirmation, used for nearly all solid tumours; Immunotherapy and Molecularly targeted therapy are newer approaches; platinum compounds (cisplatin, carboplatin, oxaliplatin) are widely used chemotherapy drugs for carcinoma.
  • Interferon Alfa-2A treats leukaemia, melanoma, and viral infections (chronic Hep-B/C) — identical to a naturally produced body protein.
  • Human organ LEAST susceptible to harmful radiation is the brain (eyes are most susceptible).

03Leukaemia & Radioisotope Therapy

  • Leukaemia = malignant progressive disease where bone marrow/blood-forming organs produce increased immature or abnormal white blood cells (leucocytes).
  • Symptoms: fever/chills, fatigue, frequent/severe infections, weight loss, swollen lymph nodes, enlarged liver/spleen, easy bleeding/bruising, night sweats, bone pain.

05Beriberi, Goitre & Scurvy — Detail

Beriberi

  • Caused by Vitamin B1 (Thiamine) deficiency; two types — Wet beriberi (heart/circulatory system, can cause heart failure) and Dry beriberi (nerve damage, muscle paralysis).
  • Due to lack of thiamine in birds, polyneuritis is caused.
  • Sources of Vitamin B1: husk of cereals, meat, liver, dried milk, nuts, oranges, legumes, yeast.

Goitre

  • Abnormal enlargement of the thyroid gland, located at the base of the neck below the Adam's apple.
  • Caused mainly by lack of iodine, necessary for thyroxine hormone synthesis.
  • Main iodine sources: seafoods and iodised salt.

Scurvy

  • Caused by Vitamin C deficiency; a diet of only milk, egg, and bread would cause scurvy.
  • Symptoms: spongy/bleeding gums, loose/falling teeth, blood vessel fragility, skin haemorrhages.
  • Indian gooseberry (Amla) is the best source of Vitamin C, useful in treating scurvy.

06Diabetes Mellitus & Diabetes Insipidus

  • Diabetes Mellitus (DM) = group of metabolic disorders with high blood sugar over prolonged periods; symptoms — frequent urination, increased thirst, increased hunger.
  • Type 1 diabetes ('insulin-dependent'/'juvenile-onset') — autoimmune; body attacks its own pancreas, damaging beta cells, causing insufficient insulin.
  • Type 2 diabetes ('non-insulin-dependent'/'adult-onset') — begins with insulin resistance; most common cause: excess body weight + insufficient exercise.
  • World Diabetes Day: 14 November, coordinated by the International Diabetes Federation; 2025 theme: 'Diabetes across life stages' (multi-year 2024–2026 theme: 'Diabetes and well-being').
  • Diabetes Insipidus (DI) — a.k.a. 'water diabetes' — caused by insufficient antidiuretic hormone (ADH/vasopressin), secreted by the pituitary gland.
  • Drinking alcohol/whiskey increases urination frequency because alcohol suppresses vasopressin secretion (not because it speeds up secretion — a reversed MCQ trap).
  • Methi (fenugreek) seeds and Curry leaf tree (Murraya koenigii) leaves are both used to help control blood sugar.
  • Frederick Banting and Charles Best discovered/isolated insulin (early 1920s) under John Macleod at the University of Toronto; James Collip helped purify it. Banting & Macleod won the Nobel Prize in 1923.
  • Diabetes cannot be controlled by vaccination — unlike Polio, Whooping Cough, Smallpox.
  • Diabetes is most commonly associated with the pancreas (produces insulin via beta cells of islets of Langerhans).
  • Blood glucose level is commonly expressed as milligram per decilitre (mg/dL).
  • Glycosylated haemoglobin (HbA1c) measurement is used for diabetes mellitus management, reflecting average blood glucose over the prior 8–12 weeks.

07Cerebral Palsy, Alzheimer's & EEG

Cerebral Palsy

  • A group of permanent movement disorders appearing in early childhood; caused by brain injury/malformation during development (before, during, or after birth) — not by muscle/nerve problems directly.
  • Symptoms: poor coordination, stiff/weak muscles, tremors; may affect sensation, vision, hearing, swallowing, speech.
  • Generally found in small children.

Alzheimer's Disease

  • A chronic neurodegenerative disease; most common cause of dementia.
  • Leading cause: accumulation of β-amyloid precursor protein plus hyperphosphorylated/cleaved tau protein; may also be genetic.
  • Affects the brain; early sign — forgetting recent events/conversations, progressing to severe memory impairment.
  • First identified by Dr. Alois Alzheimer, a German psychiatrist/neuropathologist.
  • Arvid Carlsson won the Nobel Prize in 2000 (with Eric Kandel and Paul Greengard) for work on Parkinson's disease treatment via dopamine research.

Electroencephalogram (EEG)

  • A non-invasive test recording electrical brain activity via scalp electrodes; used to diagnose seizures, epilepsy, head injuries, sleep problems, and can confirm brain death.
  • Invented by Hans Berger, a German psychiatrist.

08Hysteria, Phobias & Eating Disorders

  • Hysteria = an uncontrollable outburst of emotion/fear, marked by irrationality, laughter, weeping; a psychoneurotic disorder with sensory/motor disturbances and auto-suggestion effects. Generally occurs in young women (unmarried).
  • Anthophobia = abnormal, persistent fear of flowers (Greek: anthos=flower, phobos=fear).
  • Bulimia Nervosa = eating disorder of binge eating followed by purging (vomiting/laxatives) — a person who doesn't know when to stop eating suffers from bulimia, distinct from Anorexia Nervosa (food restriction, fear of weight gain).
  • Correct matching: Anorexia = Eating disorder, Insomnia = Sleep disorder, Dyspnoea = Shortness of breath, Anosmia = Partial/full loss of smell.

10Anosmia, Itching & Sleep Apnea

  • Anosmia = complete loss of sense of smell; commonly from nasal congestion (cold, allergy, sinus infection) or brain injury; some are born with it (congenital).
  • Scratching relieves itching because it stimulates nerves that direct the brain to increase production of antihistaminic chemicals.
  • Sleep Apnea = a serious sleep disorder where breathing is briefly and repeatedly interrupted during sleep; snoring is a noticeable sign.
  • Lukoskin (dual therapy — oral liquid + ointment) treats Vitiligo/Leucoderma; developed by DIBER, a DRDO wing, at Haldwani, Uttarakhand. Other DRDO polyherbal products: Amtooth (toothache), Eczit (eczema) — Nocdurna is NOT a DRDO product (it's for nocturnal polyuria).

11Heart Attack & Cardiac Procedures

  • Common cause: plaque buildup (atherosclerosis) in arteries preventing blood reaching heart muscle; also blood clots or torn blood vessels.
  • Sequence: plaque forms from fibrous tissue/cholesterol → narrows vessel orifice → blood clots reach coronary arteries → inadequate blood/oxygen supply.
  • Risk factors: smoking, high cholesterol, obesity, lack of exercise, diet/alcohol, stress.
  • 'Golden Hour' = the critical first hour after a heart attack onset — most deaths occur in this window if untreated.
  • Beta-blockers treat heart attack, hypertension, angina, abnormal heart rhythms.
  • Potassium is essential to regulate heartbeat; its deficiency causes low blood pressure.
  • Coronary artery bypass surgery uses a healthy vessel (leg vein, forearm/chest artery — any of these) to bypass blocked coronary arteries; the saphenous vein is commonly used.
  • Cath Lab (Cardiac Catheterization Laboratory) — special room for minimally invasive cardiovascular diagnosis/treatment, found in the Cardiology department.
  • Aspirin — a neurologically active drug (analgesic, antipyretic, anti-inflammatory) with anti-blood-clotting action — used in heart attack prevention; it is a non-narcotic (not narcotic) analgesic.
  • CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation) — emergency procedure for cardiac arrest; immediate CPR can double/triple survival chances.
  • Infrared radiation is used to relieve muscle/body aches by increasing blood flow.

12Liver Cirrhosis, Jaundice & Oral Submucous Fibrosis

  • Liver Cirrhosis — chronic liver disease with cell degeneration, inflammation, fibrous scarring; typically from alcoholism, hepatitis, or non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.
  • Symptoms: fatigue, weakness, itching, leg swelling, yellow skin, abdominal fluid buildup, spider-like blood vessels.
  • Jaundice — buildup of bilirubin in blood causing yellowish skin, mucus membranes, eye whites; bilirubin forms from RBC breakdown, excreted via bile.
  • Jaundice itself is not dangerous but may signal gallstones, liver cancer, etc.; jaundice is NOT contagious (unlike Measles, Cholera, TB).
  • Oral Submucous Fibrosis (OSF/OSMF) — chronic oral cavity disease with inflammation and progressive fibrosis of submucosal tissue, causing rigidity and inability to open the mouth; caused by habitual areca nut/tobacco/gutkha chewing.

13Itai-Itai, Minamata & Blue Baby Syndrome

  • Itai-Itai Disease — mass cadmium poisoning, Toyama Prefecture, Japan, starting ~1912; name reflects severe spine/joint pain; causes bone softening, kidney failure; cadmium released into rivers by mining companies (successfully sued).
  • Minamata Disease — neurological syndrome from severe mercury poisoning; symptoms: ataxia, numbness, muscle weakness, narrowed vision field, hearing/speech damage; first occurred in Minamata city, Japan from eating mercury-contaminated fish (Chisso Corporation wastewater, 1932–1968).
  • The Four Big Pollution Diseases of Japan: Itai-Itai, Minamata Disease, Niigata Minamata Disease, Yokkaichi Asthma.
  • Blue Baby Syndrome (Methemoglobinemia) — caused by nitrates in drinking water; nitrates convert haemoglobin to methemoglobin (cannot bind oxygen); infant condition can be fatal; also linked to congenital heart defects like Tetralogy of Fallot.
  • Knock-Knee Syndrome (Genu Valgum) — caused by fluoride toxicity, leading to osteoporosis and skeletal deformity.
  • Fluoride excess causes dental fluorosis (not 'indigestion') — a commonly mismatched pollutant-disease pair.

14Silicosis, Dropsy & Lathyrism

  • Silicosis — lung fibrosis from inhaling silica dust; risk occupations: coal/hard-rock mining, construction, tunnel work, masonry, sandblasting, glass/ceramic work.
  • Epidemic Dropsy — oedema from poisoning by Argemone mexicana (Mexican prickly poppy) seed oil adulterating mustard oil; toxic alkaloids sanguinarine and dihydrosanguinarine cause capillary dilation/permeability; results in proteinuria (albumin loss) and oedema resembling nephrotic syndrome. Argemone seeds resemble mustard seeds, enabling adulteration; major Indian outbreak in 1998.
  • Lathyrism — a neurological disorder from excessive consumption of Khesari dal (Lathyrus sativus); causes muscle weakness, spasms, and paralysis (especially lower limbs).
  • Pneumoconiosis — occupational restrictive lung disease from dust inhalation, mainly affecting coal mining industry workers.
