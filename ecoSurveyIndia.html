Chapter 01

State of the Economy: Pushing the Growth Frontier

Global environment fragile — growth holding up better than expected but risks elevated amid geopolitical tension, trade fragmentation & financial vulnerability; shocks may surface with a lag.

Headline growth

CORE STAT
7.4%
FAE FY26 Real GDP Growth
7.3%
FAE FY26 GVA Growth

India reaffirmed its status as the fastest-growing major economy for the 4th consecutive year.

Demand side

  • Private Final Consumption Expenditure (PFCE) grew 7.0% in FY26, reaching 61.5% of GDP — the highest since 2012 (FY23 also recorded 61.5%).
    • Supported by low inflation, stable employment, rising real purchasing power.
    • Strong agri performance boosted rural consumption; tax rationalisation aided urban consumption.
  • Gross Fixed Capital Formation (GFCF) grew 7.8% in FY26, share steady at 30% of GDP — buoyed by sustained public capex and revival in private investment.

Capital expenditure & State finances

  • Central govt effective capex: 2.7% of GDP (pre-pandemic avg) → ~3.9% post-pandemic → 4% of GDP in FY25.
  • SASCI (Special Assistance to States for Capital Investment) — incentivised States to maintain capex at ~2.4% of GDP in FY25.
  • Combined fiscal deficit of States: stable ~2.8% of GDP post-pandemic, edged up to 3.2% in FY25 — emerging pressure on State finances.
  • India cut general government debt-to-GDP ratio by ~7.1 percentage points since 2020, while maintaining high public investment.

External account resilience

  • Current account deficit moderate; Q2 FY26 CAD ~1.3% of GDP — favourable vs other major economies.
  • India = world's largest remittance recipient: USD 135.4 billion in FY25; rising share from advanced economies (skilled/professional workers).
  • Forex reserves: USD 701.4 billion as of 16 Jan 2026 — import cover ~11 months, covers >94% of external debt.

  • India ranked 4th globally in Greenfield investment announcements 2024 (1,000+ projects) — largest destination for Greenfield digital investments, 2020–24.

Agriculture and Food Management: Raising Productivity, Securing Income

Livestock, fisheries and foodgrain all posted strong multi-year gains, with horticulture now outproducing foodgrain by volume.

Production growth

  • Livestock sector GVA rose ~195% between FY15 and FY24.
  • Fish production increased >140% during 2014–2024 (vs the 2004–14 growth rate).
  • Foodgrain production (AY 2024–25): 3,577.3 LMT, up 254.3 LMT YoY — driven by rice, wheat, maize & coarse cereals (Shree Anna).
  • Horticulture (~33% of agri GVA): 2024–25 production 362.08 million tonnes — surpassing foodgrain production of 357.73 MT.

Chapter 14

Evolution of the AI Ecosystem in India: The Way Forward

India's AI adoption is being driven by real-world necessity, not speculative frontier use cases.
  • Smaller, task-specific models deployed across sectors allow innovation to diffuse more evenly, lower entry barriers for firms, and better fit India's diverse economic landscape.
  • India's AI demand is emerging from real-world problems rather than speculative frontier uses.
  • Growing appetite across healthcare, agriculture, urban management, education, disaster preparedness and public administration for AI systems that work on local hardware in low-resource settings.
