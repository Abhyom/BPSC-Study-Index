2

Executive Power, Head of State & Government

Who Holds Executive Power

  • Art 53(1): the executive power of the Union is vested in the President, exercised by him directly or through officers subordinate to him. It is not vested in the Prime Minister; the President is the constitutional head of the Union executive.
  • Art 77(1): all executive action of the Government of India is expressed to be taken in the name of the President (not the Prime Minister).
  • Art 77(3): the President makes rules for the more convenient transaction of the business of the Government of India and for the allocation of that business among Ministers.
  • Art 300: the legal name of the Government of India is 'The Union of India' (not Union Government, India or Bharat).
  • The Prime Minister is the Head of the Government (head of the Central Government, not of State governments); the President is the Head of the State.
  • The Indian polity is parliamentary: the Constitution provides a parliamentary form of government, and the function of Parliament includes providing a Cabinet. The executive is subordinate to the Legislature — Parliament is the supreme legislative body and the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the House of the People.

Council of Ministers — Art 74

  • Art 74(1): there shall be a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at the head to aid and advise the President, who shall, in exercise of his functions, act in accordance with such advice. Hence the Council of Ministers is a constitutional body.
  • 44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978 added the proviso: the President may require the Council of Ministers to reconsider its advice, but must act on the advice tendered after such reconsideration.
  • Art 74(2): the advice tendered by Ministers to the President shall not be inquired into in any court.
  • The Prime Minister is the real head (not nominal head) of the Council of Ministers — real power vests in him; in the parliamentary form the real executive power is exercised by the PM (the provision is traced to Art 75).
  • The Central Council of Ministers includes the Prime Minister, Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State and Ministers of State (Independent Charge).
MCQ Trap / Source ConflictThere is no Art 74(3) in the Constitution. The Council is collectively responsible under Art 75(3), not Art 74(3).
MCQ TrapThe statement 'the executive power is vested in the Prime Minister' is wrong in every form it appears — it is the President (Art 53(1)). The Council advises under Art 74(1); it does not hold the power itself.

Appointment, Qualification & Tenure

Appointment of PM and Ministers — Art 75

  • Art 75(1): the Prime Minister is appointed by the President — not elected by Lok Sabha or Parliament and not nominated by the majority party; other Ministers are appointed by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister.
  • Art 75 deals with appointment of the Council of Ministers (Art 75(1)) and their tenure; Art 75(2) is the pleasure provision.
  • The PM has full discretion in choosing the persons who serve as ministers in his Cabinet — not limited by the President's discretion, nor by any requirement of prior counselling, nor restricted to sitting MPs.
  • Art 75(2): Ministers hold office during the pleasure of the President (not 'of the Prime Minister').
  • The President administers the oath of office and secrecy to the Prime Minister and the other ministers.
  • The Prime Minister submits his resignation to the President.

Membership of Parliament & the Six-Month Rule — Art 75(5)

  • A person who is not a member of either House can be appointed minister (including Prime Minister and Cabinet Minister), but must become a member of either House — by election or nomination — within six months (any minister not a member for six consecutive months), otherwise he ceases to be a minister. The rule is Art 75(5) (not 75(2), 75(3) or 75(4)).
  • The requirement is not Lok Sabha specifically; membership of Rajya Sabha also satisfies it. Cabinet membership is therefore not restricted to the Lower House.
  • The PM is generally a member of Lok Sabha, but this is neither compulsory nor restricted; he can be a member of Rajya Sabha. Indira Gandhi was the first PM from Rajya Sabha at the time of appointment, but later became a Lok Sabha member.
  • Minimum age to become PM is the same as that for a Lok Sabha member — 25 years.
  • A minister who is a member of one House has the right to speak and take part in proceedings of the other House, but no right to vote in the House of which he is not a member. Thus, a PM belonging to the Upper House cannot vote in his own favour in a no-confidence motion.
  • A minister who resigns can make a statement in the House about his resignation only when allowed by the Speaker; he is free to make the statement or not and cannot be compelled.
MCQ Trap / Source ConflictBoth Houses' members are eligible to be Union ministers, yet that does not make the Council responsible to both Houses — the Council answers only to Lok Sabha. A PM from the Upper House does not have to shift to Lok Sabha within six months; membership of either House suffices.

Composition, Size & the Cabinet

Ranks of Ministers

  • The Constitution has no provision for classifying ministers into ranks; the classification follows British parliamentary tradition.
  • In practice the Council consists of Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State (Independent Charge), Ministers of State and Deputy Ministers (besides the PM). The claim that the Constitution itself classifies ministers into four ranks is wrong.
  • The Union Council of Ministers exercises executive authority; it seldom meets as a body. The Union Cabinet is a senior executive body inside the Council that shapes policy — the supreme decision-making body in India. Cabinet Ministers hold key portfolios.
  • Cabinet = the Prime Minister and the Cabinet-rank ministers only. Ministers of State (Independent Charge), Ministers of State and secretaries are not part of the Cabinet.
  • The PM is the Chairman of Union Cabinet meetings.
  • The Cabinet is not in the original Constitution. The word 'Cabinet' appears only in Art 352(3) (inserted by the 44th Amendment): the President shall not issue a Proclamation of Emergency unless the Union Cabinet communicates to him in writing that it may be issued. The word occurs twice in this Article. (Art 74, 356 and 76 are not the answer.)

Size Limit — 91st Amendment

  • 91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003 inserted Art 75(1A): total number of Ministers, including the Prime Minister, shall not exceed 15% of the total strength of Lok Sabha.
  • It amended Art 75 and Art 164 together: Art 164(1A) limits the Council of Ministers in a State, including the Chief Minister, to 15% of the total members of the Legislative Assembly of that State.
  • Distractor amendments (90th, 92nd, 93rd, 95th) are wrong.

Collective Responsibility & Confidence Motions

Collective Responsibility — Art 75(3)

  • The Council of Ministers (including the PM) is collectively responsible to the House of the People (Lok Sabha) — only Lok Sabha, not Parliament, Rajya Sabha, the President, the Prime Minister or the Speaker. It is a constitutional obligation under Art 75(3).
  • Because the question 'to whom is the Council responsible' appeared with Lok Sabha missing from the options, the key accepted the nearest answer — Parliament.
  • Ministerial responsibility: for every act of the bureaucrats the concerned Minister is answerable to Parliament. Ministers make policy and civil servants adhere to it; the parliamentary system works on Cabinet collective responsibility — but collective responsibility is not itself the explanation of why ministers make policy.
  • Convention: the PM resigns when he loses the majority in the Lower House — not explicitly written in the Constitution but followed because he heads the Council collectively responsible to Lok Sabha. The Cabinet enjoys the confidence of the majority in the popular chamber.

Confidence & No-Confidence Motions

  • A Motion of No-Confidence can be introduced only in Lok Sabha and, if passed by majority, the Council of Ministers has to resign.
  • It is not mentioned in the Constitution (nor is a confidence motion); the procedure is in the Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business of Lok Sabha — Rule 198. It is the mechanism to test collective responsibility under Art 75(3).
  • The member seeking leave gives written notice (Rule 198(1)(b)) to the Secretary-General of Lok Sabha; at least 50 members must support, so any MP who gathers 50 colleagues can introduce the motion at any time.
  • The Council of Ministers can move only a Confidence Motion — to prove its majority support in Lok Sabha; it cannot move a no-confidence, censure or adjournment motion.
  • First no-confidence motion: 1963, Third Lok Sabha, moved by Acharya J.B. Kripalani against the government of PM Jawaharlal Nehru.
  • Till 15 August 2023: 28 no-confidence motions (the 28th on the Manipur situation) and 11 confidence motions discussed. 14 of 28 (50%) were discussed between 1965 and 1975, of which 12 were against governments headed by Indira Gandhi.

After the Council Resigns

  • President's Rule is not imposed on resignation of the Council of Ministers.
  • The President asks the outgoing Council to continue till an alternative arrangement is made; the outgoing Council holds charge until the new Government is formed.
  • Alternative arrangement = earliest possible General Election to form a new government.

Prime Minister's Duties — Art 78 & Allocation of Business

Duties of the PM — Art 78

  • Art 78 lays down the duties of the Prime Minister (not the Speaker, Rajya Sabha Chairman or CAG).
  • Art 78(a): to communicate to the President all decisions of the Council of Ministers relating to the administration of the affairs of the Union and proposals for legislation.
  • To furnish such information relating to administration of the Union's affairs and proposals for legislation as the President may call for.
  • If the President requires, to submit for consideration of the Council any matter on which a decision has been taken by a minister but not considered by the Council.

Allocation of Business & Creation of Ministries

  • Art 77(3): rules for allocation of business among ministers are made by the President; under these rules the President allocates work/portfolios among ministers on the advice of the PM. Each Ministry is assigned to a Minister by the President on the PM's advice.
  • Ministries/Departments are created by the President on the advice of the PM under the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961 — rules made under Art 77. They are not created by the PM on the advice/consultation of the Cabinet Secretary.
  • Number of Ministries: 18 on 15 August 1947; 53 at present (the figure 36 is wrong).

Reorganisation of the Machinery

  • N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar's report 'Reorganization of the Machinery of Central Government' (1950) is the basis for the reorganisation of the Union Cabinet; it recommended grouping of ministries, improving personnel capabilities and improving the working of the O&M Division.
  • It did not recommend a minister and secretary designated solely for administrative reform.

Deputy Prime Minister

Deputy PM

  • The office and duties of the Deputy Prime Minister are not mentioned in the Constitution — it is an extra-constitutional growth created by political necessity, not by the original Constitution, the 44th Amendment or the 85th Amendment.
  • Conventionally a Cabinet Minister can be declared Deputy PM; describing a person so is a political decision and confers no power of the Prime Minister on him.
  • First Deputy PM: Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel — 15 August 1947 to 15 December 1950; also Home Minister, he played the key role in integrating over 500 princely states into the Indian Union.

Cabinet Secretariat, Committees & Administrative Bodies

Cabinet Secretariat

  • Functions directly under the Prime Minister (not under the Vice-President or the Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs); the PM has direct charge, and it is under the supervision of the PM's Office.
  • Administrative head: the Cabinet Secretary, who is the ex-officio Chairman of the Civil Services Board (not the PM) and the highest civil services officer of the Central Government (above the Attorney General, Home Secretary, Finance Secretary).
  • Has a place in the First Schedule of the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961. Subjects: (i) secretariat assistance to the Cabinet and Cabinet Committees; (ii) administration of the Rules of Business; (iii) promotion of inter-ministerial coordination — removing difficulties and differences, overcoming delays, coordinating administrative actions and policies.
  • Secretariat assistance includes: convening Cabinet meetings on the PM's orders; preparing and circulating the agenda; circulating papers on agenda cases; recording discussions; circulating the record after the PM's approval; watching the implementation of Cabinet decisions.
  • Allocation of financial resources to Ministries is not a function of the Secretariat.
  • Split system in Indian administration = policy / implementation split; the Ex-Secretariat system rests on it for efficient policy implementation.

Cabinet Committees

  • Not mentioned in the Constitution — extra-constitutional; the Rules of Business provide for their establishment.
  • Set up by the Prime Minister according to the exigencies of time and requirements of the situation.
  • Mostly chaired by the PM; sometimes by other Cabinet Ministers (e.g. Finance or Home Minister). If the PM is a member of a committee, he is necessarily its Chairman.
  • The Cabinet Committee on Parliamentary Affairs is chaired by the Minister of Defence, not the PM.

Department of Personnel & Administrative Reforms — Timeline

  • 1 Aug 1970: Department of Personnel created under the Cabinet Secretariat.
  • 7 Feb 1973: work of the Department of Administrative Reforms transferred to it; redesignated Department of Personnel and Administrative Reforms.
  • April 1977: shifted from the Cabinet Secretariat to the Ministry of Home Affairs.
  • March 1985: elevated to a full Ministry of Personnel and Training, Administrative Reforms, Public Grievances and Pensions.
  • 10 Dec 1985: redesignated Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions, with three departments — DoPT, Dept of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances, Dept of Pension and Pensioners Welfare; under the overall charge of the PM, assisted by a Minister of State.
MCQ Trap / Source ConflictThe question's 1970 statement ('Dept of Personnel set up on ARC 1966 recommendation and placed under PM's charge') was marked wrong; the source dates give the 1970 Department under the Cabinet Secretariat and the PM's charge only after 1985.

Ministries & Bodies Headed by or Linked to the PM

  • Ministry of Tribal Affairs: set up in 1999 on the bifurcation of the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment, for focused integrated socio-economic development of Scheduled Tribes; nodal ministry for policy planning and coordination of ST programmes; earlier handled by different ministries at different times.
  • Ministry of Minority Affairs: carved out of the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment on 29 January 2006; focuses on notified minorities — Muslim, Christian, Buddhist, Sikh, Parsi, Jain.
  • Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR): President is the Prime Minister.
  • National Defence Committee: headed by the Prime Minister.
  • Office of Parliamentary Secretary first created in independent India in 1951.
  • White Paper: an official document of the Government of India or a Ministry explaining its policies on a subject and collecting feedback before a bill is prepared (Green, Yellow and Blue are not India's documents in this sense).

Early Cabinets & Union Ministers

Interim Government (1946) & First Cabinet (1947)

  • Jogendra Nath Mandal was Law Minister in the interim government of 1946; Dr. B.R. Ambedkar was the first Law Minister of post-independence India.
  • R.K. Shanmukham Chetty was the first Finance Minister of independent India (1947–48) in Nehru's Cabinet (John Mathai, Nanda, Krishnamachari and Sarojini Naidu are not the answer).
  • Interim Cabinet portfolios: Dr. Rajendra Prasad — Food and Agriculture; Jagjivan Ram — Labour; Sardar Baldev Singh — Defence; C.H. Bhabha — Works, Mines and Power.
  • First Cabinet of independent India: Prasad (Food & Agriculture), Baldev Singh (Defence) and Jagjivan Ram (Labour) unchanged; C.H. Bhabha moved to Commerce — the only changed portfolio.
  • Kailash Nath Katju: joined Nehru's Cabinet in 1951 as Law Minister; same year became the third Home Minister; 1955 Defence Minister; later Chief Minister of Madhya Pradesh (1957–62).
