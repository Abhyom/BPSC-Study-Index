01 Union Public Service Commission (UPSC)

Constitutional Basis & Composition

  • Governed by Articles 315–323 in Part XIV of the Constitution.
  • Article 315 provides that there shall be a Public Service Commission for the Union and one for each State — and, if two or more States agree, one Joint Commission for that group of States.
  • The UPSC is a Constitutional body — one of the four bodies (with the Election Commission, Finance Commission, and CAG) the source repeatedly contrasts against extra-constitutional/statutory bodies such as the Planning Commission or NITI Aayog.
  • Consists of a Chairman and other members, all appointed by the President.
  • Term: 6 years or until age 65, whichever is earlier.
  • No qualification is prescribed by the Constitution itself, except that 50% of members must have held field/government experience for at least 10 years.
  • UPSC has nothing to do with any State Public Service Commission — it neither supervises SPSCs nor issues them annual guidelines.

Removal & Post-Tenure Restrictions

  • Removal is by the President, under Article 319, on grounds of insolvency, holding an office of profit, infirmity of mind/body, or misbehaviour.
  • Where removal is sought on grounds of misbehaviour, the matter is referred by the President to the Supreme Court for inquiry (under Article 145 procedure), and the Supreme Court's advice on removal is binding on the President.
  • A member (other than the Chairman) is not eligible for further government employment, except that a member can be appointed Chairman of the UPSC or of an SPSC, and a UPSC Chairman can be appointed to any other government office — but only once.

Role & Functions

  • Functions include: on the method of recruitment to civil services; on principles for appointments to civil services; on principles for promotions and transfers from one service to another; and on disciplinary matters affecting a person serving the Union/State in a civil capacity, including memorials or petitions relating to such matters (Article 320(3)(c)). Transfers of civil servants are not a UPSC function.
  • Article 320 lays out these functions of the Public Service Commissions (Union and State).
  • Article 321 allows Parliament (for the Union) or a State Legislature (for that State) to extend, by law, the functions of the UPSC or an SPSC to the services of the Union/State and to local authorities or other statutory/public bodies.
  • The UPSC derives its functions and powers cumulatively from: the Constitution, Parliamentary laws, executive rules and orders, and established conventions — not from the Constitution alone.
  • Under Article 323, the UPSC presents an annual report on its own performance to the President (SPSCs report similarly to their Governor).
  • Under Article 322, the expenses of the UPSC (and of a State/Joint Commission) are charged on the Consolidated Fund of India (or of the State, for SPSCs).
  • Under Article 315(4), the UPSC may, at a State Governor's request and with the President's approval, agree to serve all or any of that State's needs (i.e., function as that State's SPSC).
  • The UPSC was first established under the Government of India Act, 1919, which set up the first Public Service Commission on 1 October 1926 (Chairman + 4 members); it was later named the Federal Public Service Commission under the Government of India Act, 1935.
  • First woman Chairman of the UPSC: Roze Millian Bethew (served 1992–1996).

⟳ Current (2026)

  • Chairperson of the UPSC: Ajay Kumar, IAS, in office since 15 May 2025.

02 State Public Service Commission (SPSC) & Joint SPSC

  • Also governed by Articles 315–323 in Part XIV of the Constitution — the same set of Articles that governs the UPSC.
  • Consists of a Chairman and other members, appointed by the Governor of the State.
  • Term: 6 years or until age 62, whichever is earlier (note: this age ceiling is lower than the UPSC's 65).
  • Same qualification norm as UPSC: no prescribed qualification, except that 50% of members must have field experience of 10 years.
  • Removal follows the same grounds as for UPSC members, but is carried out only by the President (not the Governor).
  • An SPSC member/Chairman enjoys the same post-tenure employment restriction as UPSC — barred from further government employment except a one-time appointment as Chairman of the UPSC or of an SPSC; no second term is otherwise available.
  • SPSC's role mirrors the UPSC's, but confined to the State.
  • A Joint Public Service Commission for two or more States can be established by Parliament, by law, if the concerned States pass a resolution to that effect in their legislatures.
  • The Chairman of a Joint Public Service Commission is appointed by the President.

03 Election Commission of India (ECI)

Constitutional Basis & Composition

  • Articles 324 to 329 in Part XV of the Constitution deal with elections.
  • Article 324 establishes the Election Commission as a permanent and independent, all-India constitutional body, vested with superintendence, direction and control of preparing electoral rolls for, and conducting, elections to Parliament, every State Legislature, and the offices of President and Vice-President.
  • Composition: a Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) and other Election Commissioners, the number decided by the President from time to time [Art. 324(2)]; all are appointed by the President.
  • Until 15 October 1989 there was only a CEC; on 16 October 1989 two additional Election Commissioners were appointed for the first time, but those posts lapsed by 1 January 1990. On 1 October 1993, two additional Election Commissioners were appointed again, and the Commission has been a three-member body ever since.
  • The CEC and the two Election Commissioners have equal powers and receive equal salary/allowances/perquisites, equivalent to a Judge of the Supreme Court.
  • Tenure: 6 years or until age 65, whichever is earlier, from the date of assuming office.
  • Conditions of service of Election Commissioners are determined by the President.
  • An Election Commissioner may resign at any time by writing to the President (addressed to him, not to the CEC).

Removal

  • The CEC can be removed only in the same manner and on the same grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court [Article 324(5)].
  • Any other Election Commissioner or a Regional Commissioner can be removed by the President only on the recommendation of the CEC.

Functions & Jurisdiction

  • Superintends, directs and controls preparation of electoral rolls and conduct of elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, and the offices of President and Vice-President.
  • Notifications for elections to the Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha are issued by the President; for State Legislative Assemblies/Councils, by the Governor — the notification itself follows the ECI's recommendation on timing.
  • ECI does not conduct elections to local bodies (Panchayats/Municipalities) — these are conducted by the respective State Election Commissions under Articles 243K and 243ZA.
  • ECI also does not conduct elections for the Speaker/Deputy Speaker of the Lok Sabha or the Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha — these are internal elections by House members.
  • Under Article 103 (for MPs) and Article 192 (for MLAs), the ECI has advisory jurisdiction on post-election disqualification questions for sitting members — the President (or Governor) decides, but must first obtain and act according to the ECI's opinion.
  • The ECI resolves disputes relating to splits/mergers of recognised political parties.
  • The ECI decides the schedule for both general elections and by-elections (not the Home Ministry).
  • The Model Code of Conduct — guidelines for parties/candidates on speeches, polling booths, processions, manifestos, general conduct — was formulated by the ECI (not enacted by Parliament); it takes effect immediately on the ECI's announcement of the election schedule.

Delimitation Commission

  • Established by the Government of India (under an Act of Parliament) to redraw Assembly/Lok Sabha constituency boundaries based on the latest census, and to determine SC/ST seat reservations.
  • Constituted in 1952, 1963, 1973 and 2002 (under the respective Delimitation Acts), and again in March 2020 for delimitation of the UT of Jammu & Kashmir (chaired by Justice Ranjana Prakash Desai); the four north-eastern states originally included in the 2020 exercise were removed from its purview in March 2021.
  • Delimitation Commission orders cannot be challenged in a court of law, and when laid before the Lok Sabha/State Assembly, no modifications can be made to them.

Suffrage, Voting & Reforms

  • Article 326 provides for elections to the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies to be based on adult suffrage.
  • The right to vote and to be elected in India is a legal (statutory) right, not a fundamental or constitutional right — confirmed by the Supreme Court (e.g., Kuldip Nayar v. Union of India).
  • The 61st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1988 reduced the voting age from 21 to 18 years, amending Article 326; it came into force 28 March 1989, and was first exercised in the November 1989 Lok Sabha general elections.
  • In March 2024, Rule 27A of the Conduct of Elections Rules, 1961 was amended (after ECI consultation) to raise the eligibility age for postal ballot voting from home for senior citizens from 80 to 85 years; this facility, along with persons with 40%+ certified disability, was first used nationally in the 2024 Lok Sabha elections.
  • Election reform chronology (earliest to latest): EVM (experimental use recorded 1982) → Electors Photo Identity Card (EPIC) (scheme started 1993) → VVPAT (first used 4 September 2013, Noksen by-election, Nagaland) → NOTA (added per Supreme Court direction dated 27 September 2013).
  • The version of VVPAT machines currently used for voting in India is M3 — third-generation EVMs, with a capacity for 384 candidates and features like tamper detection and self-diagnostics.
  • National Voters' Day is celebrated on 25 January every year (started 2011), marking the ECI's foundation day.
  • Under Section 8(3) of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, a person convicted of an offence and sentenced to at least 2 years' imprisonment is disqualified from the date of conviction, and remains disqualified for a further 6 years after release; this disqualification framework was made by Parliament.
  • Under Section 33 of the Representation of the People Act 1951, a candidate cannot contest from more than two Parliamentary (or Assembly) constituencies in the same general election.
  • The Representation of the People (Amendment) Act, 1996, based on the Dinesh Goswami Committee's recommendations, provided that no election is now countermanded on the death of a contesting candidate, and increased the security deposit for Lok Sabha candidates.
  • The Dinesh Goswami Committee (Goswami was Law Minister in the V.P. Singh government, 1989) was concerned with electoral reforms, and recommended government funding of parliamentary elections.
  • A candidate who fails to secure more than one-sixth of the valid votes polled in a constituency loses their security deposit.

04 Elections, Representation & Simultaneous Polls

  • India uses both direct election (adult suffrage, for Lok Sabha and State Assemblies) and indirect election by proportional representation via single transferable vote (for President, Vice-President, Rajya Sabha, and Legislative Councils).
  • Proportional representation, as an electoral mechanism, is understood to aid representation of minorities by giving due weight to groups based on ethnicity, gender, interests and ideologies; it becomes less significant in a country with an established two-party system.
  • The High-Level Committee on Simultaneous Elections ("One Nation, One Election") was established on 2 September 2023, chaired by former President Ram Nath Kovind, with members including Amit Shah, N.K. Singh, Dr. Subhash C. Kashyap, Harish Salve, and Sanjay Kothari, and Dr. Niten Chandra as Secretary.
  • Realising "One Nation, One Election" would require amendments to Articles 83, 85, 172, 174 and 356 of the Constitution.

05 Political Parties & Anti-Defection Law

  • The Representation of the People Act, 1951 provides for registration of political parties; registration is carried out by the Election Commission of India, under the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968, which also governs recognition as a National or State party.
  • Political parties were given Constitutional status for the first time by the 52nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1985 (in force from 1 March 1985), which also added the Tenth Schedule (Anti-Defection Law) to the Constitution.
  • The Anti-Defection Law was passed by Parliament on 15 February 1985; it amended Articles 101, 102, 190 and 191 and added the Tenth Schedule, laying down the process for disqualifying members on grounds of defection.
  • Anti-defection covers: en-bloc defection in a party; smaller defections in a single spell; and party members voting/abstaining to support or topple a government. It does not cover larger defections in a party occurring across various separate spells.
  • A merger of political parties is recognised when not less than two-thirds of the members of the legislature party have agreed to the merger.
  • Questions on disqualification under the Tenth Schedule are decided by the Speaker of the Lok Sabha or the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha, not the President; the term "political party" is explicitly used in the Tenth Schedule.
  • A nominated legislator who joins a political party after six months of being appointed to the House can be disqualified under the anti-defection law; the law prescribes no fixed time-frame within which the presiding officer must decide a defection case.
  • A member who, on being elected as Chairman/Vice-Chairman/Speaker/Deputy Speaker of a House, voluntarily gives up their original party membership, is exempted from disqualification on defection grounds — both while holding that office and, if they rejoin the same party after ceasing to hold it.
  • The Jammu and Kashmir Legislative Assembly enacted an Anti-Defection Law as early as 1979, predating the 52nd Amendment.
  • Currently 6 political parties are recognised as National-level parties by the ECI: Indian National Congress, Bharatiya Janata Party, Bahujan Samaj Party, Communist Party of India (Marxist), Aam Aadmi Party, and National People's Party.
  • The Electoral Bond Scheme was notified by the Government of India on 2 January 2018, to bring transparency to political funding; only political parties registered under Section 29A of the RPA, 1951 and securing at least the prescribed vote share in the last general election were eligible; bonds were valid for 15 calendar days from issue, and were credited to the eligible party's account on the same day of deposit. State Bank of India was authorised to issue/encash the bonds through 11 branches.
  • The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885; its first president was W.C. Banerjee. The word "National" in its name reflected both its all-India character and a reaction against British rule.
  • The Bharatiya Janata Party was founded in 1980; its first president was Atal Bihari Vajpayee.
  • The Independent Labour Party was founded by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar in 1936; Ambedkar also founded the Scheduled Castes Federation (1942) and the Depressed Classes Federation (1930).
  • The Communist Party of India was founded in 1925; it split, with CPI(M) forming in 1964.
  • Political scientist Rajni Kothari described India's early post-independence party system as a "one-party dominant system."
  • Inner-party democracy refers to periodical internal elections within a party to elect its office bearers.
  • A pressure group (e.g., a trade union) does not contest elections but seeks to influence government policy/legislation.
  • The "Kamaraj Plan" (1963), prepared by then-INC President K. Kamaraj, aimed at making the Indian National Congress more vibrant.

06 Finance Commission

Constitutional Basis, Composition & Nature

  • Article 280 provides for a Finance Commission, constituted by the President every fifth year, or at an earlier time the President considers necessary.
  • Described as the "balancing wheel" of fiscal federalism; it is a quasi-judicial body, and a Constitutional (statutory-within-Constitution) body.
  • Consists of a Chairman and four other members, all appointed by the President, each for a specified/specialised period.
  • Qualifications of members are determined by Parliament by law (Article 280(2)) — not by the President: Chairman must have experience in public affairs; the four other members must respectively be (i) a High Court judge or qualified to be one, (ii) a person with specialised knowledge of government finance/accounts, (iii) a person with wide experience in financial matters and administration, and (iv) a person with special knowledge of economics. It is not mandatory that at least one member actually be a sitting/former High Court or Supreme Court judge.
  • The Chairman of the Commission is eligible for reappointment (Section 6, Finance Commission (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1951); members generally are eligible for reappointment as well.
  • Recommendations of the Finance Commission are only advisory in nature, not binding on the government.
  • First Finance Commission: constituted by presidential order dated 22 November 1951, under the chairmanship of K.C. Neogy, and set up on 6 April 1952.

Functions

  • Recommends: (1) distribution of the net proceeds of taxes between the Union and the States, and the inter-State allocation of States' respective shares; (2) principles governing grants-in-aid to States out of the Consolidated Fund of India (under Article 275); (3) measures to augment a State's Consolidated Fund to supplement resources of Panchayats and Municipalities, based on the recommendations of that State's own Finance Commission; and (4) any other matter referred by the President in the interest of sound finance.
  • It does not control withdrawal from the Consolidated Fund of India (a Parliament function), and does not supervise whether the Union/State governments levy taxes per budgetary provisions (a Finance Ministry function).
  • Under Article 281, the President causes every Finance Commission recommendation to be laid before each House of Parliament (and the Central Government presents the report along with action taken).
  • Under Article 279, the Finance Commission ascertains and certifies the net proceeds of any tax, and that certification is final.

State Finance Commission

  • Under Article 243-I, the Governor of a State constitutes a State Finance Commission, within one year of the 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act's (1992) commencement and thereafter every fifth year, to review Panchayats' financial position and make recommendations to the Governor.
  • The State Finance Commission is itself a Constitutional body (unlike, e.g., the erstwhile Planning Commission).
  • Under Article 280(3)(bb) and (c), the Union Finance Commission's own recommendations for Panchayats and Municipalities are made on the basis of the recommendations of the concerned State's Finance Commission.

Chairmen — Historical & Current

  • 12th Finance Commission (2005–2010): Dr. C. Rangarajan.
  • 13th Finance Commission (report 2010): Vijay Kelkar.
  • 14th Finance Commission (2015–2020): Y.V. Reddy; recommended raising states' share of net tax proceeds from 32% to 42%.
  • 15th Finance Commission: constituted 27 November 2017 under N.K. Singh; submitted two reports — one for FY 2020-21, and a final report covering 2021-26.

⟳ Current (2026)

  • 16th Finance Commission: constituted 31 December 2023 under Dr. Arvind Panagariya (former first Vice-Chairman of NITI Aayog); members included Annie George Mathew, Dr. Manoj Panda, T. Rabi Sankar, and Dr. Soumya Kanti Ghosh.
  • The 16th Finance Commission submitted its report to the President on 17 November 2025, for the award period 1 April 2026 – 31 March 2031; the report was tabled in Parliament (alongside the Union Budget) on 1 February 2026, recommending states retain a 41% share of central taxes — unchanged from the 15th Finance Commission.

07 GST Council

  • Article 279A, added by the 101st Constitution Amendment Act, 2016, established the GST Council to make recommendations to the Union and State Governments on GST-related issues.
  • Chaired by the Union Finance Minister; other members are the Union Minister of State for Revenue/Finance, and the Ministers in-charge of Finance or Taxation of all the States.
  • Considered a federal body where both the Centre and the States get due representation.
  • Every GST Council decision requires a majority of not less than three-fourths of the weighted votes of members present and voting, with the Centre's vote weighted at one-third and the States' combined vote at two-thirds.

08 Comptroller & Auditor General (CAG)

Constitutional Basis & Appointment

  • Under Article 148, there shall be a Comptroller and Auditor-General of India, head of the Indian Audit and Accounts Department, described as "guardian of the public purse" at both the Centre and State levels.
  • Appointed by the President, by warrant under hand and seal, for 6 years or until age 65, whichever is earlier.
  • Removable only in the same manner and on the same grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court — i.e., by presidential order after an address by each House of Parliament, supported by a majority of the total membership and a two-thirds majority of members present and voting, on grounds of proved misbehaviour or incapacity.
  • Not eligible for reappointment, and not eligible for any further office under the Government of India or any State after ceasing to hold office [Article 148(4)].
  • No Minister represents the CAG in Parliament.
  • Salary and other conditions of service are determined by Parliament by law; until so determined, they follow the Second Schedule of the Constitution — the CAG's salary is equal to that of a Supreme Court judge.
  • Administrative expenses of the CAG's office are charged to the Consolidated Fund of India [Article 148(6)].
  • Power to grant or refuse leave to the CAG vests in the President.
  • The CAG may resign at any time by addressing a resignation letter to the President.

Duties & Functions

  • Article 149 prescribes CAG's duties/powers, to be set by Parliament, under the CAG (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act, 1971.
  • Audits all expenditure and receipts related to the Consolidated Fund, Contingency Fund, and Public Account, of both the Union and each State.
  • Also audits accounts of stores and stock kept in any Union/State office or department.
  • Submits three audit reports to the President: on appropriation accounts, finance accounts, and public undertakings.
  • Reports relating to the accounts of the Union are submitted to the President, who causes them to be laid before both Houses of Parliament [Article 151(1)]; reports relating to a State's accounts go to that State's Governor, for tabling in the State Legislature [Article 151(2)].
  • The Public Accounts Committee of Parliament debates CAG reports extensively; the CAG is described as the PAC's "friend, philosopher and guide" and attends its sittings.
  • Under Article 279, CAG's function of certifying net tax proceeds is separate from — and also referenced alongside — the Finance Commission's use of that certification.
  • Dr. B.R. Ambedkar described the CAG as "probably the most important officer under the Constitution of India."
  • The CAG's (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act, 1971 came into effect in 1971, and was amended in 1976 to separate accounting from auditing — after this amendment, CAG's task is confined to auditing government accounts (though at the State level, both accounting and auditing continue to be performed by the CAG).
  • CAG also serves as External Auditor for the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) and audits the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO).

Officeholders

  • Girish Chandra Murmu was India's 14th CAG (8 August 2020 – 20 November 2024), succeeding Rajiv Mehrishi.

⟳ Current (2026)

  • 15th and current CAG of India: K. Sanjay Murthy, IAS (1989 batch), sworn in 21 November 2024, succeeding Girish Chandra Murmu.

09 Attorney General & Advocate General

Attorney General of India (AGI)

  • Provided for under Article 76; the highest law officer in the country and part of the Union Executive.
  • Appointed by the President, on the advice of the government, from among persons qualified to be appointed a Judge of the Supreme Court (i.e., a citizen of India who has been a High Court judge for 5 years, or a High Court advocate for 10 years, or, in the President's opinion, an eminent jurist).
  • Term of office is not fixed; the AGI holds office during the pleasure of the President and may be removed by the President at any time.
  • Duty: to advise the Government of India on legal matters and perform other legal duties referred/assigned by the President [Article 76(2)].
  • Has the right of audience in all courts in the territory of India in performance of duties [Article 76(3)].
  • Has the right to speak and take part in the proceedings of both Houses of Parliament (or a joint sitting) and to be a member of a parliamentary committee, but is not entitled to vote [Article 88]; enjoys the privileges/immunities available to an MP.
  • Receives remuneration as determined by the President [Article 76(4)].
  • The AGI is not a full-time government counsel and is not debarred from private legal practice (subject to restrictions such as not advising/appearing against the Government of India, or defending criminal accused without government permission).
  • Appears on behalf of the government in all Supreme Court cases; is one of the very few non-members who may participate in Parliament's proceedings without being a member (along with, in a narrower sense, the Vice-President, as ex-officio Rajya Sabha chairman).
  • The Solicitor General of India is the country's second law officer, subordinate to the AGI, assisting the Attorney General and himself assisted by several Additional Solicitors General.

⟳ Current (2026)

  • Attorney General of India: R. Venkataramani, appointed 1 October 2022, succeeding K.K. Venugopal; his term has been extended to 30 September 2027.

Advocate General for a State

  • Provided for under Article 165; the Governor of each State appoints a person qualified to be a High Court Judge as Advocate-General for that State.
  • Duty: to advise the State Government on legal matters and perform other legal-character duties assigned by the Governor [Article 165(2)]; is the first legal adviser to the State Government.
  • Holds office during the pleasure of the Governor [Article 165(3)], and receives remuneration as the Governor determines.
  • Has the right to speak in the State Legislature, but not the right to vote.

10 National Commissions for SC, ST & Backward Classes

National Commission for Scheduled Castes (NCSC)

  • Provided for under Article 338 in Part XVI of the Constitution; the current form was introduced by the Constitution (89th Amendment) Act, 2003, operative from 2004.
  • Consists of a Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson, and three other members, appointed by the President by warrant under hand and seal.
  • Term of office: 3 years.
  • Functions: to investigate, monitor, inquire, advise, and participate in the planning process concerning safeguards for Scheduled Castes; presents its report annually to the President; has the powers of a civil court.
  • Purpose: to provide Scheduled Castes safeguards against exploitation and to promote their social, economic, educational and cultural development.

National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (NCST)

  • Introduced via a new Article 338A, also by the Constitution (89th Amendment) Act, 2003.
  • Composition: a Chairperson, a Vice-Chairperson, and three full-time members, including one lady member.
  • Term of office of all four: 3 years from date of assuming charge.
  • The Chairperson holds the rank of a Union Cabinet Minister; the Vice-Chairperson holds the rank of a Minister of State; other members hold the rank of a Secretary to the Government of India.
  • Functions (Article 338A, Clause 5): to investigate, monitor, inquire, advise, and participate in the planning process; presents its report annually to the President; has the powers of a civil court.

National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC)

  • Given constitutional status under Article 338B, by the 102nd Constitution Amendment Act, 2018.
  • Consists of five members: a Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson, and three other Members, appointed by the President by warrant under hand and seal.
  • Term of office: 3 years.
  • Conditions of service and tenure of the Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson and other Members are determined by the President.
  • Functions mirror the NCSC/NCST: investigate, monitor, inquire, advise, participate in planning; report annually to the President; powers of a civil court.

11 Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities

  • The Seventh Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956 inserted a new Article 350-B in Part XVII of the Constitution.
  • Establishes a Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities, appointed by the President, for the Union level (all-India) — not one such officer per State.
  • This officer is officially designated the Commissioner for Linguistic Minorities in India.
  • Duty: to investigate all matters relating to constitutional safeguards for linguistic minorities, and to report to the President at such intervals as the President may direct.
  • The President places all such reports before each House of Parliament, and sends them to the concerned State governments.
  • The Constitution does not specify qualifications, tenure, or removal procedure for this office.
