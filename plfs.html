01

What is PLFS

PLFS is India's official nationwide labour market survey, conducted by the National Statistical Office (NSO) under the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI), to provide periodic estimates of employment, unemployment, labour force participation, and other labour market indicators.

Key Facts

Launched 2017 · Conducted by NSO · Ministry: MoSPI

Replaced the NSS Employment–Unemployment Survey (EUS)

Reports: Annual (rural + urban) & Quarterly (urban-only, pre-2025)

Purpose

Estimate employment, unemployment, LFPR, WPR, UR; analyse by gender, age, education, industry, occupation.

Exam Trap

PLFS did not invent labour statistics in India — it replaced the quinquennial EUS. BPSC option-writers love swapping "launched" with "replaced" or naming the wrong predecessor survey (Census, ASI, Economic Survey are common distractor options — see MCQ 4).

02

Why PLFS Was Needed

Pre-2017 Background

Before 2017, employment data came from the NSSO's quinquennial Employment & Unemployment Surveys (EUS) — comprehensive figures arrived only once every five years, far too infrequent for a fast-changing economy.

The Task Force on Improving Employment Data (2017), chaired by NITI Aayog Vice-Chairman Arvind Panagariya, recommended an annual household survey with quarterly urban estimates — the direct blueprint for PLFS.

High-Value Exam Trap — Two Different Committees

Aspirants routinely confuse two separate bodies. The Panagariya Task Force (2017) designed the annual + quarterly survey architecture that became PLFS. But the underlying concept of a periodic labour survey was first recommended earlier by the Amitabh Kundu Committee (2009–10), appointed by the National Statistical Commission, which proposed a quarterly urban / annual rural design and led to pilot studies in Himachal Pradesh, Odisha and Gujarat (2011–13). Both names are fair game — questions may ask "who conceived PLFS" (Kundu) versus "who designed the 2017 rollout" (Panagariya).

Quick Notes

NSSO = Data collection agency
CSO = Data analysis + GDP compilation
Both originally under MoSPI
2000: Rangarajan Committee recommends merger of NSSO + CSO
2005: Government accepts the recommendation
2019: Merger actually implemented → NSO formed

Know Your Terms

LFPR (Labour Force Participation Rate): % of persons in the labour force (working, seeking, or available for work) in the population.

WPR (Worker Population Ratio): % of employed persons in the population.

UR (Unemployment Rate): % of unemployed persons among the labour force — not the total population.

NEET: Youth Not in Employment, Education or Training — a job-quality indicator now reported in PLFS.

06

Quarterly Bulletins

Since the January 2025 revamp, PLFS releases rural + urban Quarterly Bulletins in Current Weekly Status (CWS), tracking the labour market every three months across the four quarters of 2025–26: Jul–Sep 2025, Oct–Dec 2025, and Jan–Mar 2026.

Definition Check

Usual Status (ps+ss) looks back over the last 365 days — captures long-run, stable trends. Current Weekly Status (CWS) looks back over the last 7 days — captures short-term/seasonal volatility. Annual reports use Usual Status headline figures; Quarterly bulletins use CWS. This pairing (report-type ↔ reference-period) is a favourite MCQ pattern.

BPSC Exam Trends on PLFS researched addition

Compiled from BPSC's own question-pattern behaviour and how UPSC/state-PSC prelims have historically tested PLFS — used here as a proxy since BPSC's economy section closely mirrors the UPSC prelims style.

Where PLFS sits in the syllabus

PLFS falls under the Indian Economy section of BPSC Prelims General Studies (the paper is 150 objective questions with negative marking). Economy questions in BPSC routinely blend static institutional facts (who conducts a survey, under which ministry) with current-affairs data points (latest LFPR/UR figures) — exactly the two layers this document covers.

The four recurring BPSC question-types on data-body topics

① Agency identification ② Indicator ↔ definition matching ③ Year-on-year data comparison ④ Committee / chairperson recall

Type ① and ④ are pure recall ("who conducts", "who chaired") and are the highest-frequency, lowest-difficulty question style for statistical-survey topics across BPSC and sister exams. Types ② and ③ need you to actually distinguish LFPR/WPR/UR from each other and track which figure moved between report years — exactly what the stat cards in Section 5 are built for.

Historical Fact Worth Knowing

PLFS's very first report (2017–18, released 2019) recorded India's unemployment rate at 6.1% — a 45-year high at the time — which became a major political controversy after the data reportedly leaked ahead of the 2019 general election. This single episode is a recurring "context" fact used to frame current-affairs questions on PLFS credibility and data governance.

What examiners are likely to lean on next cycle

1. The calendar-year shift (Jan 2025 revamp) is brand-new and highly "quotable" — expect it to be tested directly for a few cycles, the way earlier exams milked the 2019 NSO formation.

2. NEET and youth unemployment (15–29) figures are increasingly used in both prelims MCQs and mains essay/GS answers on jobless growth and the demographic dividend — treat these as dual-use data.

3. The Kundu Committee vs Panagariya Task Force distinction (Section 2) is the kind of "two similar-sounding facts" trap BPSC option-setters are fond of — always double-check which one is being asked about before answering.

General BPSC prelims prep signal

Across recent BPSC cycles, aspirants and coaching trackers consistently flag Polity, History (with strong Bihar-specific weightage), Geography, Economy, Science, and Current Affairs as the recurring subject buckets — and note that many questions repeat directly or in modified form across years, which is exactly why a compact, colour-coded, fact-dense revision sheet like this one pays off disproportionately versus re-reading the raw report.
