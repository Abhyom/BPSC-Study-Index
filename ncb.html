01 NITI Aayog

National Institution for Transforming India — successor think tank to the Planning Commission.

Establishment & nature

  • NITI stands for National Institution for Transforming India; established on 1st January, 2015 under a Cabinet resolution — an extra-constitutional, non-statutory body, not created by an Act of Parliament.
  • Replaced the Planning Commission. Prime Minister Narendra Modi announced the intent to abolish the Planning Commission from the Red Fort on 15 August 2014; NITI Aayog itself came into existence on 1 January 2015.
  • Functions to foster Cooperative Federalism and follows a "Bottom-Up" approach to planning.

Structure

  • Chairperson — the Prime Minister (ex-officio); acts as a government think tank, providing directional/strategic policy inputs.
  • Governing Council — headed by the PM; comprises Chief Ministers of all States/UTs and Lieutenant Governors/Administrators of UTs without a legislature. Governors of States are not members of the Governing Council.
  • Regional Councils — can be constituted for issues affecting more than one state in a region, in the PM's opinion; headed by the PM or his nominee, and include the relevant CMs and LGs/Administrators.
  • Vice-Chairperson — in charge of day-to-day activities; appointed by the PM; holds the rank of a Cabinet Minister.
  • Four full-time members holding the rank of Minister of State at the Union level.
  • Two part-time members — academics from leading universities/research organisations, appointed on a rotational basis.
  • Up to four Union Cabinet Ministers nominated by the PM as ex-officio members.
  • Chief Executive Officer (CEO) — rank of Secretary to the Government of India; appointed by the PM for a fixed tenure; serves as Member-Secretary to the Aayog.

Functions

  • Design of policy and programme framework.
  • Fostering cooperative federalism.
  • Monitoring and evaluation.
  • Acting as a think-tank, and knowledge/innovation hub.

Officeholders (historical)

  • First Vice-Chairman: Arvind Panagariya (January 2015 – August 2017).
  • Subsequent Vice-Chairmen have included Rajiv Kumar and Suman Bery (1 May 2022 – 30 April 2026).
  • Past CEOs have included Amitabh Kant and B.V.R. Subrahmanyam (CEO from May 2022, later moved to Finance Secretary).

⟳ Current (2026)

  • Vice-Chairperson: Ashok Kumar Lahiri (economist; former Chief Economic Adviser and 15th Finance Commission member), appointment notified 24 April 2026, assumed office 8 May 2026, succeeding Suman Bery.
  • CEO: Nidhi Chhibber (1988-batch IAS, Madhya Pradesh cadre), holding additional charge since an order dated 26 February 2026, succeeding B.V.R. Subrahmanyam.
  • Full-time Members notified alongside the Vice-Chairperson (24 April 2026): Rajiv Gauba, K.V. Raju, Gobardhan Das, M. Srinivas, and Abhay Karandikar.

02 Planning Commission

  • Established on 15 March 1950 by an executive resolution of the Union Cabinet — neither a constitutional nor a statutory body.
  • Set up on the recommendation of an advisory planning board chaired by K.C. Neogy.
  • The Prime Minister was its ex-officio Chairman; Jawaharlal Nehru was the first Chairman.
  • First Deputy Chairman: Gulzarilal Nanda; the Deputy Chairman's rank was equivalent to a Cabinet Minister.
  • Other Deputy Chairmen included Manmohan Singh (1985–1987), Pranab Mukherjee (1991–1996), and Montek Singh Ahluwalia (2004–2014).
  • Main function: assess the country's resources and prepare five-year plans for the central government for balanced resource utilisation; also gave suggestions on states' annual plans, with roughly 70 percent of Centre-to-State grants historically routed on its recommendation.
  • Described as the "Economic Cabinet" for both the Union and the States, owing to its extra-constitutional discretionary powers and the comprehensive scope of matters referred to it.
  • M.V. Mathur (a member of the Third Finance Commission, formed 1960) suggested a merger of the Finance Commission and Planning Commission.
  • Replaced by NITI Aayog on 1 January 2015.

03 National Development Council (NDC)

  • Constituted on 6th August, 1952 by a government (executive) resolution — non-constitutional and non-statutory.
  • Acts as a bridge between the Centre and the States; also functions as coordinator between the Planning Commission (now NITI Aayog) and State Governments.
  • Headed by the Prime Minister; members include Union Cabinet Ministers, Chief Ministers of all States, Chief Ministers/Administrators of Union Territories, and members of the Planning Commission (now NITI Aayog).
  • Functions: prescribes guidelines for formulating the National Plan (including resource assessment); considers the National Plan as formulated by the Planning Commission; considers important social/economic policy questions affecting national development; reviews Plan implementation and recommends measures to achieve Plan targets; and finally approves the five-year plans.
  • The Secretary of the Planning Commission (now NITI Aayog) also serves as the ex-officio Secretary of the NDC.
  • The NDC should meet at least twice a year.

04 Lokpal

Origins & Legislative History

  • The earliest democratic institution created anywhere for redressal of citizens' grievances is the Scandinavian institution of Ombudsman, first created in Sweden in 1809. The Indian model of the Ombudsman is the Lokpal.
  • The First Administrative Reforms Commission (1966–1970), in its interim report dated 20th October, 1966, under the chairmanship of Morarji Desai (set up 5 January 1966), recommended setting up a Lokpal at the Centre and Lokayuktas at the State level, and also recommended a Local Body Ombudsman. K. Hanumanthaiah became its Chairman in 1967; the Commission's term ended in 1970.
  • The first Lokpal Bill was introduced in 1968 in the 4th Lok Sabha; it was passed by the Lok Sabha on 20 August 1969 but lapsed when the Lok Sabha was dissolved before Rajya Sabha passage.
  • The Lokpal Bill, 2011 was introduced in the Lok Sabha on 4 August 2011 by the then Minister of Parliamentary Affairs, V. Narayanaswami; it was referred to the Parliamentary Standing Committee and was termed "weak" by civil society members.
  • Civil society representatives on the panel drafting the Lokpal Bill: Anna Hazare, Arvind Kejriwal, Shanti Bhushan, N. Santosh Hegde, and Prashant Bhushan.
  • The Lokpal institution was established for the first time in India by The Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013, which came into force on 16 January 2014.

Composition & Selection

  • Under the 2013 Act, the Lokpal comprises 1 Chairperson and not more than eight members; at least 50% of members must be judicial members, and at least 50% must be from among SCs, STs, OBCs, minorities, and women.
  • Selection Committee (Section 4(1)): Prime Minister (Chairperson), Speaker of the Lok Sabha, Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha, the Chief Justice of India (or an SC judge nominated by him), and one eminent jurist nominated by the President on the other four members' recommendation. The Chairman of the Rajya Sabha has no seat on this committee.
  • The President appoints the Chairperson and members on this Selection Committee's recommendation.
  • Salaries/allowances/service conditions: same as the Chief Justice of India for the Chairperson; same as a Supreme Court judge for other members.
  • Minimum age for Chairperson or a Member: 45 years on the date of assuming office.
  • A Chairperson or Member cannot simultaneously be an MP or a member of any State/UT legislature.

Jurisdiction & Procedure

  • Jurisdiction covers a wide range of public servants — from the Prime Minister, ministers and MPs, to Groups A, B, C and D central government employees — and even the Lokpal's own members.
  • Applies to Indian public servants both within and outside India (Section 1(3)).
  • The Act does not exempt a sitting Prime Minister from Lokpal jurisdiction, but a Lokpal inquiry against the PM is barred where the allegation concerns international relations, external/internal security, public order, atomic energy, or space; a complaint against the PM proceeds only if the full Lokpal bench considers initiation and at least two-thirds of members approve.
  • On receiving a complaint, the Lokpal orders a preliminary inquiry (to be completed within 90 days) or investigation by any agency; thereafter it may order investigation, departmental proceedings, other action, or closure.
  • The Lokpal cannot take Suo Moto action against a public servant.

Officeholders

  • India's first Lokpal: Justice Pinaki Chandra Ghose (retired SC judge), appointed 19 March 2019, oath administered 23 March 2019; retired on superannuation 27 May 2022.
  • Following Justice Ghose, Justice Pradip Kumar Mohanty held additional charge as Chairperson (28 May 2022 – 26 February 2024).

⟳ Current (2026)

  • Chairperson of Lokpal: Justice Ajay Manikrao Khanwilkar (retired Supreme Court judge), appointed 27 February 2024, oath administered 10 March 2024.
  • As of 2024, the Lokpal reached its full sanctioned strength of nine members (Chairperson plus eight), including judicial members Justice L. Narayana Swamy, Justice Sanjay Yadav, and non-judicial members such as Sushil Chandra (former Chief Election Commissioner).

05 Lokayukta

  • Lokayuktas are the state-level equivalents of the central Lokpal, dealing with corruption complaints against certain public functionaries.
  • Under the 2013 Act, every State was required to establish a Lokayukta (by state law) within one year of the Act's commencement, if not already established.
  • Term of a Lokayukta: 5 years or until age 65, whichever is earlier; not eligible for reappointment for a second term.
  • Lokayukta recommendations are only advisory, not binding on the state government.
  • Maharashtra was the first state to establish the institution of Lokayukta — through the Lokayukta and Upa-Lokayuktas Act, 1971, which came into effect on 25 October 1972.
  • Odisha was the first state to pass a Lokayukta Act (in 1970), though it came into force only in 1983; Odisha was also the first state to abolish its Lokayukta (in 1993).
  • Uttarakhand was the first state to pass a Lokayukta Bill in 2011, on 1 November 2011.
  • Sequence of state Lokayukta establishment noted in the source: Maharashtra (1972), Bihar (1973), Uttar Pradesh (1975), Madhya Pradesh (1982), Andhra Pradesh (1983), Karnataka (1984), Assam (1986).
  • Under the Uttar Pradesh Lokayukta Act, 1975, the Lokayukta submits its report to the Governor, who presents it in both Houses of the State Legislature. The UP Lokayukta tenure was amended from 8 years to 5 years or age 70 (whichever earlier), effective 26 February 2024.
  • Under Section 7 of the Rajasthan Lokayukta and Deputy Lokayukta Act, 1973, the Lokayukta may investigate: unreasonable harm/suffering caused by a public servant; abuse of official position for illegal benefit; and corruption or lack of transparency by a public servant. Section 8 of the same Act separately prescribes matters not subject to investigation.
  • In 2014, a high-level committee under Advocate General Narpat Mal Lodha was constituted by the Rajasthan government to review provisions of the existing Lokayukta Act.

06 National & State Human Rights Commissions

NHRC — Establishment & Composition

  • The NHRC is a statutory (non-constitutional) body, established on 12 October 1993 under the Protection of Human Rights Act (PHRA), 1993.
  • The President promulgated the Protection of Human Rights Ordinance, 1993 under Article 123 of the Constitution on 28 September 1993; the Act itself came into force the same date.
  • The NHRC is described as an embodiment of India's concern for promotion/protection of human rights; its functions are mainly recommendatory in nature, though it has the powers of a civil court (summoning, requiring documents) and can act suo-moto.
  • Members and authorised officers of the Commission (and State Commissions) are deemed Public Servants under Section 21 of the IPC.
  • For complaints against members of the armed forces, the Commission's role is limited to seeking a report from the Central Government and making recommendations — it does not enquire itself.
  • A Special Public Prosecutor for a Human Rights Court must be an advocate in practice for not less than 7 years (Section 31).

NHRC — Appointment, Tenure & Removal

  • Chairperson and members appointed by the President on the recommendation of a 6-member committee: Prime Minister (head), Speaker of the Lok Sabha, Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha, Leaders of Opposition in both Houses, and the Union Home Minister.
  • Chairperson must be a former Chief Justice of India or a (retired or sitting, per the 2019 amendment) Judge of the Supreme Court.
  • Under the Protection of Human Rights (Amendment) Act, 2019: term of office of the chairman/members reduced to 3 years or age 70, whichever is earlier; provision made for three members knowledgeable/experienced in human rights, of whom at least one must be a woman.
  • Chairperson/members are not eligible for further employment under the Central or State Government after their tenure.
  • Removal is by the President — either after a Supreme Court inquiry on grounds of proven incapacity/misbehaviour, or on grounds of insolvency, unsoundness of mind/body infirmity, criminal conviction, or engaging in outside paid employment.

NHRC — Officeholders

  • First Chairman (President) of the NHRC: Justice Ranganath Misra (12 October 1993 – 24 November 1996).
  • Later chairpersons have included Justice H.L. Dattu (29 Feb 2016 – 2 Dec 2020) and Justice Arun Kumar Mishra (2 June 2021 – 1 June 2024).

⟳ Current (2026)

  • Chairperson of the NHRC: Justice V. Ramasubramanian (former Supreme Court judge), took charge 30 December 2024, succeeding Justice Arun Kumar Mishra.

State Human Rights Commission (SHRC)

  • The same Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993 provides for creation of a State Human Rights Commission at the state level; it can inquire into human rights violations on subjects in the State List and Concurrent List (Seventh Schedule).
  • Chairperson and members are appointed by the Governor, on the recommendation of a committee headed by the Chief Minister, with the Speaker of the Legislative Assembly, the state's Home Minister, and the Leader of Opposition in the Assembly as members (plus, where a Legislative Council exists, its Chairman and Leader of Opposition).
  • A sitting High Court judge or sitting District Judge can be appointed as an SHRC member only after consultation with the Chief Justice of the concerned High Court.
  • Post-2019 amendment, a Chief Justice of the High Court or a Judge of the High Court is eligible for appointment as SHRC Chairperson.
  • If a vacancy occurs in the Chairperson's office, the Governor may authorise a member to act as Chairperson.
  • The SHRC submits its annual report to the State Government, which lays it before the State Legislature along with a memorandum of action taken.
  • As of the source, 25 states had constituted their State Human Rights Commissions.

07 Central & State Information Commissions

  • The Central Information Commission was established by the Central Government in 2005 by an official Gazette notification, under the Right to Information Act, 2005 — it is not a constitutional body.
  • Composition: a Chief Information Commissioner and not more than ten Information Commissioners.
  • Appointment: by the President, on the recommendation of a committee of the Prime Minister (Chairperson), the Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha, and a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the PM.
  • Original tenure under the RTI Act, 2005: up to 5 years or age 65, whichever earlier. The RTI (Amendment) Act, 2019 instead left the Chief/other Information Commissioners' tenure to be determined by the Central Government; they are not eligible for reappointment.
  • Duties: receive and inquire into complaints on RTI information requests; can order an inquiry on suo-moto grounds; has the powers of a civil court for summoning/documents.
  • Removal: by the President, on grounds of bankruptcy, unsound mind, bodily/mental infirmity, criminal conviction, engagement in paid employment, or (via Supreme Court inquiry) proved misbehaviour/incapacity.

State Information Commission (SIC)

  • An independent statutory committee under the RTI Act with jurisdiction over state government bodies, PSUs and authorities.
  • Composition: Chief Information Commissioner + up to 10 Information Commissioners, all appointed by the Governor on recommendation of the Chief Minister, a nominated Cabinet Minister, and the Leader of Opposition in the Legislative Assembly.
  • Membership qualification: eminence in public life with experience in law, science & technology, governance, social service, management, journalism, mass media, or administration; must not be an MP/MLA, affiliated with a political party, or hold a business/profession/office of profit.
  • Tenure: till age 65 or 5 years. An Information Commissioner may be elevated to State Chief Information Commissioner but for a combined maximum tenure of 5 years.
  • Removal: by the Governor on grounds of bankruptcy, unsound mind, bodily/mental infirmity, criminal conviction, or paid employment.

⟳ Current (2026)

  • Chief Information Commissioner of India: Raj Kumar Goyal (former Law Secretary of India), assumed office 15 December 2025, succeeding Heeralal Samariya.

08 Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI)

  • Set up in 1963 by a resolution of the Ministry of Home Affairs — not a statutory body; it derives its powers from the Delhi Special Police Establishment Act, 1946.
  • Establishment recommended by the K. Santhanam Committee (Committee on Prevention of Corruption).
  • Headed by a Director, assisted by a Special Director; Director's tenure is 2 years under the CVC Act, 2003, renewable by one-year extensions.
  • Director appointed by the Government of India on recommendation of a 3-member committee: the Prime Minister, the Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha, and the Chief Justice of India.
  • Functions under the aegis of the Department of Personnel, Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances & Pensions (PMO).
  • Role: prevent corruption and maintain administrative integrity; works under the supervision of the CVC for matters under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988.
  • Also investigates offences connected with infringement of economic/fiscal laws — customs and central excise, export-import control, income tax, foreign exchange regulations — typically at the request of, or in consultation with, the concerned department.
  • Can begin a probe at a state government's request if the Union government agrees, or if directed by the Supreme Court or a High Court; can take up a case suo-moto only in Union Territories.
  • Founder and first Director: D.P. Kohli (1 April 1963 – 31 May 1968).

⟳ Current (2026)

  • Director, CBI: Praveen Sood, IPS (1986-batch, Karnataka cadre), in office since 25 May 2023; his term was extended by one year to 24 May 2026 after the selection committee (PM, CJI, Leader of Opposition) could not reach consensus on a successor in May 2025. Verify current status separately if the exact date matters, as his extended term has since lapsed.

09 Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)

  • Established in 1964 by an executive resolution, on the recommendation of the Santhanam Committee (Committee on Prevention of Corruption, 1962–64); given statutory status only later, via the CVC Act, 2003.
  • Designated as the agency to receive and act on complaints/disclosures of corruption or misuse of office from a whistleblower.
  • Apex composition: Central Vigilance Commissioner + not more than two Vigilance Commissioners, appointed by the President under warrant, for a term of 4 years or age 65 (whichever earlier), on the recommendation of a 3-member committee of the PM, the Minister of Home Affairs, and the Leader of Opposition.
  • Members are not eligible for further employment after tenure; salary/allowances similar to the UPSC.
  • Removal: on Supreme Court recommendation, after inquiry, on grounds of proven incapacity/misbehaviour.
  • Bodies/persons who can approach the CVC: the Central government, the Lokpal, and whistleblowers.
  • The CVC is not itself an investigating agency — it gets investigations done through the CBI or through Chief Vigilance Officers (CVOs) in government offices.
  • Empowered to inquire into offences alleged under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 for certain categories of public servants.

10 National Disaster Management Authority & Tiers

  • Established under the Disaster Management Act, 2005, which also established the SDMA and DDMA.
  • NDMA: Prime Minister is ex-officio Chairperson; not more than 9 members nominated by the Chairperson; Vice-Chairperson has the rank of Cabinet Minister, other members the rank of Minister of State. It is the apex disaster-management body and works under the Ministry of Home Affairs.
  • NDMA's role: lay down policies, plans and guidelines for disaster management, and ensure timely/effective response to disasters.
  • SDMA: Chief Minister is ex-officio Chairperson; not more than 9 members; the Chief of the State Executive Committee is an ex-officio member. A State Executive Committee assists the SDMA and coordinates action per SDMA guidelines, replicating the NEC's powers/functions at state level.
  • DDMA: District Magistrate/Deputy Commissioner is Chairperson, the Chairman of Zilla Parishad is co-chairperson; not more than 7 members. Elected members of local authorities are ex-officio members (in tribal areas, the chief executive of the District Autonomous Council is a member); a maximum of 2 district-level officers are appointed by the State.
  • DDMA acts as the district-level planning, coordinating and implementing body for disaster management, coordinating with the upper two tiers on prevention, mitigation and preparedness.

11 National Investigation Agency (NIA)

  • Established in 2009 under the NIA Act, 2008.
  • A central agency to investigate and prosecute offences affecting the sovereignty, security and integrity of India; acts as the Central Counter-Terrorism Law Enforcement Agency without needing special permission from the states.
  • A State Government may request the Centre to hand over investigation of a case registered for a scheduled offence; the Centre may also order the NIA suo-moto to take over investigation of any scheduled offence anywhere in India.
  • Investigates/prosecutes offences under the Atomic Energy Act, 1962 and the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 1967.
  • Currently has nine branches besides specialised units.
  • The NIA (Amendment) Bill, 2019 expanded NIA's investigative jurisdiction to: human trafficking; offences related to counterfeit currency/banknotes; manufacture/sale of prohibited arms; cyber-terrorism; and offences under the Explosive Substances Act, 1908.
  • Special NIA Courts try cases under Sections 11 and 22 of the NIA Act 2008; jurisdictional questions are decided by the Central Government; presiding judges are appointed by the Central Government on recommendation of the Chief Justice of the concerned High Court.
  • The Supreme Court can transfer cases between special courts across states in the interest of justice.
  • NIA Special Courts hold all the powers of a Court of Sessions under the CrPC, 1973; appeals from their judgments (other than interlocutory orders) lie to the High Court, on both facts and law.
  • State Governments are also empowered to appoint one or more additional special courts in their states.

12 Committees & Non-Statutory Commissions

Corruption, Political Nexus & Administrative Reform

  • Vohra Committee — set up in 1993 to study the problem of criminalisation of politics and the nexus among criminals, politicians and bureaucrats.
  • Rajamannar Committee — recommended ending the Indian Administrative Services (IAS) and Indian Police Services (IPS).
  • Santhanam Committee (Committee on Prevention of Corruption, 1962–64) — chaired by K. Santhanam (with four other MPs and two senior officers); recommended establishing the CVC.
  • P.C. Hota Committee on Civil Services Reforms — constituted in February 2004; submitted its report within six months.
  • A committee under Professor Sukhadeo Thorat was constituted by the UPSC in July 2000 to review the Civil Services Examination.
  • Bakshi Tek Chand Committee (1948) — set up to enquire into the patent system and recommend reforms.
  • Kelkar Committee — studied and evaluated the public-private partnership (PPP) model in India.

Constitution-Review & Rights-related Committees

  • Swarn Singh Committee ("Constitutional Amendment Committee", 1976), under Sardar Swaran Singh — considered the priority of Directive Principles of State Policy over Fundamental Rights, and separately considered (and found inappropriate) a presidential system of governance for India; its recommendations fed into the 42nd Constitutional Amendment.
  • National Commission to Review the Working of the Constitution — set up by the NDA government on 22 February 2000 under Justice M.N. Venkatachaliah; its report is recommendatory in nature and was not approved by Parliament; it reviewed 50 years of constitutional implementation and submitted its final report on 31 March 2002.

Education & Social-Backwardness Commissions

  • Secondary Education Commission ("Mudaliar Commission"), 1952–53 — chaired by Dr. A. Laxman Swami Mudaliar (Vice-Chancellor, Madras University); aimed to improve secondary education quality, emphasising science, agriculture and technical education aligned with national development needs.
  • Mandal Commission — constituted in 1979 under the Janata Party government (PM Morarji Desai), chaired by Bindeshwari Prasad (B.P.) Mandal; tasked with identifying and recommending steps for advancement of socially/educationally backward classes; submitted its report in 1980.
  • The 1990 report of the National Commission for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes identified the main causes of atrocities against SCs/STs as: land alienation/disputes, bonded labour, and indebtedness/non-payment of minimum wages.

13 Minority, Women & Backward-Class Bodies

  • The National Commission for Minorities became a statutory body with the National Commission for Minorities Act, 1992 (in force from 17 May 1993); the first statutory Commission itself was set up on 17 May 1993, via a Gazette notification issued 23 October 1993 by the Ministry of Welfare.
  • Communities originally notified as minorities: Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists and Zoroastrians (Parsis); Jains were added as a sixth notified minority community via notification dated 27 January 2014.
  • The term "Minority" is not defined in the Constitution of India, even though the Constitution recognises and protects religious and linguistic minorities.
  • Functions of the National Commission for Women are specified in Section 10 of the National Commission for Women Act, 1990 (enforced 30 August 1990).

⟳ Current (2026)

  • Chairperson, National Commission for Backward Classes: Sadhvi Niranjan Jyoti, assumed office 18 March 2026.

14 Boundary Markers — What Counts as "Non-Constitutional"

The source repeatedly uses a small set of constitutional bodies purely as the contrast answer in "which of these is/is not a constitutional body" questions. They mark the boundary of this ledger's scope rather than being facts about non-constitutional bodies themselves.

⚠ Contrast set used throughout the source

The source consistently uses Union Public Service Commission (Art. 315), State Public Service Commission (Art. 315), Finance Commission (Art. 280), and Election Commission (Art. 324) as the constitutional bodies against which NITI Aayog, the erstwhile Planning Commission, the NDC, and the NHRC are repeatedly contrasted as non-constitutional/extra-constitutional. These four are intentionally excluded from the topic sections above, in line with this dossier's "Non-Constitutional Bodies" scope.
