Constitutional Basis & Composition of Lok Sabha

Parliament & Lok Sabha

  • Parliament is the legislative organ of the Union Government
  • Articles 79 to 122 in Part V deal with it.
  • Art 79
    • Parliament of India consists of the President and the two Houses — House of the People (Lok Sabha) and Council of States (Rajya Sabha).
  • Lok Sabha = the House of the People
  • its members are chosen by direct election.
  • Art 81
    • Not more than 530 members chosen by direct election from territorial constituencies in the States and not more than 20 to represent the Union Territories, chosen as Parliament provides by law — so maximum strength 550.
    • (As amended by the 31st Amendment 1973 and the Goa, Daman and Diu Reorganisation Act 1987.)
  • Present strength 543 = 524 from States + 19 from Union Territories.
  • Options 547, 545 and 552 are wrong as the Constitutional maximum.
  • Art 331
    • If the President opines the Anglo-Indian community is not adequately represented, he could nominate not more than 2 members of that community
    • Earlier the maximum could be 552 (530 + 20 + 2).
    • The arrangement was not extended beyond 25 January 2020 by the 104th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2019 [126th Amendment Bill 2019], so nomination of two Anglo-Indian members has ended
    • The maximum is now 550.
    • The President (not the Minorities Commission, PM or Vice-President) held the power.
  • Art 84(b)
    • Minimum age for election to Lok Sabha is 25 years and to the Council of States 30 years
    • 18 and 21 are wrong.
  • The other qualifications
    • citizen of India, must make and subscribe an oath, and other qualifications prescribed by Parliament by law.

How the Strength Grew

  • 7th Amendment 1956
    • Fixed membership at 520.
  • 31st Amendment 1973
    • Raised strength from 525 to 545
    • State seats raised from 500 to 525, UT seats reduced from 25 to 20.
  • Art 81 (as amended) now allows 530 + 20 = 550
  • 6th/22nd, 13th/38th and 11th/42nd Amendments are the wrong pairs for increasing State seats
  • 35th and 42nd/45th are wrong for the 525→545 rise.

Union Territories in Lok Sabha & Rajya Sabha

  • Lok Sabha UT seats now 19
    • Delhi 7, Andaman & Nicobar 1, Chandigarh 1, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu 2, Lakshadweep 1, Puducherry 1, Jammu & Kashmir 5, Ladakh 1.
  • Rajya Sabha UT seats 8
    • Delhi 3, Puducherry 1, Jammu & Kashmir 4.
  • Maximum UT representation in Lok Sabha is 20 (not 15 or 25)
  • there are no UT-reserved extra seats beyond that.

Seat Allocation, Census & Delimitation

Basis of Allocation

  • State-wise seat allocation in Lok Sabha is on population — not area, poverty or language.
  • Art 81
    • Each State is divided into territorial constituencies so that the ratio of the population of each constituency to the seats allotted is, as far as practicable, the same throughout the State.
    • This does not apply to a State with population up to six million.
  • Art 82
    • Allocation of seats to States rests on the 1971 Census (not 1951, 1961, 1981 or 1991)
    • Division into constituencies is done by the Delimitation Commission.
  • 84th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2001 amended the Art 81(3) proviso: the year 2000 was replaced by 2026, so the 1971-Census allocation and the total number of existing seats are frozen and cannot be altered until the first Census taken after 2026.
  • Delimitation is frozen till 2026.
  • [Outside source] Art 82 also provides for readjustment of seat allocation and constituency division after each Census
  • the freeze keeps the 1971 base.

Reserved Seats (SC / ST / Anglo-Indian)

Constitutional Provisions

  • Art 330
    • Reservation of seats for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in the House of the People.
  • Art 330(2)
    • Reserved seats in a State/UT bear, as nearly as may be, the same proportion to the State's total seats as the SC/ST population bears to its total population.
  • Art 331
    • Anglo-Indian representation in Lok Sabha.
  • Art 332
    • SC/ST reservation in State Legislative Assemblies.
  • Art 333
    • Anglo-Indian representation in State Assemblies.
    • In January 2020 the Anglo-Indian reserved seats in Parliament and State legislatures were discontinued by the 104th Amendment 2019.
  • Reserved seats are elected by all voters of the constituency — no separate electorate.

Numbers After the 2008 Delimitation

  • After the 2008 Delimitation Commission order
    • 412 General, 84 SC (up from 79), 47 ST (up from 41).
  • Examples
    • West Bengal SC seats 8 → 10
    • Andhra Pradesh (erstwhile) SC seats 6 → 7 (both 42-seat states in 1996).
    • So for a 42-seat State no stated option (21, 14, 7, 6) is correct.
  • Rajasthan
    • 4 SC, 3 ST of 25 seats.

Elections to Lok Sabha

Electoral Framework

  • Art 326
    • Elections to Lok Sabha and State Assemblies are on adult suffrage.
  • Art 324
    • Superintendence, direction and control of elections vest in the Election Commission
    • The electoral rolls are prepared under the Commission's direction (Art 324(1)), not under the President.
    • The President makes available staff to the Commission on request.
  • Nomination paper
    • Filed by any citizen whose name appears in the electoral roll of any parliamentary constituency
    • Section 4(d), Representation of the People Act, 1951 (not any resident or any citizen).
  • Elections follow First Past the Post
    • The highest-vote candidate wins — 50% of votes is not required.

Money in Elections

  • January 2022
    • ECI raised the limit for Lok Sabha candidates from Rs 70 lakh to Rs 95 lakh
    • Assembly from Rs 28 lakh to Rs 40 lakh.
  • Small States
    • Lok Sabha from Rs 54 lakh to Rs 75 lakh
    • Assembly from Rs 20 lakh to Rs 28 lakh (40% higher).
  • Security deposit
    • At the time of the old question, Rs 10,000 (general) and Rs 5,000 (SC/ST)
  • raised in 2010
    • Rs 25,000 / Rs 12,500 for Lok Sabha, Rs 10,000 / Rs 5,000 for Assembly.
  • A candidate loses the deposit if he fails to secure more than 1/6 of the valid votes polled.

Tenure, Sessions & Dissolution

Tenure of Lok Sabha

  • Art 83(2)
    • Normal tenure 5 years from the date of the first meeting after the general elections (unless sooner dissolved) — the President can dissolve it in between.
  • Art 83(2) proviso
    • During a Proclamation of Emergency Parliament may extend it by law for one year at a time
    • In no case beyond 6 months after the Proclamation ceases.
    • (Not 6 months at a time, not 2 years, and it can be extended.)
  • Dissolution before term
    • Art 85(2)(b)
    • By the President on the recommendation of the Prime Minister / Council of Ministers (not by Speaker, not at President's sole discretion).
  • Sitting ends by adjournment (Speaker; includes adjournment sine die), prorogation or dissolution (both by the President, Art 85(2)).
  • Summoning = convocation
  • prorogation ends a session
  • dissolution terminates a House.
  • Prorogation is not effected by the leader of the House alone.

Sessions & Quorum

  • Art 85(1)
    • President summons each House at such time and place as he thinks fit, but six months must not intervene between the last sitting of one session and the first of the next
    • A maximum gap, no minimum gap.
    • So Lok Sabha meets at least twice a year
    • There is no minimum number of sitting days
    • The Constitution does not provide three sessions.
  • By practice three sessions
    • Budget (Feb–May), Monsoon (Jul–Aug), Winter (Nov–Dec).
  • Art 100(3)
    • Quorum of Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha is 1/10 of the total members of the respective House — the minimum present to transact business.

Lok Sabha Timeline

  • First general election
    • 25 Oct 1951 – 21 Feb 1952, for 489 seats
    • First meeting on 13 May 1952.
    • INC won 364, CPI second with 16 — the Swatantra Party was not the largest opposition party.

Speaker & Deputy Speaker

Election & Tenure — Art 93, 94

  • Art 93
    • The House chooses two of its own members as Speaker and Deputy Speaker as soon as may be, and again on every vacancy
    • elected by all members of Lok Sabha (not nominated or appointed; not by the public, all MPs or just the majority party).
    • A non-member cannot be chosen
    • The six-month rule does not apply.
  • The Speaker holds office not at the pleasure of the President.
  • Tradition
    • Speaker from the majority, Deputy Speaker from the Opposition — not in the Constitution
    • No mandatory rule on the Deputy Speaker's party
    • There is no constitutional requirement to resign from the party on election.
  • Art 94(a)
    • Vacates office on ceasing to be a member.
  • Art 94(b)
    • Speaker resigns by writing addressed to the Deputy Speaker (and vice versa).
  • Art 94(c)
    • Removed by a resolution passed by a majority of all the then members, with 14 days' notice (not 2/3, not by the President on PM's advice, not both Houses).
  • Art 94 second proviso
    • On dissolution the Speaker does not vacate office until immediately before the first meeting of the new Lok Sabha.

During a Removal Resolution — Art 96

  • Art 96(1)
    • The Speaker (or Deputy Speaker) shall not preside, though present, while the resolution for his removal is considered
    • Art 95(2) applies as if he were absent.
  • Art 96(2)
    • He has the right to speak and take part in the proceedings and may vote only in the first instance, not in case of an equality of votes.

Powers & Voting — Art 95, 100, 108–118

  • Art 95(1)
    • The Deputy Speaker performs the Speaker's duties when the office is vacant or he is absent, has the same power as the Speaker when presiding, and no appeal lies against his rulings
    • He can vote in the event of an equality of votes.
  • Art 100(1)
    • Questions decided by majority of members present and voting excluding the Speaker
    • The Speaker does not vote in the first instance but has a casting vote in a tie — only for a tie (not to save the Government, constitutional amendments or emergency).
  • Art 108
    • President summons the joint sitting
  • Art 118(4)
    • The Speaker presides over it.
  • Art 110(4)
    • Every Money Bill carries the Speaker's certificate when transmitted to the Rajya Sabha under Art 109 and when presented to the President for assent under Art 111.
  • Rules of Procedure
    • Rule 8(1) — election of the Deputy Speaker on a date fixed by the Speaker, communicated by the Secretary
    • Rule 8(2) — motion moved by one member and seconded by another (not by the Speaker and PM).

Pro-tem Speaker

  • The outgoing Speaker vacates office just before the first meeting of the new House, so the President appoints a senior member as Pro-tem Speaker.
  • Procedure
    • The Legislative section prepares a list of the senior-most members
    • Submitted to the Minister of Parliamentary Affairs, who sends a note to the President for approval.
  • Function
    • administer the oath to newly elected members and conduct proceedings until a regular Speaker is chosen.

Lok Sabha Secretariat

  • Works under the direction and control of the Speaker (not the Home Ministry, Parliamentary Affairs Ministry, PM or President)
  • has supreme authority over its staff, premises and assets, exercised through the Secretary — Art 98 and Rules 124 and 124-A.
  • Art 98(3)
    • The President frames recruitment and service rules for secretariat staff after consulting the Speaker
    • Individual appointment orders are issued by the Speaker under Rule 6, Lok Sabha Secretariat (Recruitment and Conditions of Service) Rules, 1955.

Leader of the House, Opposition, Women Members & Oaths

Leader of the House

  • The Prime Minister (Art 75(1)) — head of the Council of Ministers and leader of the majority party — is the Leader of the Lok Sabha.
  • If he is not a member of the lower House, he nominates another minister as Leader of the House: in the 14th Lok Sabha Manmohan Singh (Rajya Sabha) was PM while Pranab Mukherjee led the Lok Sabha.

Leader of Opposition

  • Recognition by the Speaker needs the party to hold at least 10% of the House (54 of 543; the other explanation says 55) — not 50, 60, 80, 75 or 1/3.
  • First recognised in 1969
  • before that the leader of the largest opposition party was called LoP without formal status or privilege.
  • No party reached the minimum in 2014 and 2019, so the post was vacant in the 16th and 17th Lok Sabha
  • in the 18th Rahul Gandhi (INC) is LoP.

Women Members

  • 17th Lok Sabha
    • 78 women (over 14%) — 41 BJP and 6 INC
    • Most from Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal (11 each).
  • 2019
    • Highest ever (78)
    • Lowest was 1977 with 19.
  • 106th Amendment Act, 2023
    • One-third reservation of all seats for women in Lok Sabha, State Assemblies and the Delhi Assembly, including SC/ST seats.

Who Takes Which Oath

  • President — oath by the Chief Justice of India (Art 60)
  • Vice-President — by the President (Art 69)
  • PM and Council of Ministers — by the President (Art 75(4))
  • Supreme Court Judges — Art 124(6).
  • Speaker and Deputy Speaker do not take an oath of office (Art 93–95).
  • Lok Sabha members make and subscribe an oath (qualification for membership).

Composition of Rajya Sabha

Constitutional Basis

  • Rajya Sabha = the Upper House = Council of States.
  • Art 79
    • Parliament consists of the President, the House of the People and the Council of States — not just the two Houses.
  • Art 80
    • fixed maximum strength 250
    • not more than 238 representatives of the States and Union Territories (elected indirectly) and 12 members nominated by the President from persons with special knowledge or practical experience in literature, science, art and social service (Art 80(1)(a) and clause (3)).
  • Present strength 245
    • 233 representatives (225 from State Legislative Assemblies + 8 from UTs — Delhi 3, Puducherry 1, Jammu & Kashmir 4) + 12 nominated.
    • The options 280/20 nominated, 275/18 and 252/12 are wrong.
  • Representation therefore includes nominated members and indirectly elected members
  • no member is directly elected by citizens.
  • The Rajya Sabha was first constituted on 3 April 1952.

Who Elects Members

  • Art 80(4)
    • Representatives of each State are elected by the elected members of the State Legislative Assembly through proportional representation by the Single Transferable Vote
    • Not by the Legislative Council, both bodies, or municipalities/Gram Panchayats.
  • Representation of States is in the ratio of population (not equal for all States, nor by area or revenue)
  • seats are fixed by the Fourth Schedule.
3

Membership, Tenure & Qualification

Permanent House

  • Art 83(1)
    • The Council of States is a Permanent House — not subject to dissolution.
    • It is called permanent because it cannot be dissolved
    • No one (President, Vice-President, Supreme Court, Prime Minister, Council of Ministers) can dissolve it, and it does not dissolve with the Lok Sabha.
  • One-third of members retire, as nearly as possible, at the expiry of every second year
  • each member's term is 6 years (not 5, 3, 2 or life).
  • The statements 'term five years' and 'not less than 25 years of age' are wrong.
  • Quorum
    • 1/10 of the total membership of the House (Art 100(3)).

Qualification

  • Citizen of India, at least 30 years of age (25, 32, 35 wrong)

Chairman, Deputy Chairman & Vice-President

Chairman

  • The Vice-President is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (Art 64, Art 89(1))
  • not appointed by the President, not elected by Parliament or a State council, not the PM, CJI or Speaker.
  • The Vice-President is not a member of either House
  • if an MP is elected Vice-President, his seat in that House is deemed vacant on assuming office.
  • Rajya Sabha is therefore the House presided over by a non-member (Lok Sabha, Assemblies and Councils are not).
  • The Vice-President is elected by an electoral college of members of both Houses of Parliament (Art 66(1)), elected or nominated.
  • Nominated members cannot vote in the presidential election (whose college is elected MPs and State Assembly members) but can vote in the Vice-President's election.
  • When he acts as President he does not draw the Chairman's salary
  • he receives the President's salary and allowances.
  • While a resolution to remove him as Vice-President is under consideration he does not preside.
  • Present Chairman
    • Vice-President C.P. Radhakrishnan (earlier answer to the question: Hamid Ansari).

Deputy Chairman & Others

  • The House elects a Deputy Chairman from among its own members — he is a member
  • he resigns in writing to the Chairman
  • he is removed by a resolution passed by an absolute majority of the total members with 14 days' notice
  • when the Chairman's post is vacant he performs the Chairman's duties.
  • While presiding he does not vote in general, only a casting vote in a tie
  • he does not preside while a resolution for his own removal is considered.
  • There is also a panel of Vice-Chairmen.
  • The senior-most minister who is a Rajya Sabha member is appointed Leader of the House by the Prime Minister.
  • The Attorney General of India has the right to speak in both Houses
  • a minister who is a member of Rajya Sabha may take part in Lok Sabha deliberations (nominated experts and the Deputy Chairman may not).

Special & Exclusive Powers of Rajya Sabha

Parliament Legislating on State List

  • Art 249
    • If the Rajya Sabha passes a resolution by 2/3 of members present and voting that it is necessary or expedient in the national interest, Parliament (not the Rajya Sabha alone) may legislate on the State-List subjects specified in the resolution
    • The law may operate over the whole or any part of India.
    • The resolution is valid for 1 year, extendable by passing a further resolution.
    • (Lok Sabha majority, simple majority or 2/3 of total membership are wrong.)
  • Art 250
    • Parliament may legislate on any State-List matter while a Proclamation of Emergency is in operation
    • The law lapses automatically six months after the emergency ceases.
  • Art 252
    • Parliament legislates for two or more States by their consent, adopted by any other State later.
  • Art 253
    • Parliament may make any law for the whole or part of India to implement international agreements, conventions or decisions of international conferences.

All India Services — Art 312

  • Art 312(1)
    • If the Rajya Sabha declares by a resolution supported by not less than 2/3 of members present and voting (not of total membership) that it is necessary in the national interest, Parliament may by law create one or more All India Services, including All-India Judicial Services.
  • Creation of All India Services and the Art 249 authorisation are the exclusive powers of the Rajya Sabha.
  • Changing a State's territory or name, amending election procedure of the President, or determining Election Commission functions are not Rajya Sabha special powers.
7

Rajya Sabha vs Lok Sabha

Money Bills

  • Art 109
    • A Money Bill cannot be introduced in the Rajya Sabha
    • It is introduced only in the Lok Sabha with the President's prior recommendation.
    • After passage it is transmitted to the Rajya Sabha for recommendations, which must be returned within 14 days
    • The Lok Sabha may accept or reject all or any of them and still proceed.
    • So the Rajya Sabha has no power to reject or amend a Money Bill — a substantially amended bill leaves Lok Sabha free to proceed.
  • The Lok Sabha is not required to accept the Rajya Sabha's recommendations.
  • Ordinary Bills, Constitution Amendment Bills and State Reorganisation Bills can be first introduced in either House.
  • Art 113(2)
    • Estimates are submitted as Demands for Grants to the House of the People, which alone may assent or refuse
    • The Rajya Sabha cannot vote on them, but may discuss the Annual Financial Statement (without voting).

Exclusive vs Equal Powers

  • Lok Sabha exclusive
    • Introducing/rejecting/amending a Money Bill, the accountability of the Council of Ministers (Art 75(3)), and a no-confidence motion
    • Cut motions are also Lok Sabha-only.
  • Not exclusive to Lok Sabha
    • ratifying an Emergency (Art 352: both Houses approve within one month) and impeaching the President (Art 61(1): charge may be preferred by either House).
  • Equal power
    • Amending the Constitution (Art 368(2))
    • Bill in either House, passed in each by a majority of total membership and 2/3 of members present and voting, then President's assent.
  • Failure of the Government in the Rajya Sabha has no consequence
  • no resignation, dissolution or President's Rule
  • because the Council is responsible only to the Lok Sabha.

Nature, Composition & History of Parliament

Nature & Composition

  • Parliament of India = supreme legislative body
  • India has a parliamentary system based on the Westminster model.
  • The right to amend the Constitution is vested in Parliament under Art 368.
  • Art 79 (Part V)
    • Parliament = President + Council of States (Rajya Sabha) + House of the People (Lok Sabha) — these three are the integral parts.
    • The Vice-President, Prime Minister, Council of Ministers, Speaker, Secretariat and Minister of Parliamentary Affairs are not parts of Parliament.
  • Art 59(1)
    • The President is not a member of either House, does not sit in Parliament, attend its meetings or vote
    • Yet is an integral part of it (so 'not a part of Parliament' is wrong).
  • Council of States
    • Not more than 250 members.
    • Not more than 238 representatives of States and UTs.
    • 12 nominated members.
  • House of the People
    • Not more than 552 members (the earlier maximum).
    • Not more than 530 State representatives — Art 81(1)(a), direct election from territorial constituencies.
    • Not more than 20 UT representatives — Art 81(1)(b).
    • Not more than 2 nominated Anglo-Indians.
  • Missing item 'X'
    • 20 UT representatives.
  • Anglo-Indian nomination to Lok Sabha has ended
    • Not extended beyond 25 January 2020 by the 104th Amendment Act, 2019 (via Art 334).
    • Nominated members therefore now exist only in the Rajya Sabha (12)
    • Earlier Lok Sabha had 2 and Rajya Sabha 12.

History, Buildings & Limits

  • Parliament was constituted on 13 May 1952
  • its Golden Jubilee was celebrated on 13 May 2002.
  • New Parliament building designed by architect Bimal Patel (spelled Vimal in the explanation)
  • inaugurated by PM Narendra Modi on 28 May 2023.
  • The earlier building was designed by Sir Edwin Lutyens and Sir Herbert Baker.
  • Vitthalbhai Patel was the first Speaker of the Central Legislative Assembly (nationalist, elected 1925; tenure 24 Aug 1925 – 28 Apr 1930)
  • he mooted a separate Parliamentary secretariat in January 1926.
  • Parliament's sovereignty is restricted by judicial review
    • Laws cannot infringe the Basic Structure
    • The Supreme Court checks their validity (not the President's, Leader of Opposition's or PM's powers).

Indian Parliamentary Group

  • Formed in 1949 under a motion of the Constituent Assembly (Legislative) of 16 August 1948
  • an autonomous body (statement 'not autonomous' is wrong).
  • Membership is open to all MPs and ex-MPs
  • an MP can become a Life Member
  • on ceasing to be MP he becomes an Associate Life Member.
  • Managed by an Executive Committee
  • the Speaker of Lok Sabha is the ex-officio President.
  • Vice-Presidents
    • The Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha and the Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha serve as the ex-officio Vice-Presidents of the Group (not the Chairman of Rajya Sabha).
  • Aims
    • Personal contacts among MPs, seminars and lectures on political, defence, economic, social and educational problems, foreign visits and goodwill missions
    • Acts as the link with foreign Parliaments and as the National Group of the Inter-Parliamentary Union and main branch of the Commonwealth Parliamentary Association in India.
    • The statement on ex-MP membership is treated as correct.

Sessions, Presidential Address, Officers & Secretariat

Sessions

  • Art 85(1)
    • The President summons each House so that six months do not intervene between the last sitting of one session and the first sitting of the next
    • A maximum gap of 6 months (not 4 months, a year, or a gap fixed by the President).

  • Usually three sessions
    • Budget, Monsoon, Winter.

Special Address — Art 87

  • Art 87(1)
    • The President addresses both Houses assembled together (not a 'joint sitting', 'joint meeting' or 'joint session') at the commencement of the first session after each general election to the House of the People, and at the commencement of the first session of each year
    • He informs Parliament of the causes of its summons.
  • The address highlights the Government's policy priorities and plans
  • executive power vests in the President and is exercised on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the PM.

Officers & Secretariat

  • Officers of Parliament
    • Speaker, Deputy Speaker and Secretary-General of Lok Sabha; Chairman and Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha.
  • Art 98(1)
    • Each House has a separate secretarial staff
    • Posts common to both Houses may be created.
  • Art 98(2)
    • Parliament may by law regulate their recruitment and conditions of service.
    • The Secretariat is independent of the Government — not under the Minister of Parliamentary Affairs, the President or the Supreme Court.

Members — Absence, Dual Membership, Office of Profit & Voting

Vacation of Seat

  • Art 101(4)
    • If a member remains absent from all meetings for 60 days without informing the House, the House may declare his seat vacant
    • In the count, no account is taken of any period during which the House is prorogued or adjourned for more than four consecutive days.
    • (6 months, 2 months, 3 months or 1 year are wrong.)
  • The first member disqualified for such absence was Barjinder Singh Hamdard, an Independent MP of the Council of States from Punjab, in 2000.
  • Art 101(2) with the Prohibition of Simultaneous Membership Rules, 1950 (Rule 2): a person cannot be in Parliament and a State Legislature at once
  • if elected to both, his seat in Parliament falls vacant unless he resigns from the State Legislature within 14 days.

Office of Profit

  • Art 102(1)(a)
    • A person is disqualified from being chosen or being a member of Parliament if he holds an office of profit under the Government of India or of any State, other than an office declared by Parliament by law not to disqualify its holder.
    • (102(1)(b), (c), (d) are the other grounds: [Outside source] unsound mind declared by a court, undischarged insolvent, and non-citizenship / foreign allegiance.)
  • 'Office of profit' is not defined in the Constitution or in the Representation of the People Act, 1951.
  • The Parliament (Prevention of Disqualification) Act, 1959 lists exempted offices
  • it has been amended five times.
  • Who decides the office of profit
    • Parliament for the Union (Art 102(1)(a)), State Legislatures for States (Art 191(1)(a)).
    • Whether a person is disqualified is decided by the President on the advice of the Election Commission (Art 103(1),(2)) at the Centre and by the Governor on its advice (Art 192(1),(2)) in States.

Voting & Nominated Members

  • Elected members of the State Legislative Assembly vote in elections to Lok Sabha (as citizens), Rajya Sabha and the President
  • members of the Upper House of the State Legislature, or of Parliament's Houses alone, do not have this combined right.
  • Earlier a Rajya Sabha candidate had to be an ordinary resident/voter of the State
  • the 2003 amendment of the Representation of the People Act, 1951 removed it and the Supreme Court upheld it in 2006.
  • Not every Rajya Sabha member is elected by Assemblies (12 are nominated), and the Vice-President need not be a Rajya Sabha member.
  • Art 54
    • The President is elected by an electoral college of the elected members of both Houses and State Legislative Assemblies, so nominated members cannot vote in the presidential election.
    • There is no constitutional bar on a nominated member being a Union Minister
    • He must be an MP or become one within six months.

Privileges & Immunities

Parliamentary Privileges

  • Art 105 gives the powers, privileges and immunities of the Houses, their members and committees (not Art 104, 82 or 117).

Parliamentary Devices & Procedure

Conduct & Terms

  • Yielding the floor
    • The Speaker/Chairman stops one member and lets another speak.
  • Crossing the floor
    • A member changes party allegiance — leaving one party and joining another or becoming independent.
  • Decorum
    • Members enter and leave the chamber without disturbing the House.
  • Interpellation
    • Demanding that a Government official explain an Act or policy.
  • Zero Hour is an Indian innovation (since the early sixties), the time after Question Hour when urgent matters are raised, sometimes with prior permission of the chair
  • maximum one hour
  • duration not fixed in the Rules.
  • Lok Sabha
    • Usually 12 noon – 1 pm, after Question Hour
  • Rajya Sabha
    • 11 am – 12 noon, before Question Hour.
  • Question Hour
    • Lok Sabha 11 – 12
    • Rajya Sabha (since 2014) 12 – 1.
  • Rule 377 (Lok Sabha)
    • Matters outside points of order and the rules on questions, calling attention and motions can be raised with written notice to the Secretary-General and the Speaker's consent
    • The number per day was increased from 20 to 30 in 2015.
  • Rule 184
    • Discussion followed by voting, so a ruling party with fewer votes may lose its position — the reason the government avoids it.

Questions & Motions

  • Starred question
    • Asterisk-marked
    • oral answer
    • supplementary questions can be asked
    • Only 20 listed for oral answer per day.
  • Unstarred
    • No oral answer, no supplementaries.
  • Short Notice
    • Urgent matter, shorter notice than ordinary, supplementaries allowed.
  • Adjournment Motion
    • To discuss a definite matter of urgent public importance where a starred question, short-duration discussion or notice resolution would be too late
    • A means of control over the Council of Ministers along with Question Hour and supplementary questions.
  • Calling Attention
    • With the Speaker's prior permission a member draws a Minister's attention to an urgent public matter
    • The Minister makes a brief statement or asks for time
    • An Indian concept, like an adjournment motion without condemnation, which has restricted the scope of the adjournment motion.
  • Censure Motion
    • Expresses strong disapproval of Government policies
    • reasons are mandatory
    • It differs from a no-confidence motion and does not need leave of the House — BPSC deleted the question because both (b) and (d) are correct.
  • Cut motions (Lok Sabha) reduce demands for grants
    • policy cut (demand reduced to Re 1), economy cut (by a specified amount), token cut (by Rs 100)
    • The aim is to draw attention to the matter specified
    • If adopted and the Government lacks numbers it must resign.
    • Vote on account is not 'sanction of all demands of the year'.
  • Closure motions cut short debate (four kinds)
  • the Guillotine puts the undiscussed clauses of a Bill or resolution to vote when the allotted time is over.

Stages of a Bill & Private Members

  • A Bill passes three stages in either House
    • first reading — leave to introduce and introduction
    • second reading — general discussion on the Bill as a whole, then clause-by-clause consideration (as introduced or reported by a Select or Joint Committee)
    • third reading — debate confined to arguments, then the member in charge moves that the Bill be passed.
  • Private Member's Bill
    • Presented by any member other than a Minister (nominated status is irrelevant)
    • Needs notice and leave to introduce
    • no motion for leave is needed if the Bill was published in the Gazette earlier.
    • About a dozen have been passed since independence, so none was passed 'for the first time'.

Bills, Lapse & Joint Sitting

Introduction & President's Sanction

  • An ordinary Bill, Constitution Amendment Bill and State Reorganisation Bill can be introduced in either House
  • a Money Bill only in Lok Sabha
  • a Bill on creation of All India Services only in Rajya Sabha (per source).
  • Prior sanction of the President is needed to introduce Bills on: formation of a new State; taxation affecting States; altering State boundaries; Money Bills.

Lapse of Bills — Art 107, 108(5)

  • 107(5)
    • A Bill pending in Lok Sabha, or passed by it and pending in Rajya Sabha, lapses on dissolution of Lok Sabha (subject to Art 108).
  • 107(4)
    • A Bill pending in Rajya Sabha and not passed by Lok Sabha does not lapse on dissolution.
  • 107(3)
    • No Bill lapses on prorogation.
  • 108(5)
    • A joint sitting may be held and a Bill passed even if Lok Sabha is dissolved after the President has notified his intention to summon the joint sitting
    • Such a Bill does not lapse
    • If the Bill has already lapsed no joint sitting can be summoned.

Joint Sitting — Art 108

  • The President summons both Houses to a joint sitting to resolve a deadlock on a Bill
  • Art 109 is Money Bills, not the joint sitting.
  • Presided by the Speaker (Art 118(4))
  • in his absence the Deputy Speaker, then the Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha, then a person decided by the members present.
  • Not the President, PM, Vice-President, Rajya Sabha Chairman, CJI or a Pro-tem Speaker.
  • Decided by a simple majority of members present and voting of both Houses (Art 108(4)), not 2/3 or an absolute majority
  • quorum 1/10 of the total members of both Houses (Lok Sabha Rules, not 1/6).
  • Applicable only to ordinary Bills (and financial Bills that are not Money Bills)
  • not Money Bills (Rajya Sabha cannot block them, must return within 14 days), not Constitution Amendment Bills (each House passes separately under Art 368) and not Appropriation Bills
  • also not for electing the President or Vice-President.
  • Three joint sittings since 26 Jan 1950
    • 1961 — Dowry Prohibition Bill (first)
    • 1978 — Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill (second)
    • March 2002 — Prevention of Terrorism Bill (POTA) (third).
    • The first joint meeting concerned the Dowry Prohibition Bill (not Hindu Code, Bank Nationalisation or Gold Control).

Money Bills & Financial Bills

Money Bill — Art 109, 110

  • Art 109
    • Special procedure
  • Art 110
    • Definition (Art 111: assent — the source calls it 'permission on Bills').
    • A Money Bill is introduced only in Lok Sabha (not Rajya Sabha, either House, joint sitting or the Public Accounts Committee) on the recommendation of the President.
  • Art 110(1)
    • A Bill is a Money Bill if it contains only provisions on: (a) imposition, abolition, remission, alteration or regulation of any tax
    • (b) borrowing or guarantees by the Government of India or amending law on its financial obligations
    • (c) custody of the Consolidated or Contingency Fund and payments into or withdrawals from them
    • (d) appropriation from the Consolidated Fund
    • (e) declaring expenditure charged on the Consolidated Fund or increasing it
    • (f) receipt of money on account of the Consolidated Fund or Public Account, its custody or issue, or the audit of accounts of the Union or a State
    • (g) incidental matters.
    • Fines and penalties are not a Money Bill matter.
  • Art 110(3)
    • The Speaker's decision is final on whether a Bill is a Money Bill — not challengeable in any court, in either House or by the President
    • He need consult no one
    • He certifies it when it is sent to Rajya Sabha and when presented for assent.

Rajya Sabha & Money Bills — Art 109(2)-(5)

  • After Lok Sabha passes it, the Bill goes to Rajya Sabha for recommendations, to be returned within 14 days (with or without recommendations)
  • Rajya Sabha can neither reject nor amend it and may withhold it at most 14 days (not 12, 16, 18 days, 1 month, 4 or 6 months).
  • 109(3)
    • If Lok Sabha accepts any recommendation, the Bill is deemed passed by both Houses with those amendments
  • 109(4)
    • If it accepts none, it is deemed passed in the form passed by Lok Sabha
  • 109(5)
    • If not returned within 14 days, it is deemed passed in Lok Sabha's form.
    • Lok Sabha need not accept recommendations
    • No 2/3 majority, joint session or President's reference is needed — it just passes the Bill again, accepting or rejecting.
  • The President cannot return a Money Bill for reconsideration (as stated in the source options)
  • an ordinary Finance Bill can be returned.

Finance Bills

  • Money Bills (Art 110), Financial Bills I (Art 117(1)) and Financial Bills II (Art 117(3)) are the three kinds of financial Bills
  • every Money Bill is a financial Bill but not every financial Bill is a Money Bill.
  • Financial Bill I
    • Has matters of Art 110(1)(a)-(f) but is not exclusively a Money Bill — introduced only in Lok Sabha on the President's recommendation
    • Afterwards governed like an ordinary Bill, so Rajya Sabha can reject or amend it and a joint sitting is possible.
  • Financial Bill II
    • Provisions on expenditure from the Consolidated Fund
    • Introduced in either House, but the President's recommendation is essential for its consideration
    • A Bill merely involving expenditure and no Art 110 matter can be initiated in either House.
  • The Finance Bill is presented in the Lower House (Art 117(1): no introduction in Council of States, only on the President's recommendation)
  • in the question, Finance Bill means Money Bill
  • Rajya Sabha can delay it only 14 days (an ordinary Bill has no time limit).
  • Rajya Sabha can stop a Financial Bill like an ordinary Bill
  • if it is a Money Bill, only for 14 days.
Source ConflictStatement-type questions treat the Finance Bill inconsistently: one explanation says the Rajya Sabha can amend a Money Bill and that a Finance Bill can only be introduced in Lok Sabha
the other explains that Financial Bill II can be introduced in either House and that a joint sitting is possible for Financial Bills.
Use the Art 117 distinction above.

Consolidated Fund, Contingency Fund & Charged Expenditure

Funds

  • Art 265
    • No tax can be levied or collected except by authority of law (not Art 266, 300 or 368).
    • Art 300 (the source) deals with suits and proceedings
    • Art 368 with amendments.
  • Art 266
    • All revenues received by the Government of India, loans (treasury bills, ways and means advances) and repayments form the Consolidated Fund of India, under the control of Parliament
    • All revenues from taxes and other receipts for conducting government business are credited to it.
    • The Public Account's disbursements need no parliamentary authorisation — it is in the executive's hands.
  • No money can be withdrawn from the Consolidated Fund without Parliament's authorisation — only through an Appropriation Bill under Art 114 (114(3))
  • not by the CAG, Finance Minister, President, PM or an authorised minister.
  • Art 267
    • Contingency Fund of India
    • An imprest established by Parliament by law, placed at the President's disposal to meet unforeseen expenditure pending authorisation by Parliament under Art 115 or 116
    • The President can spend from it before Parliament's approval.

Charged Expenditure — Art 112(3)

  • Expenditure charged on the Consolidated Fund is not voted by Parliament but can be discussed in either House.
  • It includes
    • Emoluments of the President
    • Salaries and allowances of the Chairman and Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha and Speaker and Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha
    • debt charges
    • Salaries, allowances and pensions of Supreme Court Judges, pensions of Federal Court and High Court Judges
    • Salary, allowances and pension of the CAG
    • Sums to satisfy court or arbitral awards
    • And anything declared by the Constitution or Parliament.
  • Not charged
    • Salary and allowances of the Vice-President (he draws pay as Rajya Sabha Chairman, which is charged), of the Prime Minister, and of the Election Commission members.
    • Allowances of the Deputy Chairman, the CJI, CAG and UPSC Chairman (as listed in the options) are charged or treated as charged.

Budget, Grants & Financial Control

Annual Financial Statement & Preparation

  • Art 112(1)
    • The President causes the Annual Financial Statement (the Budget
    • The word 'Budget' does not appear in the Constitution; 'Appropriation Bill', 'Consolidated Fund of India' and 'Annual Financial Statement' do) to be laid before both Houses
    • The Union Finance Minister lays it on behalf of the President, not the Prime Minister.
    • It is a statement of estimated receipts and expenditure for a financial year (1 April to 31 March).
  • The Ministry of Finance prepares the Union Budget
  • the Department of Economic Affairs prepares and presents it (not Revenue, Financial Services or Expenditure).
  • Economic Survey is presented before the Budget (not after the Budget or Finance Bill).

Demands for Grants — Art 113

  • Art 113(2)
    • Estimates are submitted as Demands for Grants to the Lok Sabha, which may assent, refuse, or reduce
  • Art 113(3)
    • No demand except on the President's recommendation.
    • Generally one demand per ministry/department, more for large ones
    • 102 demands in the 2025-26 Budget and 101 in 2021-22.
  • Related Articles
    • Art 115 supplementary/additional/excess grants
    • Art 116 Vote on Account, Vote of Credit and exceptional grants — outside normal financial legislation.

Vote on Account & Interim Budget

  • Vote on Account (Art 116(1)(a))
    • When the Appropriation Bill is not passed, Lok Sabha can authorise withdrawal from the Consolidated Fund for a fixed period
    • Used when there is no time to pass the full Budget before the new financial year or elections are near
    • It covers expenditure only, whereas an Interim Budget covers both receipts and expenditure.
    • It is not for loans, RBI borrowing or grants-in-aid, and not limited to caretaker governments.
  • If the annual Union Budget is not passed by Lok Sabha the PM submits the resignation of the Council of Ministers.

Budget Stages & Parliamentary Control

  • Six stages
    • Presentation
    • General discussion
    • Scrutiny by departmental committees
    • Voting on Demands for Grants
    • Appropriation Bill
    • Finance Bill. 'Normal financial legislation' = presentation, discussion, Appropriation Bill and Finance Bill (not vote on account, vote of credit or exceptional grants).
  • Methods of control over finance
    • Laying the Annual Financial Statement
    • Withdrawal only after the Appropriation Bill
    • Supplementary grants and vote on account
    • Introducing the Finance Bill — a Parliamentary Budget Office reviewing programmes against macro-economic forecasts is not one of them.
    • Parliament cannot impose or raise a tax without the President's recommendation
    • It has no power to vote on charged expenditure.
  • The Estimates Committee is the largest financial committee
    • 30 members, all from Lok Sabha — no Rajya Sabha member.
    • Parliament controls administration through its committees.

Nature, Types & Classification of Committees

Why Committees Exist

  • Reason
    • Parliament's work is varied in nature and considerable in volume, its time is limited, and it cannot give close consideration to every legislative matter.
    • A good deal of its business is therefore transacted by Parliamentary Committees.
    • Parliament is too unwieldy to deliberate effectively on its own.
  • Role
    • Parliament is assisted by committees in the discharge of its duties.
    • Parliament exercises control over the administration through its committees (not through advisory committees of ministries, periodic reports from administrators or writs).
  • Constitution
    • Committees are appointed or elected by the Houses or nominated by the Speaker / Chairman.
  • Working
    • Committees work under the direction of the Speaker / Chairman.
    • They submit their report to the House, or to the Speaker / Chairman as applicable.

Two Kinds

  • Standing Committees
    • Permanent bodies, constituted every year or periodically.
    • They work on a continuous basis.
  • Ad-hoc Committees
    • Temporary bodies.
    • They cease to exist on completing the task assigned to them.
  • Example
    • The Joint Committee on the Stock Market Scam.
  • Six classes of Standing Committees
    • (i) Financial Committees.
    • (ii) Department-related Standing Committees (total 24).
    • (iii) Committees of Enquiry.
    • (iv) Committees to Scrutinise and Control.
    • (v) Committees relating to the day-to-day business of the House.
    • (vi) Housekeeping or Service Committees.
  • Financial Committees
    • Public Accounts Committee, Estimates Committee and Committee on Public Undertakings — three in all.
  • Committee of Enquiry
    • Includes the Committee on Petitions, the Committee on Privileges and the Ethics Committee.
  • Housekeeping Committee
    • The Joint Committee on Salaries and Allowances of Members belongs here — it is not a Financial Committee.

House Composition & Ministry Role

  • Ratio
    • Most Standing Committees (leaving out some exceptional ones) have members from Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha in the ratio 2 : 1.
  • All other joint committees also have approximately 2
    • 1.
  • Matching by type
    • Public Accounts Committee — Financial
    • Committee on Petitions — Functional
    • Joint Committee on Stock Market Scam — Ad hoc
    • Departmental Committees — Standing.
  • Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs
    • Works under the overall direction of the Cabinet Committee on Parliamentary Affairs.
    • The Minister of Parliamentary Affairs nominates MPs on committees, councils, boards and commissions set up by the Government of India in various ministries.
  • Consultative Committees for Railway Zones
    • Constituted by the Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs, with MPs of the respective zones elected as members (not the President, Ministry of Railways or Ministry of Transport).
  • CAG
    • Art 148 provides for the Comptroller and Auditor General.
    • The CAG is not a tool of Parliamentary control over public expenditure — the PAC, Estimates Committee and Committee on Public Undertakings are.

Public Accounts Committee (PAC)

Composition & Tenure

  • Strength
    • Not more than 22 members.
    • 15 elected by Lok Sabha every year from among its members, by proportional representation by the single transferable vote.
    • Not more than 7 members of Rajya Sabha nominated by that House and associated with the Committee.
    • (Not 11/5, 10/5 or 17/10; not 25 Lok Sabha members.)
  • Tenure
    • One year.
    • The Committee is formed every year.
  • Chairman
    • Appointed by the Speaker from among its members — not nominated by the PM, the President or the Rajya Sabha Chairman.
    • The Lok Sabha appoints the chairpersons of all Parliamentary committees.
  • Opposition chairman
    • Till 1966-67 the Chairman belonged to the ruling party.
    • In 1967-68 the Speaker appointed an opposition member as Chairman for the first time
    • The practice has continued since.
  • Ministers
    • A Minister is not eligible to be a member.
  • Members
    • Only MPs — no eminent persons from industry and trade.

Origin & Functions

  • Origin
    • First set up in 1921 under the Government of India Act, 1919
    • In existence since then.
    • One of the most important Committees of Parliament.
  • Assisted by
    • The Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG).
  • Main function
    • Examine the annual audit reports of the CAG (not to interfere in day-to-day activities, recommend to the PM or pass orders).
    • The reports are laid before Parliament by the President.
  • Basis of deliberation
    • Appropriation Accounts of Railways, Defence Services, P&T Department and other Civil Ministries, CAG reports on them, and CAG reports on revenue receipts.
    • This includes the examination of the appropriation and finance accounts of the Government.
  • Purpose
    • Acts as Parliament's financial check on the executive.
    • It ensures public money is spent in accordance with Parliament's decisions.
    • It calls attention to waste, extravagance, loss and nugatory expenditure.
    • It highlights misuse and misappropriation of public money before each House, and recommends action against responsible authorities.
  • Scrutiny
    • The CAG's report (not the Consolidated Fund, Public Account or Contingency Fund as such).

Reporting

  • Report goes to
    • The Speaker of Lok Sabha, who gets it presented in the Lok Sabha
    • A copy is laid on the Table of the Rajya Sabha.
    • Ordinarily it presents reports to the House
    • Only when the House is not in session may it present the report to the Speaker and later to the House.
  • Not submitted to
    • The CAG, the Minister of Parliamentary Affairs, the President, the PM or the Finance Minister.

Estimates Committee & Committee on Public Undertakings

Estimates Committee

  • Founded
    • Constituted for the first time in 1950.
  • Strength
    • 30 members, elected every year by Lok Sabha from among its members.
    • No Rajya Sabha member — drawn entirely from Lok Sabha.

  • Chairman
    • Appointed by the Speaker from among its members.
  • Tenure
    • One year (not 2, 3 or 4).
  • Function
    • Suggest alternative policies to bring efficiency and economy in administration.
    • Report on improvements in organisation, efficiency or administrative reform consistent with the policy underlying the estimates.
    • It monitors the examination of estimates, austerity and the expenditure of funds.

Committee on Public Undertakings (PUC)

  • Strength
    • 22 members — 15 elected by Lok Sabha and 7 from Rajya Sabha.
    • Rajya Sabha has representation.
  • Tenure
    • One year.
    • A Minister is not eligible for election.
  • Function (a)
    • Examine the reports and accounts of public undertakings specified in the Fourth Schedule to the Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in Lok Sabha.
  • Function (b)
    • Examine the reports, if any, of the CAG on public undertakings.
  • Function (c)
    • Examine, in the context of autonomy and efficiency of the public undertakings, whether their affairs are managed in accordance with sound business principles and prudent commercial practices.
  • Function (d)
    • Exercise such other functions of the PAC and Estimates Committee in relation to public undertakings as are not covered by (a)–(c) and as may be allotted by the Speaker from time to time.

  • Not associated
    • Estimates Committee.
    • It is the only one of the three constituted from Lok Sabha alone.
  • Committee on Government Assurances
    • Has Rajya Sabha representation (each House has its own such committee).

Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955

Basics & Extent

  • Purpose
    • Prescribes punishment for the preaching and practice of untouchability and for the enforcement of any disability arising from it.
  • Section 1(2)
    • Extends to the whole of India (no exception for J&K, Scheduled areas or Goa, Daman and Diu).
  • Not an offence
    • Untouchability does not constitute an offence when the accused and the victim belong to the same social group.

Nature of Offences

  • Section 15(1)
    • Notwithstanding the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, all offences are cognizable and triable summarily.
    • They are also non-compoundable.
    • Punishment is imprisonment and fine.
  • Trial court
    • First Class Judicial Magistrates are empowered to hold special courts.
    • In a metropolis the trial is by a Metropolitan Magistrate.
    • In Uttar Pradesh, in each district, the courts of Chief Judicial Magistrates / Judicial Magistrates are designated Special Courts.
  • Provisional key
    • MPPSC first marked 'compoundable' as the answer
    • The revised key gave bonus marks to all.

Section-wise Facts

  • Section 3
    • Punishment for enforcing religious disabilities.
  • Section 4
    • Enforcing social disabilities. [Outside source]
  • Section 5
    • Refusing admission to any hospital, dispensary or educational institution (established or maintained for the benefit of the general public or any section) on the ground of untouchability.
  • Punishment
    • Imprisonment ≥ 1 month and ≤ 6 months, and fine ≥ Rs 100 and ≤ Rs 500.
  • Section 6
    • Refusing to sell goods or render services.
  • Section 7
    • Other offences arising out of untouchability. [Outside source]
  • Section 10
    • Abetment of offence.
    • A public servant who wilfully neglects the investigation of an offence is deemed to have abetted it.
  • Section 10A
    • Power of the State Government to impose collective fine.
  • Section 12
    • Presumption by courts.
    • If an act constituting an offence is committed in relation to a member of a Scheduled Caste, the court presumes it was on the ground of untouchability, unless the contrary is proved.
    • Applies to Scheduled Castes only (not STs).
  • Section 13

Right to Information Act, 2005

Passage & Status

  • Passage
    • Passed by Lok Sabha on 11 May 2005 and Rajya Sabha on 12 May 2005.
  • Assent
    • President Dr A.P.J. Abdul Kalam on 15 June 2005.
  • Fully in force
    • 12 October 2005 (after 120 days).
  • Nature
    • A legal right
    • The Supreme Court also treats it as a fundamental right — freedom of thought and expression includes it.
    • It is not clearly mentioned in the Constitution.
    • In Raj Narain v State of UP (1976) the Court held it a fundamental right under Art 19.
  • Objectives
    • Empower citizens
    • Promote transparency and accountability
    • Tackle corruption
    • Make democracy work in a real sense.
    • Not 'empower women and weaker sections'.
    • An informed citizen can keep vigil on the instruments of governance.
  • Meaning (Section 2)
    • Access to information controlled by public officials — inspecting records, documents and works, and taking samples.
  • Preamble
    • Informed citizenry and transparency of information
    • Revelation of information in practice may conflict with other public interests and confidentiality of sensitive information
    • Harmonise these interests while preserving the paramountcy of the democratic ideal.
  • Section 6(2)
    • An applicant need not give reasons.
    • A Madras High Court bench (Justices N. Paul Vasanthakumar and K. Ravichandrababu) said the object must be disclosed, but the Act has no such obligation.
  • Bureaucracy
    • The Act has generally increased the sense of responsibility in bureaucracy but still has miles to go.

Information Commissions

  • Central Information Commission
    • Constituted w.e.f.
    • 12 October 2005
    • Jurisdiction over all central public authorities.
  • Section 12
    • Constitution of the CIC (not about its powers).
  • Section 18
    • Powers and functions of the Commissions.
  • Section 19
    • Appeals.
  • Section 20
    • Penalty — imposed by the CIC or State Information Commission (not High Court).
  • Section 25
    • Report on implementation.
  • State Commissions
    • Appointed by the Governor on the recommendation of a committee under Section 15(3): Chief Minister (Chair), Leader of Opposition in the Legislative Assembly, and a Cabinet Minister nominated by the Chief Minister.
    • Chief Justice of High Court is not a member.
  • Eligibility
    • A State Chief Information Commissioner and not more than ten State Information Commissioners
    • Persons of eminence in public life with knowledge of law, science and technology, social service, management, journalism, mass media, administration and governance.
    • Must not be an MP or a member of any State/UT legislature.
  • Tenure
    • Originally 5 years (or age 65, whichever earlier).
    • The RTI (Amendment) Act, 2019 removed this and substituted a term as prescribed by the Central Government.
  • Jammu & Kashmir
    • After the Presidential notification of 6 August 2019 abolished J&K's special status under Art 370, the Act applies in the UTs of J&K and Ladakh.
