Budget: Constitutional Framework & Process

🟨 Concept   🟦 Fact/Number   🟩 Person/Institution   🟥 MCQ Trap   🟪 Rule/Formula

1. Types of Budgets

Gender Budget

Start: 2008-09

Objective: Allocating separate funds for women's development, education and health; reflects govt's commitment to women.

Green Budget

Start: 2020-21

Objective: Using the budget for environmental protection & tackling climate change; evaluates environmental impact of budgetary policies.

Child Budget

Start: 2013-14

Objective: Highlight schemes run for welfare of children.

Bihar is the first state to prepare Child Budget formulation process with support of UNICEF & ADRI (in 2019).

Outcome Budget

Start: 2006-07

Objective: Not just spending money, but seeing what outcome resulted from that spending. An 'Achievement Report' is also presented.

2. Three Government Accounts (Public Finance)

1. Consolidated Fund of the State — Article 266

• Main govt account.

• All revenues (tax + non-tax), loans raised, and loan recoveries credited here.

• No money withdrawn without legislative approval through the Appropriation Act.

2. Contingency Fund of the State — Article 267

• Used for urgent & unforeseen expenditure.

• Placed at the disposal of the Governor.

• Later recouped from Consolidated Fund after legislative approval. ₹350 crore (30,000 cr India).

3. Public Account of the State — Article 266

• Includes PF, small savings, deposits, reserve funds, etc.

• No legislative approval required for withdrawal.

3. Constitutional Articles: Union vs State (Budget)

Financial Component Central Govt (Union) State Government
Annual Financial Statement (Budget) Article 112 Article 202
Voting on Demands for Grants Article 113 Article 203
Appropriation Bill Article 114 Article 204
Finance Bill (Taxation / Revenue) Art 110 (Def.) & Art 117 Art 199 (Def.) & Art 207

Under Article 202, it is the constitutional obligation of the State Government to present the Annual Financial Statement (Budget) before the Legislature.

4. Steps in Passage of the State Budget (Articles 202-207)

Preparation of Demands for Grants (DoG)

Departments prepare expenditure estimates and submit to the Finance Department before the budget is finalized.

Presentation of Budget — Article 202

The Annual Financial Statement (Budget) is laid before the Legislature by the Finance Minister.

General Discussion

Overall discussion on fiscal policy and priorities, without voting.

Examination by DRSC

Demands for Grants referred to Departmentally Related Standing Committees for detailed scrutiny; committees submit reports to the Legislature.

Voting on Demands for Grants — Article 203

Only the Legislative Assembly votes. Cut Motions may be moved.

Appropriation Bill — Article 204

Authorizes withdrawal from the Consolidated Fund of the State.

Finance Bill — Article 207

Enacts the taxation proposals of the budget.

Governor's Assent

Budget becomes legally operational.

6. Revenue vs Capital — Budget Structure (Art. 112 / 202)

🅡 REVENUE
Receipt — TaxDirect: Corporation Tax, Income Tax, STT
Indirect: GST, Excise, Custom
Receipt — Non-TaxSelling goods/services, Dividend & Profit, Interest received, Grants received
ExpenditureInterest paid on loans, Grants given, Subsidies, Defence, Salaries & Pension
🅒 CAPITAL
Receipt — DebtInternal borrowings, External borrowings
Receipt — Non-DebtLoan recovered, Disinvestment
ExpenditureLoans given, Capital goods / assets purchase

Total Receipts = Total Expenditure — Yes, they are always presented as equal in the budget's accounting framework (balanced by borrowing/deficit financing).

THE ULTIMATE 2-LINE TRICK — RECEIPT
Asset ↓ or Liability ↑ = CAPITAL
Otherwise → REVENUE
THE ULTIMATE 2-LINE TRICK — EXPENDITURE
Asset ↑ or Liability ↓ = CAPITAL
Otherwise → REVENUE

Quick Fire:

  • Tax → Revenue Receipt
  • Salary → Revenue Expenditure
  • Borrowing → Capital Receipt
  • Highway construction → Capital Expenditure

8. Point of Incidence vs Point of Impact (Direct & Indirect Tax)

Direct Tax — Impact = Incidence (Same Person)

Government levies taxe.g. Income Tax
→
Point of ImpactTaxpayer pays it first (immediate money burden)
=
Point of IncidenceSame taxpayer bears final burden — cannot be shifted

Indirect Tax — Impact ≠ Incidence (Shifted)

Government levies taxe.g. GST on seller
→
Point of ImpactSeller/manufacturer pays first — initial liability
→ shifted →
Point of IncidenceFinal consumer bears actual burden (via higher price)

Point of Impact = where the tax is first imposed / initially paid. Point of Incidence = where the final money burden actually rests, after any shifting.

9. Railway Budget History (from committee timeline)

1920-21 — Acworth Committee

10-member committee (incl. 3 Indians) headed by British railway economist Sir William Acworth, constituted to review Indian Railways' finances & administration; recommended consolidation, nationalisation and separation of railway finances from general revenues.

1923 — Financial Commissioner for Railways appointed

Groundwork laid for separating railway accounts.

20 September 1924 — Separation Convention

Railway finances formally separated from the General Budget under Viceroy Lord Reading (Viceroy 1921–26); Railway Budget introduced separately from 1924-25.

1925 — First separate Railway Budget presented

Practice continued every year thereafter.

1947 — First Railway Budget of Independent India

Presented by John Mathai on 20 Nov 1947.

21 September 2016 — Merger approved

Modi government approved merging Railway & General Budgets, based on NITI Aayog white paper "Dispensing with the Railway Budget" by the Bibek Debroy Committee.

2017 (Budget 2017-18) — Merger implemented

Ended a 92-year-old practice; Railway Budget merged into the Union Budget, presented by FM Arun Jaitley.

Do not confuse direction: 1924 = SEPARATED (Acworth Committee) vs 2017 = MERGED BACK (Bibek Debroy Committee).

10. Types of Deficit — Formulas & Meaning

Revenue Deficit (RD)
RD = RE − RR
Full form: Revenue Deficit = Revenue Expenditure − Revenue Receipts.
One-liner: Govt's day-to-day spending exceeds its regular income.
Fiscal Deficit (FD)
FD = TE − (RR + Non-debt CR)
Full form: Fiscal Deficit = Total Expenditure − (Revenue Receipts + Non-Debt Capital Receipts).
One-liner: Total gap the govt must fill by borrowing.
Primary Deficit (PD)
PD = FD − Interest Payment
Full form: Primary Deficit = Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payments.
One-liner: Borrowing need excluding the burden of past debt's interest.
Effective Revenue Deficit (ERD)
ERD = RD − Grants for Capital Assets
Full form: Effective Revenue Deficit = Revenue Deficit − Grants-in-aid for creation of Capital Assets.
One-liner: Real "unproductive" revenue gap, after removing grants that actually build assets.
TYPES OF DEFICIT
Revenue DeficitRE > RR
Shows weakness in day-to-day finances
Fiscal DeficitTotal borrowing requirement of govt
Primary DeficitFiscal deficit minus interest burden
Effective Revenue DeficitRevenue deficit minus capital-asset grants
